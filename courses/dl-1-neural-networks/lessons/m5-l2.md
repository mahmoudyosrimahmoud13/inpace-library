# Vectorizing across examples

Stack examples as columns and every line above works for all `m` at once: `Z[1]` is `(n_h, m)`. **Rows** are hidden
units, **columns** are examples. Reading a shape tells you what it holds.

In PyTorch the same layer is `nn.Linear(n_x, n_h)`, applied to `X` of shape `(m, n_x)`; it computes `X·Wᵀ + b`.
