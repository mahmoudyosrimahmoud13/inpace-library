# Level check: can you do A1?

The CEFR describes each level by what you **can do**. Read each line and answer honestly: *yes*, *almost*, or *not yet*.

## I can...

- introduce myself and others, and use du and Sie correctly;
- ask and answer simple questions about where I live, my family and my job;
- talk about my day with times, using the verb in second position and separable verbs;
- ask the way and understand simple directions;
- order food and drink, ask prices and understand numbers to 100;
- say what I like, prefer and don't like;
- make simple plans and appointments with können, müssen and wollen;
- understand slow, clear speech on these topics.

For every *not yet*, go back to that unit: listen to the conversation again, shadow it, and redo the voice note.

## Final tasks

1. **Listening:** finish the A1 part of the DW course *Nicos Weg* on [DW Learn German](https://learngerman.dw.com/) and watch two [Easy German](https://www.youtube.com/@EasyGerman) episodes with German subtitles. Write down five new words you understood.
2. **Speaking:** record a **2-minute** voice note about yourself: who you are, your family, your day, your town, what you like eating, and your plans for the weekend. Use notes with key words, not a script.
3. **Writing:** write a short email (about 80 words) to a new friend introducing yourself.

## What next?

Keep listening every day (aim for 20 minutes) and move on to **German A2**, where you'll talk about the past (the Perfekt), use the dative, compare things and explain reasons. If you need an A1 certificate (for example for a visa), look at the **Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1** or the **telc Deutsch A1**.
