# Talking about your family

In the break, Anna shows Samir a photo of her family. Listen for der, die, das and mein, meine.

## Listen first: the conversation

**A family photo**

| German | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Samir:** Ist das deine Familie? | Is that your family? |
| **Anna:** Ja. Das ist mein Vater, und das ist meine Mutter. Sie wohnen in Krakau. | Yes. That's my father, and that's my mother. They live in Kraków. |
| **Samir:** Und wer ist das? | And who's that? |
| **Anna:** Das ist mein Bruder Piotr. Er ist dreißig Jahre alt und hat zwei Kinder. | That's my brother Piotr. He's thirty and has two children. |
| **Anna:** Hast du auch Geschwister? | Do you have brothers and sisters too? |
| **Samir:** Ja, ich habe eine Schwester und zwei Brüder. Meine Schwester ist Lehrerin. | Yes, I have a sister and two brothers. My sister is a teacher. |
| **Anna:** Bist du verheiratet? | Are you married? |
| **Samir:** Nein, ich bin ledig. Aber ich habe eine Katze. Sie heißt Mimi. | No, I'm single. But I have a cat. Her name is Mimi. |

## Shadow it

Play the conversation in your head or read it aloud **three times**: first slowly, then at normal speed, then without looking at the meaning column. If you have the audio from the links below, pause after each line and copy the speaker's rhythm and melody, not just the words. This is called **shadowing**, and it trains your ear and your mouth together.

## Words and phrases to keep

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| mein Vater / meine Mutter | my father / my mother |
| meine Eltern | my parents |
| mein Bruder / meine Schwester | my brother / my sister |
| die Geschwister | brothers and sisters |
| mein Sohn / meine Tochter | my son / my daughter |
| mein Mann / meine Frau | my husband / my wife |
| Hast du Geschwister? | Do you have brothers and sisters? |
| Wie alt bist du? | How old are you? |
| Ich bin dreißig (Jahre alt). | I'm thirty. |
| Ich bin verheiratet / ledig. | I'm married / single. |
| Wer ist das? | Who is that? |
| Das ist... | That's... / This is... |

## Say it right

**Final hardening.** At the end of a word or syllable, **b, d, g** sound like **p, t, k**: *und* ("unt"), *Tag* ("tahk"), *halb* ("halp"), *Kind* ("kint"). And **-ig** at the end sounds like *ich*: *ledig*, *zwanzig*.

**sch** = English *sh*: *Schwester*. At the start of a word, **st** and **sp** are said *sht* and *shp*: *Stadt*, *sprechen*, *Straße*.

Say: *und, Kind, Tag, ledig, Schwester, Sport, Stadt*.
