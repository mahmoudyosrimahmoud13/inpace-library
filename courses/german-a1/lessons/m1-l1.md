# Start here: how this course works

This course takes you through German level **A1** with a method based on what research says about how people learn languages. Each unit has two lessons, and each lesson has a clear job.

## The method, in six habits

1. **Listen first.** Every unit opens with a conversation you can understand with the help of the meaning column. Understanding lots of real language (called *comprehensible input*) is the engine of learning. Grammar rules help, but they don't replace listening.
2. **Learn phrases, not lone words.** You get the 10 to 14 phrases that people really use in that situation. A phrase like *Wie heißt du?* is ready to use today; you can take it apart later.
3. **One grammar point at a time.** Each unit explains one point, shows it in examples so you can *notice* it, and gives a short practice.
4. **Speak every unit.** Each unit ends with a voice note you record in inPace. Speaking is a separate skill: you only get better at it by doing it. Record, listen, fix one thing, record again.
5. **Review from memory.** Every grammar lesson starts with questions on the unit before. Pulling an answer out of your memory makes it stick much better than reading it again.
6. **Listen every day outside the lessons.** 15 to 20 minutes of German you mostly understand: the free DW course videos in the links below are sorted by level.

German has a reputation for being hard, but it's very regular, and many words are close to English (*Haus, Hand, Wasser, kommen*). The three genders (der, die, das) and the cases come slowly, one unit at a time.

## Special letters and capitals

German writes **ä ö ü** and **ß** (a double s). On a phone, hold the letter to choose it; on a computer, add the German keyboard layout, or write *ae, oe, ue, ss* if you must. **All nouns start with a capital letter**: *der Tisch, die Familie, das Auto*. That's a gift for learners: you can always spot the nouns.

## How to use each unit

- **Lesson A (listen and say):** read and listen to the conversation, shadow it three times, learn the phrases, practice the sound of the unit.
- **Lesson B (grammar and speak):** review from memory, learn the grammar point, practice, then record your voice note and write a few lines.
- **The checkpoint:** a voice note, a short piece of writing, and a quiz. If you get less than 70% on the quiz, go back to the lesson the questions came from.
