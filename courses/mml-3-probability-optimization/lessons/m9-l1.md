# Read: convex optimization (7.3) and further reading (7.4)

Convex problems are the "nice" ones: **every local minimum is a global minimum**, and good algorithms find it.

![A convex function (chords above the curve) and a non-convex one (two valleys)](convex.png)

- **Convex sets**: the segment between any two points stays inside. **Convex functions**: the chord between any two
  points of the graph lies above it. Tests: f″ ≥ 0 in 1D; a positive semidefinite Hessian in general.
- Useful facts: sums of convex functions are convex; norms are convex; least squares and regularized least squares
  are convex; logistic regression's loss is convex. Deep networks are **not**.
- **Linear programming (7.3.1)**: linear objective, linear constraints; its dual is again a linear program.
- **Quadratic programming (7.3.2)**: quadratic objective (positive definite), linear constraints. The SVM is one.
- **Legendre-Fenchel transform and convex conjugate (7.3.3)**: f*(s) = sup_x (sᵀx − f(x)). It explains duality from
  another angle. The hardest part of the chapter: read it for the idea, and remember that ½x² is its own conjugate.

Then try the chapter's exercises; 7.1 to 7.4 are a good set.
