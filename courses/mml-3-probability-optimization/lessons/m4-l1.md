# Read: the Gaussian distribution (6.5)

The most important distribution in the book. Learn these results well; chapters 9 to 11 use them constantly.

![Samples from a correlated 2D Gaussian, and how conditioning on x₁ shifts and narrows x₂](gaussian_2d.png)

- **Density**: p(x) = (2π)^(−D/2) |Σ|^(−1/2) exp(−½(x − μ)ᵀΣ⁻¹(x − μ)). The exponent is a squared distance with the
  inner product given by Σ⁻¹ (the **Mahalanobis distance**), so contours are ellipses along the eigenvectors of Σ.
- **Marginals and conditionals (6.5.1)** of a Gaussian are Gaussian. For a split x = (a, b):
  - marginal p(a) = N(μ_a, Σ_aa): just read off the block;
  - conditional p(a | b) = N(μ_a + Σ_abΣ_bb⁻¹(b − μ_b), Σ_aa − Σ_abΣ_bb⁻¹Σ_ba). The mean moves linearly with b; the
    variance shrinks and doesn't depend on b.
- **Product of two Gaussian densities (6.5.2)** is an unnormalized Gaussian (precisions add).
- **Sums and linear transformations (6.5.3)**: if x ~ N(μ, Σ), then Ax + b ~ N(Aμ + b, AΣAᵀ). Independent Gaussians add
  to a Gaussian with added means and variances. A mixture of Gaussians, however, is not Gaussian (chapter 11).
- **Sampling (6.5.4)**: x = μ + Lz with Σ = LLᵀ (Cholesky) and z standard normal.
