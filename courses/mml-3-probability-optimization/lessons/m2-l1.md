# Read: sum rule, product rule and Bayes' theorem (6.3)

Three rules carry almost all of probabilistic machine learning:

- **Sum rule** (marginalization): p(x) = Σ_y p(x, y) (or ∫ for continuous y). "Sum out what you don't care about."
- **Product rule**: p(x, y) = p(y | x) p(x). A joint is a marginal times a conditional.
- **Bayes' theorem**: p(x | y) = p(y | x) p(x) / p(y). In learning language:
  **posterior = likelihood × prior / evidence**.

The **evidence** p(y) = ∫ p(y | x) p(x) dx just normalizes, but it is often the hard part to compute; the book points
ahead to chapter 8 where it's used to compare models.

**Do on paper:** a medical test with numbers (the Check yourself page has one). Notice how a small prior keeps the
posterior small even for a good test.
