# Read: discrete and continuous probabilities (6.2)

![Three distributions you'll meet constantly: Binomial, Gaussian and Beta](distributions.png)

- **Discrete**: a **probability mass function** p(x) = P(X = x), summing to 1. Joint p(x, y), and tables of them.
- **Continuous**: a **probability density function** f(x) ≥ 0 that integrates to 1. P(a ≤ X ≤ b) is the area under
  f. A density value can be larger than 1; only areas are probabilities. And P(X = exactly a) = 0.
- **Cumulative distribution function** F(x) = P(X ≤ x) works for both.
- **Contrasting them (6.2.3)**: the book's figure compares the discrete and continuous uniform distribution. The book
  says "probability distribution" for both and is careful about the difference only where it matters.
