## The product in one paragraph

inPace is a learning app that runs **only on your own device**. You follow courses, and when you finish a part
you save a **milestone**. You can send your milestones to someone you trust (a **checker**), who can see that the
work is really yours and really in order, and approve it. There are no accounts and no servers: people exchange
small signed files or links over WhatsApp, Telegram or email.

## The rules every design decision follows

1. **Local first.** All data lives in one folder on the device (`vault.db` plus course files). The app works offline.
2. **No accounts, still trustworthy.** Trust comes from cryptography, not from a company's database: every
   person has a signing key, and every milestone is signed and chained.
3. **One codebase everywhere.** The same Python app runs on Windows, Mac, Linux and Android.
4. **Plain words.** The interface avoids jargon. You'll see this in the code too: names like `go()`,
   `page()`, `say()`.
5. **Nothing silent goes online.** The app reaches the internet only when you ask (the course library, an
   update check you allowed, a YouTube video you pressed play on).

## Why these rules matter in an interview

When someone asks "why didn't you just build a web app with a backend?", the honest answer is a trade-off:
a backend gives easy sync and analytics, but costs money to run, holds people's data, and makes trust depend on
the company. inPace chose the opposite corner: zero running cost, full privacy, and trust that anyone can verify.
The price is harder sync (you move data with an encrypted backup) and no central dashboard.
