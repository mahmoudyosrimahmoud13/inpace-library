# Wrap-up: write your report, and what comes next

## Write it up

In this module's task box (or a README with your code), report on the three architectures you built:

1. **CNN**: the architecture, its test accuracy, and the table of normal and shifted test accuracy for the fully
   connected network, the CNN, and the CNN with shift augmentation. One sentence on equivariance versus invariance.
2. **Transformer**: the model size, how many of the 300 test lists it sorted perfectly, and what the attention pattern
   showed. One sentence on why a bigram model could never do this.
3. **Graph network**: the accuracy from two labels, the member it got wrong, and the over-smoothing numbers.

## What you can do now

- Explain convolution, padding, pooling, and why CNNs suit images (chapter 10).
- Derive and implement scaled, multi-head, causal self-attention, and a full transformer layer (chapter 12).
- Explain tokenization, autoregressive generation and sampling strategies (chapter 12).
- Build a graph convolutional network, and explain permutation equivariance and over-smoothing (chapter 13).
- See the common thread: each architecture respects a symmetry of its data.

## Next: part 4

So far, every model has been trained to predict an output from an input. Part 4 turns to models of the data itself:
graphical models (chapter 11), sampling and Markov chain Monte Carlo (chapter 14), and **latent variables**, hidden causes
behind what we observe (chapters 15 and 16). You'll implement the EM algorithm, samplers that draw from complicated
distributions, and probabilistic PCA. They're the foundations of the generative models in part 5.
