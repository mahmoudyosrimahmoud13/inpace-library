The situation: training data is clean scanned digits; users send noisy, blurred phone photos. The notebook walks
through the whole strategy, with real numbers from our run:

1. **Splits:** train and train-dev from clean scans; dev and test from users' photos.
2. **Four errors:** human 0.2%, train 0.4%, train-dev 4.5%, dev 35.0%. Data mismatch (30.5%) is by far the biggest
   problem, not bias or variance.
3. **Error analysis** on the 70 dev mistakes, with a surprise: the obvious suspect, noise, isn't it. 26% of mistakes
   are in the noisiest quarter of dev images, about what chance gives, and faint images are wrong *less* often. The
   real pattern is in the confusions: the model pulls digits toward **1** and **4** (8→1, 6→4, 1→4, 5→1). Counting
   beats guessing.
4. **One metric and a limit:** macro F1 0.644, prediction time well under 1 ms.
5. **Fix the mismatch** with synthetic phone-style images: dev error 35.0% → 22.5%; test error checked once: 20.5%.
6. **Transfer learning** with 100 labelled photos: 38% from scratch vs 28% pre-trained and fine-tuned.
