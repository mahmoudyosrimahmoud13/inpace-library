# Wrap-up: write your report, and what comes next

## Write it up

In this module's task box (or a README next to your code), report on the course's three projects:

1. **EM for Gaussian mixtures**: the number of iterations, a sentence on why the log-likelihood never went down, and the
   agreement of K-means and EM with the true groups. Why did EM win?
2. **PCA and probabilistic PCA**: how many components keep 90% of the variance, the denoising result, and what the
   likelihood comparison showed.
3. **Generating digits**: the judge's confidence for one Gaussian, the 10-component mixture and the class-conditional
   model, and one imagined digit.

## What you can do now

- Read and draw directed graphical models, and read conditional independences off them (chapter 11).
- Sample with rejection and importance sampling, Metropolis–Hastings, and Langevin dynamics, and explain when each fails
  (chapter 14).
- Derive and implement K-means and EM, and explain EM with the evidence lower bound (chapter 15).
- Derive PCA two ways, fit probabilistic PCA in closed form, and use both to compress, clean and generate (chapter 16).

## Next: part 5

Your digit generator used a **linear** decoder (PCA) and a **Gaussian mixture** in its latent space. Part 5 makes both
deep: **generative adversarial networks** (chapter 17), **normalizing flows** (chapter 18), **variational autoencoders**
(chapter 19), which maximize the same evidence lower bound you met in section 15.4, and **diffusion models** (chapter 20),
which are annealed Langevin dynamics with a learned score. You'll train a VAE and a diffusion model on digits, with your
own minigrad library.
