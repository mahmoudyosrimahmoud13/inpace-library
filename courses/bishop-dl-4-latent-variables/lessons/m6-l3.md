## Probabilistic PCA (16.2)

Turn PCA into a generative model, drawn as a simple graph z → x:

```text
z ~ N(0, I)                  (M-dimensional latent variable)
x = W z + μ + ε,   ε ~ N(0, σ² I)        (D-dimensional data)
```

- The marginal of x is Gaussian with covariance `C = W Wᵀ + σ² I` (16.2.2): a full covariance described by far fewer
  numbers.
- Maximum likelihood has a closed form (16.2.3): σ² is the **average of the discarded eigenvalues**, and W is built from
  the top M eigenvectors, scaled by `(λᵢ − σ²)^½`. As σ² → 0, it becomes ordinary PCA.
- Why bother? It gives a **likelihood** (to compare models), handles missing data, and you can **sample** new data:
  draw z, map it through W. You'll do all of that tomorrow.
- Relatives (16.2.4–16.2.6): **factor analysis** (a separate noise level per dimension), **ICA** (non-Gaussian latent
  variables), and **Kalman filters** (a linear-Gaussian latent chain over time, like a continuous HMM). Read for the ideas.

## The ELBO again (16.3)

The EM algorithm of chapter 15 works for continuous latent variables too, and gives an iterative way to fit probabilistic
PCA and factor analysis without computing the full covariance matrix (16.3.1–16.3.3).

## Nonlinear latent variable models (16.4)

The step to deep learning. Replace `W z + μ` with a **neural network** `g(z)`:

- Data near a **curved** manifold now fits (16.4.1).
- But the likelihood needs an integral over z that has no closed form (16.4.2), and that's the central difficulty of
  deep generative models.
- **Four approaches to generative modelling** (16.4.4): read this subsection carefully, it's the map of part 5:
  **GANs** avoid the likelihood altogether, **normalizing flows** make g invertible so the likelihood can be computed,
  **variational autoencoders** maximize the ELBO with a learned q, and **diffusion models** learn to undo noise step by
  step.
