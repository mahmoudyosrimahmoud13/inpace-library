# Start here

This is part 4 of 5 of the course series on **Deep Learning: Foundations and Concepts** by Christopher M. Bishop and
Hugh Bishop (Springer, 2024). Part 4 covers chapter 11 (graphical models) and chapters 14 to 16 (sampling and latent
variables). It's the most probabilistic part of the series, and it lays the foundations for the generative models of
part 5: variational autoencoders and diffusion models are built directly on the ideas here.

## Get the book

Each **Read** lesson opens the right pages of *your own copy* (the Springer PDF, or read free online at
[bishopbook.com](https://www.bishopbook.com) using the section numbers). On this course's page, press **Find it for me**
in the box **"This course reads a book you get yourself"**.

## Before you start

You need part 1's probability (Bayes' theorem, the Gaussian, likelihood, KL divergence). Parts 2 and 3 help but aren't
required: this course is mostly NumPy and probability, with no neural networks until the very end.

## The big project: models that explain data with hidden causes

You'll build, from scratch:

1. **Samplers** that draw from distributions you can only evaluate up to a constant: rejection sampling, importance
   sampling, Metropolis–Hastings, and Langevin dynamics (the engine of diffusion models).
2. **K-means** and the **EM algorithm for Gaussian mixtures**, with the log-likelihood rising at every step, as the
   theory promises.
3. **PCA and probabilistic PCA** on handwritten digits, used to compress, to clean up noisy images, and finally, with a
   Gaussian mixture in the PCA space, to **generate new digits**: your first generative model of images.

## Module projects for your CV

Days 1 to 4 each end with a real project that you build, run and publish on GitHub, with PyTorch tensors doing the
maths:

- a **Bayesian network** for the Titanic, learned from data, answering questions in both directions;
- a **part-of-speech tagger** with a hidden Markov model and the Viterbi and forward algorithms;
- **Bayesian diagnosis by MCMC**, with random-walk and Langevin samplers and per-patient uncertainty;
- an **image codec** built on K-means, measured against PNG and JPEG.

Days 5 to 7 are the big project. Each module project lesson comes with a notebook (attached to the lesson; on the website
it opens in Google Colab) and the finished code under `projects/` in the course files. For the projects:
`pip install torch pandas pyarrow scikit-learn pillow matplotlib`.
