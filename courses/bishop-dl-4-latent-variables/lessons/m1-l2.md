## Why draw probability as a graph?

A joint distribution over many variables can be written in many ways with the product rule. A **directed graph**
(11.1.1) makes one particular way visible: each node is a variable, and an arrow from a to b means "b is directly
generated from a". The graph shows the structure of the model at a glance, and it's how deep generative models are
described.

## Factorization (11.1.2)

The rule that connects a graph to a distribution: the joint is the product of each variable's distribution **given its
parents**.

```text
p(x₁, …, x_K) = Π p(x_k | parents of x_k)
```

Practise reading it both ways: from the graph, write the product; from a product, draw the graph. The graphs must have
**no directed cycles**.

## Why it saves parameters (11.1.3)

For K-state discrete variables, a fully general joint over M variables needs K^M − 1 numbers: exponential in M. If all
the variables are independent (no links at all), it needs only M(K − 1), but that model can't capture any relationships.
Graphs in between, such as a chain where each variable depends only on the previous one, trade flexibility for size.
The art of modelling is choosing which links to keep. The same idea, sharing parameters across the conditionals, gives models that
are compact but still flexible.

## Continuous and mixed graphs (11.1.4–11.1.7)

- **Gaussian variables** (11.1.4): if each node is a linear function of its parents plus Gaussian noise, the whole joint
  is one big Gaussian. Many classic models (and the probabilistic PCA of day 6) are such linear-Gaussian graphs.
- **A binary classifier** (11.1.5) drawn as a graph, and **parameters and observations** (11.1.6): learn the notation:
  **shaded** nodes are observed, small solid dots are fixed parameters, and a **plate** (a box with N in the corner) means
  "repeat this part for each of the N data points".
- **Bayes' theorem on a graph** (11.1.7): observing a variable and reasoning backwards to its causes is just Bayes' theorem,
  and the graph tells you which terms you need.
