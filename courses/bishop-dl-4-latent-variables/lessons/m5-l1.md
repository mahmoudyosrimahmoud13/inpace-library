## EM for Gaussian mixtures (15.3.1)

Alternate two steps until the log-likelihood stops changing:

- **E step**: with the parameters fixed, compute every responsibility γ(zₙₖ), the posterior probability that point n
  came from component k.
- **M step**: with the responsibilities fixed, re-estimate each component as if they were known weights: Nₖ = Σₙ γ(zₙₖ)
  (the effective number of points), the mean is the γ-weighted average, the covariance the γ-weighted covariance, and
  πₖ = Nₖ / N.

Every iteration is guaranteed **not to decrease** the log-likelihood. You'll check that in code.

## Relation to K-means and other mixtures (15.3.2–15.3.3)

K-means is EM's limit when every component has the same tiny variance: responsibilities become 0 or 1, and the M step
becomes "move to the mean". The same algorithm works for mixtures of other distributions; read the Bernoulli mixture
example on binary digit images.

## The evidence lower bound (15.4)

Why does EM work? Introduce **any** distribution q(z) over the latent variables. Then, for every q:

```text
ln p(X | θ) = L(q, θ) + KL( q(Z) ‖ p(Z | X, θ) )
```

- L(q, θ) is the **evidence lower bound** (ELBO). Since KL ≥ 0, it's always below the log-likelihood (the "evidence").
- **E step** = choose q to be the exact posterior p(Z | X, θ). The KL term becomes zero, so the bound touches the
  log-likelihood.
- **M step** = raise L with respect to θ, with q fixed. The log-likelihood rises at least as much.

## Why this matters for part 5

For a Gaussian mixture, the exact posterior over z is easy. For a deep generative model, it isn't. The fix will be to
choose q from a family a neural network can produce, and maximize the ELBO over both: that's a variational autoencoder.
