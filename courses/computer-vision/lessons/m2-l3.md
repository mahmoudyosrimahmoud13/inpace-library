# Project 2: count and measure with classic vision

You'll segment the coins, count and measure them with contours, and find the same photo again after it's rotated and
shrunk.

The code is above this text. Save it as `p2_measure.py`.

## Read the code

- **`segment()`** blurs, then thresholds two ways:
  - **Otsu** picks one threshold for the whole image.
  - **Adaptive** picks one per neighbourhood (101 pixels wide), which copes with light that's brighter on one side.
  Then an **opening** removes specks and a **closing** fills gaps inside coins.
- **`find_coins()`** finds the outer contours, drops tiny ones and small pieces cut by the photo's edge, and measures
  each: the enclosing circle (centre and radius), the area, and **circularity** (1.0 for a perfect circle).
- **Matching**: ORB finds up to 1,000 keypoints in each photo, the brute-force matcher pairs their descriptors, and
  RANSAC fits a rotation-and-scale that most pairs agree with, ignoring the bad ones.

## Run it

```bash
python p2_measure.py
```

The real output:

```text
The photo has 24 coins.

1. otsu     threshold: 21 objects found, 42% of pixels marked as coin
1. adaptive threshold: 22 objects found, 34% of pixels marked as coin

2. Measurements (22 objects): diameter 39 to 109 pixels, median 46; circularity 0.42 to 0.90
   the 3 biggest: [69, 107, 109] px; the 3 smallest: [39, 39, 39] px
   not round (circularity under 0.7): objects [20, 21, 22]. Look at p2_coins.png: two touching coins seen as one, or a coin cut by the photo's edge. Day 4 splits the touching ones.
   saved p2_coins.png with every coin circled and numbered

3. Find the photo again, turned 30 degrees and shrunk to 80%: 981 and 974 keypoints, 498 matches, 423 kept by RANSAC
   recovered: rotation 30.0 degrees, scale 0.80   (true: 30.0 and 0.80)
```

What it shows:

- **Adaptive thresholding finds more coins than Otsu.** The light in this photo is uneven, so one threshold for the
  whole photo merges or loses some.
- **22 objects for 24 coins**, and circularity tells you where the problem is: the two big, not-round objects (about
  108 pixels) are each two touching coins seen as one. The third is a coin cut by the photo's edge. Contours can't
  split touching objects; day 4 can.
- **RANSAC recovers the rotation and scale exactly**, even though some of the 498 matches are wrong. That's how
  panoramas are stitched and objects are found in a new picture.
