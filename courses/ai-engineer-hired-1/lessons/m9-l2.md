# References, offers and negotiation

## References

The field guide notes many top companies now ask for 2 to 3 references from recent managers and colleagues. Ask people early, tell them which role and what to highlight, and thank them whatever happens.

## Know your number

Research ranges on [levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/), Glassdoor and local salary surveys for your level and city (or remote region). When asked early, you can say you'd like to learn more about the role first, or give a researched range.

## Negotiate kindly and honestly

Haseeb Qureshi's widely shared [Ten Rules for Negotiating a Job Offer](https://haseebq.com/my-ten-rules-for-negotiating-a-job-offer/) sum it up: get everything in writing, keep the door open, information is power, stay positive, don't be the decision maker, have alternatives, give reasons, be motivated by more than money, understand what the company values, and be winnable. Never invent a competing offer.

## Compare offers on more than salary

Learning (who'll you learn from, what will you build), the team's AI maturity (do they have evals and production users?), total pay and its currency, remote rules, visa or relocation support, and stability. Your first AI role is mostly about what you'll learn in it.
