# A CV that gets read

Rules from the [Tech Interview Handbook's resume guide](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/) and the [field guide](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/ai-engineering-field-guide):

- **One page**, made in Word or Google Docs, sent as a PDF. Standard fonts, 10pt or more. **No multi-column layouts and no text in headers or footers**: applicant tracking systems (ATS) often scramble them. Test: copy your PDF into a plain text file; if the order is broken, the ATS sees it broken too.
- **Order:** a headline and a short summary (under 50 words), contact, skills, experience, projects, education (education first if you're a student or have under 3 years of experience).
- **Mirror the job's words** where they're true for you, and spell out abbreviations once. Don't stuff keywords: a person reads it after the ATS.
- **Impact, not tool lists.** The field guide's example: "reduced customer support response time by 40%" beats a list of model names.

## The bullet formula

Google's former head of people operations, Laszlo Bock, popularised: **"Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]."** Start with a strong verb, give a number, say how.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Worked on a chatbot using LangChain and OpenAI | Built a support chatbot answering 70% of tickets without escalation (measured on 300 real tickets) using hybrid retrieval and an evaluation harness in CI |
| Responsible for data pipelines | Cut nightly pipeline time from 3 h to 40 min by moving joins to BigQuery and partitioning by date |

## Honesty rule

Every number must be one you measured and can explain. Interviewers dig into CV bullets; an inflated number found out in the hiring manager round ends the process. Also avoid a CV that reads like a chatbot wrote it (the field guide notes recruiters spot them): write it yourself, then use AI only to check grammar.
