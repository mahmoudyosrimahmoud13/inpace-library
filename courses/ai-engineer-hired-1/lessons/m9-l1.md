# Your story bank

Behavioral rounds ask about the past to predict the future: *Tell me about a time when...* Answer with **STAR**: the **S**ituation and **T**ask in two sentences, the **A**ctions you took (most of the time, and "I", not "we"), the **R**esult with a number and what you learned. Amazon describes STAR on its interview pages, and its [Leadership Principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles) are a good list of what behavioral rounds look for.

## Prepare 8 stories that cover most questions

1. A project you're proud of (DocAnswer works)
2. A hard technical problem you debugged
3. A failure or mistake, and what you changed
4. A disagreement, and how it was resolved
5. Working with unclear requirements
6. Learning something fast
7. Going beyond your role / ownership
8. Handling a tight deadline

Write each in STAR notes (not a script), say each out loud in under 2 minutes, and map each to 2 or 3 likely questions.

## "Tell me about yourself" (90 seconds)

**Present** (who you are and what you build), **past** (the 1 or 2 experiences that led here, with a number), **future** (why this role and this company). End with a hook: "Happy to go deeper into DocAnswer's evaluation setup."

## Questions to ask them

Ask real ones: *How do you evaluate model changes before release? What does success look like in the first 90 days? What's the hardest problem the team has right now?* They show you think like an engineer, and the answers tell you if you want the job.
