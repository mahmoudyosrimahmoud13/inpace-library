# Read: partial derivatives and gradients (5.2)

For f(x₁, …, xₙ), the **partial derivative** ∂f/∂xᵢ holds the other variables fixed. Collect them in a row: the
**gradient** ∇ₓf = [∂f/∂x₁, …, ∂f/∂xₙ].

![The gradient points uphill, at right angles to the contour lines](gradient_field.png)

- The gradient points in the direction of **steepest increase**; its length is how steep. Gradient **descent** walks
  the other way.
- Note the book's convention: gradients are **row vectors** (1 × n). That makes the chain rule a plain matrix product.
  Many other texts use columns; just keep track of shapes.
- **Rules (5.2.1)** carry over: product rule, sum rule, and the chain rule, now with matrices. The **chain rule for
  multiple variables (5.2.2)**: if x depends on t, df/dt = (∂f/∂x)(∂x/∂t), a row times a column.

**Do on paper:** the example with f(x₁, x₂) = x₁²x₂ + x₁x₂³ and its partial derivatives.
