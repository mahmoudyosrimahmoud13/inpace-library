# Read: eigendecomposition and diagonalization (4.4)

If A has n linearly independent eigenvectors, put them as the columns of P and the eigenvalues on the diagonal of D:

**A = PDP⁻¹**

- Read it right to left: change to the eigenbasis (P⁻¹), scale each axis by its eigenvalue (D), change back (P).
  It's the change of basis from course 1 that makes the matrix diagonal.
- **Powers become easy**: Aᵏ = PDᵏP⁻¹. That's how you analyze repeated application (Markov chains, PageRank,
  gradient descent on a quadratic).
- For **symmetric** A, P can be **orthogonal**: A = PDPᵀ. No inverse to compute.
- Not every matrix is diagonalizable (defective ones like the shear aren't). That's the motivation for the SVD, which
  works for **every** matrix, even rectangular ones.

The book's figure (a unit circle mapped through Pᵀ, D, P) is worth staring at: rotate, stretch along the axes, rotate
back.
