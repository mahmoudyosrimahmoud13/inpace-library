# Read: matrix approximation (4.6)

Write the SVD as a sum of rank-1 matrices: A = Σᵢ σᵢuᵢvᵢᵀ. Keep only the first k terms and you get **Â(k)**, a rank-k
approximation.

![Low-rank approximations of a picture, and how fast the singular values fall](low_rank.png)

- **Eckart-Young theorem**: Â(k) is the **best** rank-k approximation of A (in spectral norm and Frobenius norm), and
  the error in spectral norm is exactly σ_{k+1}.
- If the singular values fall fast (right panel), a few terms carry most of the information. That's **compression**
  and **denoising**, and it is exactly what PCA does to data.
- The **spectral norm** ‖A‖₂ = σ₁: the most A can stretch any unit vector.

The Stonehenge example in the book is the same idea as the picture above: rank 1 already shows the big shapes, rank 5
is recognizable, and the storage is tiny compared to the full image.
