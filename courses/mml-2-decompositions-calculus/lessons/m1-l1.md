# Start here

This is part 2 of 4 of **Mathematics for Machine Learning**: **matrix decompositions and vector calculus** (chapters 4 and 5). It assumes part 1 (vectors, matrices, rank, inner products, projections).

## First, get the book (free)

This course follows **Mathematics for Machine Learning** by Marc Peter Deisenroth, A. Aldo Faisal and Cheng Soon Ong
(Cambridge University Press, 2020). The authors give the PDF away for personal use, but it may not be shared, so it is
**not inside this course**. Each lesson opens the right pages of *your own copy*.

1. Download the PDF from [mml-book.com](https://mml-book.com) (the link "PDF of the book").
2. On this course's page, in the box **"This course reads a book you get yourself"**, press **Find it for me**. inPace
   looks in your Downloads, Documents and Desktop folders (on a phone: Download, Documents, Books) and recognizes the
   book by its title and number of pages, whatever the file is called.
3. If it's somewhere else, choose the folder or the file yourself.

If inPace warns that the page count is different, you have another printing: the lessons may open a page or two off.
The course expects the 412-page PDF from mml-book.com.

*Using an older inPace? Before 1.20, rename the file to `mml-book.pdf` and put it in this course's folder. Before 1.21,
the optional task and explanation still count as required. Updating inPace fixes both.*

About 1 to 1.5 hours a day, for 8 days. The SVD (day 4) and matrix gradients (day 7) are the hardest; plan an extra day for either if you need it.

## How each module works

- **Read** lessons open the book at the right pages, with notes under them: what matters most, what to skim, and the
  intuition behind the formulas. Read with a pencil. The book's examples are worth redoing by hand.
- **Watch** lessons play a 3Blue1Brown video that shows the same idea moving. Watch it before or after the reading,
  whichever you like.
- **Explore** lessons are small interactive pages: drag, slide and see the math change.
- **Check yourself** pages give you exercises with instant feedback and worked answers. They don't count for anything.
- Each module ends with a **quiz** you need 70% to pass to open the next module. There's also a **task** and a short
  **explanation in your own words**: they're optional, but doing them is the best way to make it stick.
