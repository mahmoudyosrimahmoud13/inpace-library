# Read: determinant and trace (4.1)

Chapter 4 is about breaking matrices into simple, meaningful pieces. It starts with two numbers that summarize a
square matrix.

**Determinant**

- **Meaning**: det(A) is the factor by which A scales **area** (2D) or **volume** (3D). The sign says whether
  orientation flips (a mirror image).
- **det(A) ≠ 0 ↔ A is invertible ↔ rank is full.** A zero determinant means space gets squashed to lower dimension.
- How to compute: 2 × 2: ad − bc. 3 × 3: Sarrus' rule. Larger: **Laplace expansion** along a row or column (fine by
  hand for small matrices), or, much faster, Gaussian elimination to a triangular matrix, then multiply the diagonal.
- Rules to know: det(AB) = det(A)det(B), det(Aᵀ) = det(A), det(A⁻¹) = 1/det(A), and det(λA) = λⁿdet(A) for n × n.
  Swapping two rows flips the sign; adding a multiple of a row changes nothing.

**Trace** = sum of the diagonal. tr(AB) = tr(BA) (the "cyclic" property, used constantly in matrix calculus). It
doesn't depend on the basis, just like the determinant.

**Characteristic polynomial** p(λ) = det(A − λI) closes the section: its roots are the eigenvalues (tomorrow).
