# Read: gradients of matrices and useful identities (5.4–5.5)

**Gradients of matrices (5.4)**: the derivative of an m × n matrix with respect to a p × q matrix is a 4-dimensional
**tensor** (m × n × p × q). Two practical ways out: flatten matrices into vectors, or compute partial derivatives entry
by entry. Read the examples to see both, but don't get stuck here.

**Useful identities (5.5)**: a short table you'll look up again and again. The ones to know by heart:

- ∂(aᵀx)/∂x = aᵀ
- ∂(xᵀAx)/∂x = xᵀ(A + Aᵀ), which is 2xᵀA for symmetric A
- ∂(xᵀa)/∂x = aᵀ and ∂ tr(AX)/∂X uses the cyclic property of the trace

Test them numerically: compare with a finite difference (f(x + εeᵢ) − f(x))/ε in NumPy. That habit catches most
mistakes in hand-derived gradients.
