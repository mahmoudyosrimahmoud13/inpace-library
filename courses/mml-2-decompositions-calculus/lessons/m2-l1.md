# Read: eigenvalues and eigenvectors (4.2)

An **eigenvector** of A is a non-zero x that A only stretches: **Ax = λx**. The stretch factor λ is its **eigenvalue**.

![Most vectors change direction; eigenvectors only stretch](eigen.png)

**How to find them**

1. Solve det(A − λI) = 0 (the characteristic polynomial) for λ.
2. For each λ, find the kernel of A − λI: that's the **eigenspace**.

**Facts to remember**

- det(A) = product of the eigenvalues, tr(A) = sum of the eigenvalues. Great for checking your work.
- Eigenvalues can be **complex** (rotations have no real eigenvectors) or **repeated**. The **geometric
  multiplicity** (dimension of the eigenspace) can be smaller than the algebraic one: the shear [[1, 1], [0, 1]]
  has λ = 1 twice but only one eigen-direction. Such matrices are "defective".
- Eigenvectors for **different** eigenvalues are linearly independent.
- **Spectral theorem** (the most important result here): a **symmetric** matrix has real eigenvalues and an
  **orthonormal basis of eigenvectors**. Covariance matrices are symmetric, so PCA (course 4) relies on this.
- Every matrix AᵀA is symmetric and positive semidefinite (eigenvalues ≥ 0). That's how the SVD will be built.
