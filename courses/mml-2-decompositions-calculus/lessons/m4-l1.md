# Read: the SVD (4.5)

The **SVD** works for **any** m × n matrix: **A = UΣVᵀ**, with U (m × m) and V (n × n) orthogonal and Σ (m × n)
"diagonal" with the **singular values** σ₁ ≥ σ₂ ≥ … ≥ 0.

![Rotate, stretch, rotate: the SVD applied to the unit circle](svd.png)

**Read it as three moves**: Vᵀ rotates (in the input space), Σ stretches along the axes (and changes dimension),
U rotates (in the output space). Every linear map is "rotate, stretch, rotate".

**How it's built (4.5.2)**

- AᵀA is symmetric positive semidefinite, so it has an orthonormal eigenbasis: those eigenvectors are the columns of
  **V**, the right-singular vectors.
- σᵢ = √(eigenvalue of AᵀA).
- The left-singular vectors are uᵢ = Avᵢ / σᵢ (the columns of **U**); they are eigenvectors of AAᵀ.

**SVD vs eigendecomposition (4.5.3)**: the SVD always exists, uses two different bases (input and output), and its
"eigenvalues" are never negative. For symmetric positive semidefinite matrices the two coincide.

**The movie-ratings example** at the end is the one to remember: the SVD finds "taste" directions (sci-fi lovers,
art-house lovers) from a table of ratings. Course 4's PCA is the same computation on a data matrix.

**Do on paper:** the 2 × 3 worked example, at least the AᵀA step and one singular vector.
