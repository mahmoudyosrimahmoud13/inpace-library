# Read: higher-order derivatives, linearization and Taylor series (5.7–5.9)

- **Hessian (5.7)**: the matrix of second partial derivatives ∂²f/∂xᵢ∂xⱼ. It's symmetric for smooth f, and describes
  curvature. At a critical point (∇f = 0): all eigenvalues of the Hessian positive → minimum; all negative → maximum;
  mixed signs → **saddle point**.
- **Multivariate Taylor series (5.8)**: f(x₀ + δ) ≈ f(x₀) + ∇f(x₀)δ + ½δᵀH(x₀)δ + … The quadratic term is what makes
  step sizes tricky in course 3: a very curved direction needs a small step.

![Taylor polynomials of cos x get better near 0 as the degree grows](taylor.png)

The higher-order terms are tensors; the book's example with f(x, y) = x² + 2xy + y³ shows the bookkeeping. Then try
some of the chapter's exercises (5.7 at the end is a good backprop exercise).
