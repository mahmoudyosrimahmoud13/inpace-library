# Read: gradients of vector-valued functions (5.3)

When f: ℝⁿ → ℝᵐ has several outputs, its derivative is the **Jacobian** J, an **m × n** matrix: row i is the gradient
of output fᵢ.

- For f(x) = Ax, the Jacobian is simply **A**.
- The absolute value of the **determinant** of a Jacobian measures how a change of variables scales volume. You'll
  need it for probability densities in course 3 (6.7).
- Always check **shapes**: (outputs) × (inputs). The worked examples (gradient of least squares in particular) are the
  ones that matter for machine learning.

**Do on paper:** the least-squares example: L(θ) = ‖y − Φθ‖², with the chain rule through the error e = y − Φθ.
The result, ∂L/∂θ = −2(y − Φθ)ᵀΦ, will come back in chapter 9.
