# Liabilities: payables, accruals and loans

Liabilities are the company's obligations. Reading them well tells you **who** the company owes, **how much**, and **when**.

## Current liabilities, Green Valley 2025

| | |
|---|---|
| Accounts payable | 32,000 |
| Accrued expenses | 6,000 |
| Income tax payable | 4,000 |
| Current portion of long-term loan | 10,000 |
| **Total** | **52,000** |

- **Accounts payable:** what Green Valley owes suppliers for goods and services received. Usually no interest, due in 30 to 90 days.
- **Accrued expenses:** costs incurred but not yet billed or paid: wages for the last days of December, utilities, interest. Recorded with adjusting entries, as in the first course.
- **Income tax payable:** tax on 2025's profit, paid next year.
- **Current portion of long-term debt:** the part of a loan due within the next 12 months.

Other common current liabilities: **unearned revenue** (customer deposits), **VAT or sales tax payable** (tax collected from customers, owed to the government), **payroll liabilities** (income tax and social insurance withheld from salaries, owed to the government), **short-term bank loans and overdrafts**.

## Payables turnover

How quickly does the company pay its suppliers?

> **Days payable outstanding (DPO) = Average accounts payable ÷ COGS × 365**

Green Valley: average payables = (28,000 + 32,000) ÷ 2 = 30,000. DPO = 30,000 ÷ 294,000 × 365 = **about 37 days**.

Paying later keeps cash in the business longer, but paying too late damages relationships and loses early-payment discounts.

## Loans

Green Valley borrowed 60,000 some years ago, repaid in installments of 10,000 a year, at 7% interest.

| | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Total loan | 60,000 | 50,000 |
| Due within a year (current) | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| Due after a year (non-current) | 50,000 | 40,000 |

Each payment has two parts:

| | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Loan payable (principal) | 10,000 | |
| Interest expense | 4,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cash | | 14,000 |

Only the **interest** is an expense. The **principal** reduces the liability. (Interest is roughly 7% of the average balance over the year.)

## Interest coverage

Can operating profit easily pay the interest?

> **Interest coverage = Operating profit ÷ Interest expense** = 74,000 ÷ 4,000 = **18.5 times**

Very safe. Below about 2 to 3 times, lenders get nervous.

## Provisions and contingencies

- A **provision** is a liability of uncertain amount or timing that's *probable*: warranties on goods sold, a lawsuit the company will probably lose. It's estimated and recorded.
- A **contingent liability** is only *possible*, or can't be measured: it's not recorded, only described in the notes. Always read that note.

## Leases

Under IFRS 16, most leases (renting a shop for 5 years, leasing vehicles) appear on the balance sheet: a **right-of-use asset** and a **lease liability**. Green Valley's rent is a short, cancellable lease, so it's simply an expense here. In real annual reports, lease liabilities can be large: look for them.

## Try it

A company borrows 100,000 on January 1 at 8% interest, repaying 25,000 of principal every December 31. Write the December 31 payment entry for the first year, and show how the loan appears on the balance sheet after it.

**Answer:** Debit Loan payable 25,000, Debit Interest expense 8,000 / Credit Cash 33,000. Balance sheet: loan 75,000, of which **25,000 current** and **50,000 non-current**.
