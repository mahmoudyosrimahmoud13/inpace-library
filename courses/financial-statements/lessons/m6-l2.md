# Equity, retained earnings and dividends

Equity is the owners' claim. In a company it has two main parts, and a statement that explains how it moved.

## The parts of equity

| | 2025 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Share capital | 80,000 | 80,000 |
| Retained earnings | 56,000 | 8,000 |
| **Total equity** | **136,000** | **88,000** |

- **Share capital** (and *share premium* if shares were sold above their nominal value): money owners invested by buying shares.
- **Retained earnings:** all profits earned since the company started, minus all dividends paid. It's an accounting figure, **not a pile of cash**. Those profits have already been spent on inventory, equipment and paying debts.
- Some companies also show **reserves** (amounts set aside by law or by the board, common in Egypt and the Gulf) and **other comprehensive income** (certain gains and losses that skip the income statement, like some currency translation differences).

## Dividends

When the board **declares** a dividend, it becomes a liability; when paid, cash goes out. Dividends reduce retained earnings directly: they're **not an expense**.

| | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| On declaration: Retained earnings | 8,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dividends payable | | 8,000 |
| On payment: Dividends payable | 8,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cash | | 8,000 |

**Payout ratio** = Dividends ÷ Net profit = 8,000 ÷ 56,000 = **14%**. Green Valley keeps most of its profit to grow.

## Statement of changes in equity

**Green Valley Trading, year ended 31 Dec 2025**

| | Share capital | Retained earnings | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Balance, 1 Jan 2025 | 80,000 | 8,000 | 88,000 |
| Net profit for the year | | 56,000 | 56,000 |
| Dividends | | (8,000) | (8,000) |
| **Balance, 31 Dec 2025** | **80,000** | **56,000** | **136,000** |

If the company issued new shares, that would be a line under share capital. If it bought back its own shares (*treasury shares*), equity would go down.

## Return on equity

How much profit does the company make for each unit the owners have in it?

> **ROE = Net profit ÷ Average equity**

Average equity = (88,000 + 136,000) ÷ 2 = 112,000. ROE = 56,000 ÷ 112,000 = **50%**.

That's very high (a small, growing, low-asset shop). For large companies, 10% to 20% is common. Note that a company can raise ROE by borrowing more (less equity for the same profit), which also raises risk. Always look at ROE together with debt.

## Negative equity

If accumulated losses exceed what owners invested, equity becomes negative: liabilities exceed assets. It's a serious warning sign, and in many countries it triggers legal steps (the owners must decide whether to add capital or close).

## Try it

A company starts the year with share capital 200,000 and retained earnings 50,000. During the year it issues new shares for 40,000, makes a net profit of 70,000 and pays dividends of 30,000. Prepare its statement of changes in equity.

**Answer:** share capital 200,000 + 40,000 = **240,000**; retained earnings 50,000 + 70,000 − 30,000 = **90,000**; total equity **330,000** (from 250,000).
