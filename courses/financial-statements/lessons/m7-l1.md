# The cash flow statement: three kinds of cash

Green Valley made **56,000** of profit in 2025, but its cash went up only **18,000**. Profit isn't cash: sales on credit aren't collected yet, inventory bought isn't sold yet, equipment is paid at once but expensed over years. The **cash flow statement** explains, line by line, why cash changed.

## Three sections

| Section | What's in it | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Operating activities** | Cash from running the business | Cash from customers, paid to suppliers and staff, interest and tax paid |
| **Investing activities** | Cash spent on or received from long-term assets | Buying equipment, selling a building, buying investments |
| **Financing activities** | Cash from or to owners and lenders | New loans, loan repayments, issuing shares, paying dividends |

The three sections add up to the change in cash, which must match the balance sheet.

## Green Valley's 2025 cash flow statement

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cash flows from operating activities** | **56,000** |
| **Cash flows from investing activities** | |
| Purchase of equipment | (20,000) |
| **Net cash used in investing** | **(20,000)** |
| **Cash flows from financing activities** | |
| Repayment of long-term loan | (10,000) |
| Dividends paid | (8,000) |
| **Net cash used in financing** | **(18,000)** |
| **Net increase in cash** | **18,000** |
| Cash at 1 January | 20,000 |
| **Cash at 31 December** | **38,000** |

The operating figure is built in the next lesson. For now, see the story: the business generated 56,000 of cash from operations, spent 20,000 on new equipment, and used 18,000 to repay debt and pay owners. 18,000 was left over.

## Reading the pattern of signs

| Operating | Investing | Financing | Usually means |
|---|---|---|---|
| + | − | − | Healthy, mature: operations pay for investment, debt and dividends (Green Valley) |
| + | − | + | Growing fast: borrowing or raising money to invest more |
| − | − | + | Start-up or in trouble: living on new money |
| − | + | + / − | Selling assets to survive. A serious warning |

## Free cash flow

A number analysts love:

> **Free cash flow = Cash from operations − Capital spending** = 56,000 − 20,000 = **36,000**

It's the cash the business produces after keeping its equipment up to date: what's truly available to repay debt, pay dividends or grow.

## Where interest and dividends go

Under **IFRS** companies can choose (and must disclose):
- Interest paid: operating or financing
- Dividends paid: operating or financing (usually financing)
- Interest and dividends received: operating or investing

Under **US GAAP**, interest paid and received and dividends received go in operating; dividends paid in financing. We put Green Valley's interest in operating.

## Profit vs operating cash: the quality check

If operating cash flow is **regularly below** net profit, ask why. Common reasons: receivables or inventory piling up, or aggressive revenue recognition. Over several years, good companies turn most of their profit into operating cash. Green Valley's operating cash (56,000) equals its profit this year: a good sign.

## Try it

Sort into operating (O), investing (I) or financing (F): cash received from customers; buying a delivery truck; taking a bank loan; paying salaries; selling an old building; paying dividends; paying suppliers; issuing new shares.

**Answers:** customers O; truck I; loan F; salaries O; building I; dividends F; suppliers O; shares F.
