# The balance sheet: what you own and owe

The balance sheet (IFRS calls it the **statement of financial position**) lists assets, liabilities and equity **on one day**, sorted so a reader can judge safety at a glance.

## How the items are sorted

**Current** means within one year (or the normal operating cycle if longer).

- **Current assets** are listed by how quickly they turn into cash: cash, then receivables, then inventory (it must be sold first, then collected), then prepaids (never turn into cash, but save cash).
- **Non-current assets** are used for years: equipment, buildings, vehicles, and **intangible assets** like software, patents and brand names bought from others.
- **Current liabilities** are due within a year, including the **part of a long-term loan due next year**. Green Valley's 50,000 loan is split: 10,000 current, 40,000 non-current.
- **Equity** is share capital (what owners put in) plus **retained earnings** (profits kept in the business).

Under IFRS, many companies show non-current items **first**. The content is identical, only the order changes.

## What a balance sheet value means

Most assets are shown at **historical cost**, less depreciation or allowances. Not at market value:

- Equipment at 80,000 doesn't mean it would sell for 80,000.
- Green Valley's brand and loyal customers aren't on the balance sheet at all (it built them itself).
- So **equity is not what the company is worth**. A buyer might pay much more (or less).

Exceptions: some investments are shown at fair value, inventory is written down if it can only be sold below cost, and receivables are reduced for customers who won't pay.

## The links you can already see

- Retained earnings went from 8,000 to 56,000, a rise of 48,000. Net profit was 56,000. So 8,000 must have gone out as **dividends** (Day 6).
- Accumulated depreciation rose by 10,000: that's the year's depreciation expense on the income statement.
- Equipment at cost rose by 20,000: Green Valley bought new equipment.
- The loan fell by 10,000: a repayment.
- Cash rose 18,000.

Each of these will show up on the cash flow statement.

## Try it

Classify as current asset (CA), non-current asset (NCA), current liability (CL), non-current liability (NCL) or equity (E): delivery van; customer deposits for orders next month; inventory; a 3-year bank loan, of which 12,000 is due this year; retained earnings; software licence bought for 4 years.

**Answers:** van NCA; deposits CL (unearned revenue); inventory CA; loan: 12,000 CL and the rest NCL; retained earnings E; software NCA (intangible).
