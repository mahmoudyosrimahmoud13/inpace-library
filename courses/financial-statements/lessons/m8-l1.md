# Ratio analysis: the full toolkit

You've met most ratios along the way. Here they are in one place, grouped by the question they answer, with Green Valley's 2025 values.

## Profitability: does it make money?

| Ratio | Formula | Green Valley |
|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | Gross profit ÷ Net sales | 40.0% |
| Operating margin | Operating profit ÷ Net sales | 15.1% |
| Net margin | Net profit ÷ Net sales | 11.4% |
| Return on assets (ROA) | Net profit ÷ Average total assets | 56,000 ÷ 206,000 = 27.2% |
| Return on equity (ROE) | Net profit ÷ Average equity | 56,000 ÷ 112,000 = 50.0% |

## Liquidity: can it pay the next year's bills?

| Ratio | Formula | Green Valley |
|---|---|---|
| Current ratio | Current assets ÷ Current liabilities | 2.85 |
| Quick ratio | (Cash + Receivables) ÷ Current liabilities | 1.60 |

## Efficiency: how well does it use its assets?

| Ratio | Formula | Green Valley |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory turnover | COGS ÷ Average inventory | 294,000 ÷ 56,000 = 5.25 times |
| Days inventory | 365 ÷ Inventory turnover | 70 days |
| Receivables days (DSO) | 365 ÷ (Net sales ÷ Avg receivables) | 31 days |
| Payables days (DPO) | Avg payables ÷ COGS × 365 | 37 days |
| Asset turnover | Net sales ÷ Average total assets | 490,000 ÷ 206,000 = 2.38 |

**Cash conversion cycle** = Days inventory + Receivables days − Payables days = 70 + 31 − 37 = **64 days**. From paying a supplier to collecting from the customer, Green Valley's cash is tied up for about two months. Shorter is better.

## Solvency: is it safe long term?

| Ratio | Formula | Green Valley |
|---|---|---|
| Debt to equity | Total liabilities ÷ Equity | 0.68 |
| Interest coverage | Operating profit ÷ Interest | 18.5 times |

## Cash: is profit real?

| Ratio | Formula | Green Valley |
|---|---|---|
| Operating cash to net profit | Cash from operations ÷ Net profit | 1.00 |
| Free cash flow | Operating cash − Capital spending | 36,000 |

## How ROE is built: the DuPont view

ROE can be split into three drivers:

> **ROE = Net margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier**
> = (Net profit ÷ Sales) × (Sales ÷ Avg assets) × (Avg assets ÷ Avg equity)
> = 11.4% × 2.38 × 1.84 = **50%**

It tells you **why** ROE is high or low: profitable sales, using assets intensively, or borrowing a lot. Two companies with the same ROE can be very different businesses.

## Rules for using ratios well

1. **Compare.** Against last year (trend) and against similar companies (benchmark). A ratio alone says little.
2. **Know the industry.** Supermarkets have thin margins and fast turnover; software the opposite.
3. **Check definitions.** "Debt" might mean all liabilities or only loans. Averages or year-end figures? Be consistent.
4. **Look behind the number.** A better current ratio might just mean unsold inventory piling up.
5. **Combine ratios into a story.** "Margins steady, sales up 17%, collections on time, debt falling, cash matching profit" is a story. One ratio is not.

## Green Valley in one paragraph

Green Valley is a profitable, growing shop: 40% gross margin and 11% net margin, sales up 17%. It collects in about a month and pays suppliers in about five weeks. It's financially safe (current ratio 2.9, interest covered 18 times) and became safer during the year by repaying debt from its own cash. Its profit turned fully into cash. A lender would be comfortable; an owner might ask whether 70 days of inventory could be cut to free up cash.
