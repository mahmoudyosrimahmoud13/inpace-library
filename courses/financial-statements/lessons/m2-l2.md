# Margins: profit as a share of sales

A profit of 56,000 means nothing alone. Is it good for sales of 490,000? For sales of 5 million? Margins put profit in proportion.

## The three margins

| Margin | Formula | Green Valley |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross margin** | Gross profit ÷ Net sales | 196,000 ÷ 490,000 = **40.0%** |
| **Operating margin** | Operating profit ÷ Net sales | 74,000 ÷ 490,000 = **15.1%** |
| **Net margin** | Net profit ÷ Net sales | 56,000 ÷ 490,000 = **11.4%** |

Read them as "out of every 100 of sales":
- **40** is left after paying for the goods,
- **15** after running the business,
- **11** after interest and tax, for the owners.

## What moves each margin

- **Gross margin** moves with **prices and purchase costs**. Raising prices, buying cheaper, or selling more of the high-margin products raises it. Discounts and price wars lower it.
- **Operating margin** also moves with **running costs**. If sales grow 20% while rent and salaries stay the same, operating margin rises: that's *operating leverage*.
- **Net margin** also moves with **debt and tax**. Two shops with the same operations can have different net margins if one borrowed heavily.

## Margins differ a lot by industry

| Business | Typical gross margin | Typical net margin |
|---|---|---|
| Supermarket | 20–30% | 1–4% |
| Clothing shop | 50–60% | 5–10% |
| Software company | 70–90% | 15–30% |
| Restaurant | 60–70% (food cost only) | 3–10% |

So a 3% net margin is normal for a supermarket and terrible for software. **Compare a company with itself over time, and with its own industry.**

## Common-size income statement

Divide every line by net sales. It turns the statement into percentages, so you can compare companies of different sizes, or the same company across years:

| | 2025 | % of sales |
|---|---|---|
| Net sales | 490,000 | 100.0% |
| COGS | 294,000 | 60.0% |
| Gross profit | 196,000 | 40.0% |
| Salaries | 70,000 | 14.3% |
| Rent | 24,000 | 4.9% |
| Marketing | 12,000 | 2.4% |
| Depreciation | 10,000 | 2.0% |
| Utilities | 6,000 | 1.2% |
| Operating profit | 74,000 | 15.1% |
| Interest | 4,000 | 0.8% |
| Tax | 14,000 | 2.9% |
| Net profit | 56,000 | 11.4% |

Now it's obvious that salaries are the biggest cost after the goods themselves.

## Growth

Compare with last year. If 2024 net sales were 420,000:

> Sales growth = (490,000 − 420,000) ÷ 420,000 = **16.7%**

Healthy growth with stable or rising margins is what investors want. Growth with falling margins often means the company is buying sales with discounts.

## Try it

Two shops: Shop A sales 200,000, net profit 20,000. Shop B sales 800,000, net profit 40,000. Which has the better net margin? Which made more profit?

**Answer:** A's margin is 10%, B's is 5%, so **A is more efficient**. But **B made more profit** (40,000). Both facts matter: margin shows efficiency, the amount shows scale.
