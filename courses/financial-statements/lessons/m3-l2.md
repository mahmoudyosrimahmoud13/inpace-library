# Working capital: reading the balance sheet for safety

The first question a bank or supplier asks a balance sheet: **can this company pay its bills in the next year?**

## Working capital

> **Working capital = Current assets − Current liabilities**

Green Valley 2025: 148,000 − 52,000 = **96,000**. In 2024: 114,000 − 46,000 = 68,000. Comfortable, and improving.

Negative working capital means bills due within a year are bigger than what will turn into cash within a year. That's dangerous for most businesses (supermarkets are an exception: customers pay cash at once while suppliers wait 60 days).

## Current ratio

> **Current ratio = Current assets ÷ Current liabilities**

2025: 148,000 ÷ 52,000 = **2.85**. Each 1 of short-term bills is covered by 2.85 of short-term assets.

- Below 1: warning.
- 1.2 to 2: typical for many businesses.
- Very high (like 4 or 5) can also be a problem: too much cash sitting idle, or too much inventory not selling.

## Quick ratio (acid test)

Inventory might be slow to sell, and prepaids don't turn into cash. The quick ratio leaves them out:

> **Quick ratio = (Cash + Receivables) ÷ Current liabilities**

2025: (38,000 + 45,000) ÷ 52,000 = **1.60**. Even without selling a single item of stock, Green Valley can pay its short-term bills.

## Debt and solvency

Longer term, how much of the business is financed by borrowing?

> **Debt to equity = Total liabilities ÷ Total equity**

2025: 92,000 ÷ 136,000 = **0.68**. In 2024: 96,000 ÷ 88,000 = 1.09. Green Valley now relies much less on outside money, because it kept its profits.

Some analysts use only interest-bearing debt (the loans: 50,000 ÷ 136,000 = 0.37). Always check which definition is used.

## What changed between the years, in one glance

| | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Working capital | 68,000 | 96,000 | Stronger |
| Current ratio | 2.48 | 2.85 | Stronger |
| Quick ratio | 1.26 | 1.60 | Stronger |
| Debt to equity | 1.09 | 0.68 | Less risky |

## Read the parts, not just the totals

Ratios can look good while something is going wrong inside:

- **Receivables rising faster than sales:** customers are paying slower, or some won't pay. Green Valley's receivables rose 18% (38,000 to 45,000) while sales rose about 17%. Fine.
- **Inventory rising faster than sales:** goods aren't selling, or might be out of date. Green Valley's inventory rose 15%. Fine.
- **Cash falling while profit rises:** profit isn't turning into cash. Always look at the cash flow statement next.

## Try it

A company has: cash 10,000; receivables 15,000; inventory 40,000; accounts payable 30,000; short-term loan 20,000. Calculate working capital, the current ratio and the quick ratio. What worries you?

**Answers:** current assets 65,000; current liabilities 50,000. Working capital **15,000**, current ratio **1.3**, quick ratio (10,000 + 15,000) ÷ 50,000 = **0.5**. Worry: it can only pay its bills if it sells its inventory quickly.
