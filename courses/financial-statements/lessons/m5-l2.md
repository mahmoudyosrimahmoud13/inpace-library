# Fixed assets and depreciation methods

Equipment, buildings and vehicles are recorded at cost, then their cost is spread over the years they're used: **depreciation**. In the first course you used the straight-line method. Here are the others, and what happens when an asset is sold.

## What goes into cost

Everything needed to get the asset ready for use: price, delivery, installation, testing, import duties. Not: repairs after it's running (those are expenses), or training staff.

**Land is never depreciated**: it doesn't wear out. A building on it is.

## The example

A machine costs **50,000**, should last **5 years**, and will be worth **5,000** at the end (salvage value). It should produce 100,000 units in its life.

### Straight-line

> (Cost − Salvage) ÷ Years = (50,000 − 5,000) ÷ 5 = **9,000 per year**

The same every year. Good when the asset is used evenly. The most common method.

### Double-declining balance

Twice the straight-line rate (2 × 1/5 = **40%**), applied to the **book value at the start of each year**. Salvage is ignored at first, but you stop when book value reaches salvage.

| Year | Book value at start | Depreciation (40%) | Book value at end |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50,000 | 20,000 | 30,000 |
| 2 | 30,000 | 12,000 | 18,000 |
| 3 | 18,000 | 7,200 | 10,800 |
| 4 | 10,800 | 4,320 | 6,480 |
| 5 | 6,480 | 1,480 (down to salvage) | 5,000 |
| | | **45,000** | |

Higher at the start, lower later. Good for assets that lose value fast or are most productive when new (computers, vehicles).

### Units of production

> Rate per unit = (50,000 − 5,000) ÷ 100,000 units = **0.45 per unit**

If year 1 produces 30,000 units: 30,000 × 0.45 = **13,500**. Good when wear depends on use (machines, delivery trucks by kilometre).

## All three give the same total

Each method depreciates exactly 45,000 in total. They only change **which years** carry the cost, and therefore each year's profit.

## Selling or scrapping an asset

Compare what you get with the **book value** (cost − accumulated depreciation):

The machine (straight-line) is sold after 3 years for **25,000**. Accumulated depreciation = 3 × 9,000 = 27,000. Book value = 50,000 − 27,000 = **23,000**. Gain = 25,000 − 23,000 = **2,000**.

| | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | 25,000 | |
| Accumulated depreciation | 27,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Machine | | 50,000 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Gain on sale of equipment | | 2,000 |

Both the cost and its accumulated depreciation leave the books. A gain or loss goes below operating profit (or in "other income").

## Related ideas

- **Amortization** is the same thing for intangible assets (software, patents, licences), usually straight-line.
- **Impairment:** if an asset's value suddenly drops below its book value (a machine made obsolete by new technology), it's written down at once.
- **Estimates change.** If the machine will clearly last 7 years instead of 5, the remaining book value is spread over the new remaining life from now on. Past years aren't redone.

## Try it

A van costs 30,000, salvage 6,000, life 4 years. Find year 1 and year 2 depreciation by straight-line and by double-declining balance.

**Answers:** straight-line: 6,000 each year. Double-declining (50%): year 1: 15,000 (book value 15,000); year 2: 7,500 (book value 7,500). Year 3 would be only 1,500, to stop at the salvage of 6,000.
