# FIFO, weighted average, and LIFO

When you buy the same item at different prices during the year, which price goes to COGS and which stays in ending inventory? That's the **cost flow** question.

## The example

Green Valley sells one model of kettle:

| Date | Event | Units | Cost per unit | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jan 1 | Beginning inventory | 100 | 10 | 1,000 |
| Mar 5 | Purchase | 200 | 12 | 2,400 |
| Aug 9 | Purchase | 100 | 15 | 1,500 |
| | **Available** | **400** | | **4,900** |
| | Sold during the year | 300 | | |
| | In stock Dec 31 | 100 | | |

Prices rose during the year. The question is how to split 4,900 between the 300 sold and the 100 left.

## FIFO (first in, first out)

Assume the **oldest** units are sold first.

- COGS = 100 × 10 + 200 × 12 = **3,400**
- Ending inventory = 100 × 15 = **1,500**

Ending inventory shows recent prices (close to what it would cost to replace). When prices rise, FIFO gives **lower COGS and higher profit**.

## Weighted average

Use the average cost of all units available.

- Average = 4,900 ÷ 400 = **12.25**
- COGS = 300 × 12.25 = **3,675**
- Ending inventory = 100 × 12.25 = **1,225**

Smooths out price swings. Common for goods that are mixed together (fuel, grain) and in many accounting systems.

## LIFO (last in, first out)

Assume the **newest** units are sold first.

- COGS = 100 × 15 + 200 × 12 = **3,900**
- Ending inventory = 100 × 10 = **1,000**

When prices rise, LIFO gives the highest COGS, lowest profit and lowest tax. **LIFO is not allowed under IFRS**, only under US GAAP. You need to recognize it when reading American statements.

## Side by side

| | FIFO | Weighted average | LIFO |
|---|---|---|---|
| COGS | 3,400 | 3,675 | 3,900 |
| Ending inventory | 1,500 | 1,225 | 1,000 |
| Check: total | 4,900 | 4,900 | 4,900 |
| Gross profit if sales were 6,000 | **2,600** | 2,325 | 2,100 |

The total cost is always the same 4,900. The methods only decide **when** it hits the income statement: now (COGS) or later (when the remaining stock is sold).

## Important points

- The method is about **cost**, not physical movement. A supermarket sells old milk first, but could still use weighted average.
- **Specific identification** (tracking the actual cost of each item) is used for unique, expensive items: cars, jewelry, houses.
- A company must use its method **consistently** and disclose it in the notes. Changing it to boost profit isn't allowed without good reason and disclosure.
- When comparing two companies, check their methods. In rising prices, a FIFO company looks more profitable than an identical LIFO company.

## Try it

Beginning: 50 units at 20. Purchases: 100 at 22, then 50 at 25. Sold: 150 units. Find COGS and ending inventory with FIFO and weighted average.

**Answers:** available 200 units costing 1,000 + 2,200 + 1,250 = 4,450.
- FIFO: COGS = 50 × 20 + 100 × 22 = **3,200**; ending = 50 × 25 = **1,250**.
- Average: 4,450 ÷ 200 = 22.25; COGS = 150 × 22.25 = **3,337.50**; ending = 50 × 22.25 = **1,112.50**.
