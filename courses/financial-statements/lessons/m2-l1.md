# The income statement, line by line

The income statement shows **revenue earned and expenses incurred over a period**, ending with profit. Companies show it in one of two layouts.

## Single-step

All revenues in one block, all expenses in another, one subtraction. Simple, but it hides useful subtotals.

## Multi-step (the useful one)

It shows profit at several levels, each answering a different question:

**Green Valley Trading, Income statement, year ended 31 Dec 2025**

## Each line explained

- **Sales revenue:** the price of everything sold and delivered.
- **Sales returns and allowances:** goods customers sent back, and discounts given after the sale for damaged goods. They reduce revenue. **Net sales** is the real top line.
- **Cost of goods sold (COGS):** what the goods that were *sold* cost Green Valley to buy. Not what it bought this year: what it **sold**. (Day 4 shows how it's calculated.)
- **Gross profit:** what's left from sales after the cost of the goods. It pays for everything else.
- **Operating expenses:** the costs of running the business: salaries, rent, marketing, depreciation, utilities. Sometimes split into *selling* and *administrative* expenses.
- **Operating profit,** also called **EBIT** (earnings before interest and tax): profit from the business's main activity, before financing and tax. The best number for comparing how well two businesses *operate*.
- **Interest expense:** the cost of borrowing. It's below operating profit because it depends on how the company is **financed**, not how it operates.
- **Income tax expense:** tax on the year's profit.
- **Net profit** (net income, "the bottom line"): what belongs to the owners.

## Other income and expenses

Gains and losses outside the main business go below operating profit, so they don't distort it: interest earned on deposits, a gain on selling an old truck, foreign exchange gains or losses.

## EBITDA

You'll hear **EBITDA**: operating profit with depreciation and amortization added back. For Green Valley: 74,000 + 10,000 = **84,000**. It's popular because it's close to operating cash, but it ignores the real cost of replacing equipment. Use it with care.

## Earnings per share

Listed companies show **EPS** = net profit ÷ number of shares. If Green Valley had 80,000 shares, EPS = 56,000 ÷ 80,000 = **0.70**.

## Try it

A company has: sales 300,000; returns 5,000; COGS 180,000; operating expenses 70,000; interest 6,000; tax at 25%. Find net sales, gross profit, operating profit and net profit.

**Answers:** net sales 295,000; gross profit 115,000; operating profit 45,000; profit before tax 39,000; tax 9,750; **net profit 29,250**.
