# Project 8: watch the model, catch drift, and retrain

You'll run the champion week by week through May to December 2012, monitor its errors and inputs, and compare three
retraining policies.

The code is above this text. Save it as `p08_monitor.py`.

## Read the code

- **`fit(df, end)`** trains on everything before `end`. It also measures an **honest reference error**: a model
  trained up to 4 weeks before `end`, scored on those last 4 weeks, which it never saw.
- **`simulate(df, policy)`** starts with a model trained up to 1 May 2012 and walks through the year a week at a
  time. Each week it:
  - predicts the week's hours and, when the real counts "arrive", scores them;
  - computes `beyond_training`: the share of hours whose `last_week_avg` is higher than anything in the training
    data;
  - raises an **alert** after 2 weeks in a row with an error over 1.3 times the reference, or when over half the hours
    are beyond training;
  - retrains according to the policy: `never`, `every 4 weeks`, or `on alert`.
- **`psi()`** computes the population stability index, for the exercises.

python p08_monitor.py

What it shows:

- **In early June, 88% of hours were busier than anything the model had seen**, and it was predicting 32 bikes too
  few per hour. The input check caught it that same week, while the error was still under the alert line.
- **Late November is Thanksgiving**: one very bad week (MAE 60.7), then another, and the error rule fires.
- **From July to October the model is steadily too low** (bias around -20 to -35) while its MAE stays under 1.3 times
  the reference. The alert rules never fire. A monitor sees only what you ask it to.
- **Never retraining costs the most**: 49.1 bikes per hour on average. Retraining on alerts gets to 40.1 with 3
  retrains; every 4 weeks gets to 37.5 with 8. Here the schedule wins, because demand kept growing all year.

## Make it yours

1. Add a **bias alert**: 3 weeks in a row with bias beyond ±20. How many retrains now, and what's the MAE?
2. Combine policies: every 8 weeks, plus alerts. Find the best trade-off between retrains and MAE.
3. Use `psi()` on `temp` with a **monthly** window against the same month a year earlier. When is temperature drift
   real, and when is it just the season?
4. Read the `p06` prediction log format and write `monitor_log.py`: given a log file and the real counts, produce this
   week's row of the table.

## What to take away

- Compare live errors with an honest pre-launch error, not the training error.
- Pick input checks that match how your model fails.
- Measure your retraining policy, don't guess it. Every retrain goes through the gate.
