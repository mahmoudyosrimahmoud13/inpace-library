# Monitoring, drift, and retraining

Deployed models decay. Monitoring tells you when, and retraining fixes it. Both are easy to get wrong.

## What changes

- **Data drift (covariate shift)**: the inputs change. Hotter summers, a new city district, demand levels never seen
  in training.
- **Concept drift**: the relationship changes. Same weather, same hour, but people now ride more (or less) for other
  reasons.
- **Upstream changes**: a feed changes units, a column goes missing. Day 2's checks catch the loud ones.

## What to watch

1. **Errors, when the true answers arrive.** The best signal, but late: Bikecast learns the real count an hour later;
   a credit model may wait months. Compare each week's error with the error you measured **on unseen data before
   going live**. Comparing with the training error would make every week look bad.
2. **Inputs, right away.** Did the distribution of an input move? Measures like the **population stability index
   (PSI)** compare histograms. Choose the reference and the window with care: one week against a whole year shows
   the seasons as drift every time.
3. **What the model can't handle.** Tree models can't predict beyond the range they trained on. So for Bikecast the
   most useful input check is simple: what share of hours have a demand level higher than anything in training?
4. **Outputs**: the predictions' average and spread, and how often they hit limits.

## Alerts without noise

Weekly errors jump around: a holiday week, a storm. Alert on **sustained** problems (two bad weeks in a row) or
**clear** ones (most hours outside the training range), not on every bump. An alert nobody trusts is worse than none.

## When to retrain

- **On a schedule** (every 4 weeks): simple, predictable, and retrains even when it's not needed.
- **On an alert**: fewer retrains, but you depend on the monitor catching problems.
- **Both**: a schedule as a floor, alerts for surprises.

Every retrain goes through the same gate and registry as any other model. Retraining is a release.
