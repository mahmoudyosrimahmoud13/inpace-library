# Read: the offer and beyond (Part VIII)

Read **Part VIII** today: *Handling offers and rejection*, *Evaluating the offer*, *Negotiation* and *On the job*. This
part is short, and it can be worth more money than everything else in the book.

## Handling offers and rejection

- **Deadlines**: if you need more time to hear from other companies, ask for it politely. Many companies agree to a
  reasonable extension.
- **Rejection** is normal, even for strong engineers. Interviews are noisy: the same person can pass at one company and
  fail at another the same week. Ask the recruiter if they can share any feedback, thank them, and note the
  question topics in your log. Many companies let you apply again after six months to a year.

## Evaluating the offer

Compare offers on the **whole** package, not just salary:

| Part | Questions to ask |
|---|---|
| Salary | How does it compare with the market for this role and city? |
| Bonus | Is it guaranteed or depends on performance? How often is it paid in full? |
| Equity (shares) | How much, over how many years, and is the company public? Private shares may be worth nothing. |
| Benefits | Health insurance, leave, remote work, equipment, learning budget |
| Growth | Will you learn? Who is your manager? What does the team work on? |
| Life | Commute, working hours, stability of the company |

Salary data sites and people you know in the industry tell you what's normal for the role.

## Negotiation

Most companies expect some negotiation, and asking politely rarely loses an offer. Practical points:

- **Let them name a number first** when you can. If asked for your expectation early, give a researched range, or say
  you'd like to learn more about the role first.
- **Be specific and polite**: "I'm excited about this role. With my other offer at X, could you get closer to Y?"
- **Other offers are your strongest argument.** That's one reason to interview at several companies at once.
- **Negotiate the whole package**: if salary is fixed, ask about a signing bonus, equity, start date or title.
- Once you accept, stop negotiating and keep your word.

## On the job

The last section looks at your career after you join. Its four pieces of advice: **set a timeline** for where you want
your career to go, and check it every year; **build strong relationships**, because your next job often comes through
your network; **ask for what you want** from your manager; and **keep interviewing**, at least once a year, to keep your
skills fresh and know what's out there.

## After reading

Write the three things that matter most to you in a job, in order. When an offer arrives, judge it against that list
before you look at the salary.

## What to take away

- Rejection is normal and noisy; ask for feedback when you can, learn, and apply again later.
- Compare the whole package, and know your own priorities.
- Negotiate politely and specifically, ideally with other offers in hand.
