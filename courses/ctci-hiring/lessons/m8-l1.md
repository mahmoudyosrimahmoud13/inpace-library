# Mock interviews, and your plan for the next weeks

You now know the whole process and every topic. What turns knowledge into offers is **practice under interview
conditions**. Today you run your first mock interview and plan the weeks until your real ones.

## Run a mock interview

Find a partner: a friend who codes, a classmate, a colleague. Take turns being the interviewer, which teaches you as much
as being the candidate.

**Setup**: 45 minutes, a shared document or a plain editor without autocomplete, video on, and one problem the candidate
hasn't seen. The interviewer chooses it from the LeetCode lists in this course, at Medium level once you're ready.

**The interviewer**:

1. Reads the problem out loud and answers questions, but doesn't volunteer hints for the first 10 minutes.
2. Gives small hints when the candidate is stuck for a few minutes ("What if the list were sorted?").
3. Asks for the Big O at the end, and one follow-up ("What if the data doesn't fit in memory?").
4. Writes feedback in four parts: problem solving, coding, verification, communication (day 1's list).

**The candidate**: follows the method out loud. Listen, example, brute force, optimize, walk through, implement, test.

Spend 10 minutes on feedback after each mock. One specific note ("you started coding before checking your idea on the
example") is worth more than "good job".

No partner? Record yourself on video solving a problem out loud, then watch it. It's uncomfortable, and it works.

## A plan for the next six weeks

| Weeks | Each week |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | 2 problems a day from the course lists, Easy then Medium; redo any you needed help with three days later |
| 3 to 4 | 2 Medium problems a day, mixed topics, timed; one mock interview a week; your behavioral stories said out loud |
| 5 to 6 | Medium and some Hard; two mock interviews a week; one design question a week; apply and schedule interviews |

Adjust it to your time, but keep three habits: **a little every day**, **out loud**, and **a log** of what you missed.
Your log tells you which topic to review next.

## Applying

- Apply to several companies at about the same time, so offers arrive close together (it helps when you negotiate).
- Referrals work better than cold applications. Ask people you know at the company, briefly and politely, with your CV
  attached.
- Schedule the companies you care most about **last**, after a few practice interviews elsewhere.

## The day of the interview

- Sleep. A rested brain solves problems faster than a crammed one.
- Have water, paper and a pen. For video interviews, test your camera, microphone and internet the day before.
- If a question goes badly, let it go. Each interview is scored on its own, and the next interviewer doesn't know how
  the last one went.

## What to take away

- Mock interviews with feedback are the fastest way to improve; take turns as interviewer.
- Practise a little every day, out loud, with a log, for several weeks.
- Apply in a batch, use referrals, and save your top companies for last.
