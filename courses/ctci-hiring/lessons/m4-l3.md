# Practice: your first five problems, out loud

Today you use the method on real problems. These five are rated Easy on LeetCode, and each uses an idea from the last
two days. Solve them in the order given.

## How to do each one

1. Set a timer for **25 minutes**.
2. Talk out loud the whole time, or write your thinking as comments. Follow the flow: listen, example, brute force,
   optimize, walk through, implement, test.
3. Write the Big O of your solution before you press Submit.
4. If you're stuck at 25 minutes, read one hint or the first paragraph of a discussion post, then try for 10 more
   minutes. Only then read a full solution, and come back to the problem in three days to solve it without help.
5. Write one line in your log: problem, time, what you missed.

## The problems

| # | Problem | The idea it practises |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Two Sum](https://leetcode.com/problems/two-sum/) | Trade memory for time: a dictionary turns O(n²) into O(n) |
| 2 | [Contains Duplicate](https://leetcode.com/problems/contains-duplicate/) | A set, as in day 3's Build lesson |
| 3 | [Valid Palindrome](https://leetcode.com/problems/valid-palindrome/) | Two pointers moving towards each other |

| 5 | [Merge Two Sorted Lists](https://leetcode.com/problems/merge-two-sorted-lists/) | Walking two sorted inputs at once |

## Check your solutions

For each problem, ask yourself:

- Did I state a brute force and its Big O before optimizing?
- Is my final solution at the best conceivable runtime? If not, why not?
- Did I test an empty input and a one-item input?
- Could someone read my code without my explanation?

## What to take away

- Practise with a timer and out loud, as in a real interview.
- Read hints before solutions, and redo problems you needed help with a few days later.
- Keep a log: it shows you what to study next.
