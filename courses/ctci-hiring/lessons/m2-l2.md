# A CV that gets you to the interview

A recruiter often decides in under a minute whether your CV goes forward. Your CV has one job: to make that quick
decision easy. Here is a checklist you can apply today.

## The shape

- **One page** unless you have about ten years of experience.
- Sections in this order: contact line, experience (or projects, if you have little experience), education, skills.
- A plain layout with one column. Many companies read CVs with software (an applicant tracking system, ATS) first, and
  tables, columns and icons can confuse it.
- Save it as a PDF, named `FirstName-LastName-CV.pdf`.

## Bullets that show results

Each bullet says what you did and what came of it. A useful pattern: **accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z**.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Worked on the backend of the app | Cut API response time from 900 ms to 200 ms by adding caching for the product list |
| Responsible for testing | Wrote 120 automated tests that caught 14 bugs before release |
| Made a website for a shop | Built an online shop (Django, PostgreSQL) that took 300 orders in its first month |

Start each bullet with a strong verb (built, cut, designed, led, automated). Use numbers whenever you have them: users,
speed, money, time saved, size of data.

## Projects

If you have little work experience, projects are your experience. For each one: a name linked to its GitHub repository,
one line saying what it does, the technologies, and one result. Choose projects that are:

- **finished** and runnable, with a README that has screenshots or results;
- **real**: something people use, or a problem you actually had;
- **yours**: you can explain every part in an interview.

## Skills

List languages and tools you could use in an interview tomorrow. Don't rate yourself with stars or bars; they say
nothing. Leave out things everyone has (Microsoft Word, "teamwork").

## Before you send it

- Read it out loud. Fix every sentence that sounds odd.
- Check that every link works.
- Change the order of bullets to match each job ad: the most relevant first.
- Ask an engineer you know to spend two minutes on it and tell you what they remember. That's what a recruiter will
  remember too.

## What to take away

- One page, one column, results in every bullet, numbers wherever you can.
- Projects are experience when they're finished, real, and explained in a good README.
- Adjust the order for each job you apply to.
