# Read: the interview process, behind the scenes, special situations (Parts I to III)

Read **Parts I, II and III** of the book today. They're short and easy to read, and they explain the rules of the game
before you start training for it.

## Part I: The interview process

Its sections are *Why?*, *How questions are selected*, *It's all relative* and *Frequently asked questions*. As you
read, look for answers to these questions:

- Why do companies use algorithm questions at all, when the job is mostly building features?
- Who chooses the question you get: a central list, or your interviewer?
- What does "it's all relative" mean for you when a question feels very hard?

The last point matters most. You are compared with **other candidates who got the same question**, not with a perfect
answer. If a question is hard for you, it's probably hard for everyone, and a calm, steady attempt can still score well.

## Part II: Behind the scenes

This part describes how interviews worked at Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Apple, Facebook and Palantir when the book was
written. Read it for the **pattern**, not the details: company processes change every few years. Notice what they have in
common:

- a screening step, by phone or online, before the full day;
- several interviews in a row with different engineers;
- someone (a hiring committee, a manager, a "bar raiser") who decides, using the interviewers' written feedback.

Before a real interview, look up that company's current process on its careers page and in recent candidate reports.

## Part III: Special situations

Read the sections that fit you, and skim the rest:

- *Experienced candidates*: more weight on design and on what you've built.
- *Testers and SDETs*, *Product (and program) management*, *Dev lead and managers*: other roles, other questions.
- *Startups* and *Acquisitions and acquihires*: smaller, less formal processes.
- *For interviewers*: worth reading even as a candidate. Seeing what interviewers are told to look for tells you what
  to show them.

## After reading

Write down three things that surprised you, and one company you'd like to work for. Then find that company's current
interview steps (its careers pages usually describe them) and compare them with Part II.

## What to take away

- Interviews are scored relative to other candidates on the same question.
- Most big companies follow the same shape: a screen, a series of interviews, then a decision from written feedback.
- Company details change; check the current process before you interview.
