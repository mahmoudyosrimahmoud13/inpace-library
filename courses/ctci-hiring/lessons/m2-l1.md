# Read: before the interview (Part IV)

Read **Part IV** today. Its three sections are *Getting the right experience*, *Writing a great resume* and the
*Preparation map*.

## Getting the right experience

If you're a student or switching careers, this section is about what to do **months** before you apply. Read it with
one question in mind: what can I add to my record in the next three months? Typical answers:

- a project that real people use, or that solves a real problem, published on GitHub with a clear README;
- an internship, a part-time job, or freelance work, even small;
- contributions to an open-source project;
- courses where you built something, not just watched.

Projects count most when you can talk about them in depth: the hard part, the choices you made, and what you'd change.

## Writing a great resume

The book's advice is short and practical. As you read, check your own CV against it. The next lesson gives you a full
checklist.

## The preparation map

The map is a timeline of what to do in the months and weeks before your interviews. Copy it into your own calendar,
adjusted to the time you have. If you only have a few weeks, the order still holds: Big O and the core data structures
first, then practice problems every day, then mock interviews in the last week.

## After reading

Make a short plan with dates: which project you'll improve or build, when your CV will be ready, and how many problems
you'll solve each week.

## What to take away

- Experience you can talk about in depth (projects, internships, open source) matters more than a long list.
- Plan your preparation on a calendar, starting with fundamentals and ending with mock interviews.
