# Level check: can you do B1?

At B1, you're an **independent user**: you can deal with most situations while travelling, talk about your experiences and plans, and explain your opinions. Answer honestly: *yes*, *almost*, or *not yet*.

## I can...

- give my opinion on a familiar topic, with reasons and connectors, and react to other people's opinions;
- express wishes, hopes, advice, feelings and doubts with the subjunctive;
- talk about hypothetical situations (*si tuviera..., haría...*) and make polite requests;
- describe my studies and career, and handle a simple job interview in formal Spanish;
- write a clear formal email and an informal message;
- report what someone told me, asked me or asked me to do;
- understand the main points of conversations, podcasts and videos at natural speed on everyday topics;
- keep a conversation going for several minutes, even if I make mistakes.

For each *not yet*, return to the unit, re-listen, and redo the voice note: your second recording is usually much better.

## Final tasks

1. **Listening:** watch three Advanced videos on [Dreaming Spanish](https://www.dreaming.com/) and one [Easy Spanish](https://www.youtube.com/@EasySpanish) episode without subtitles. For each, write a 3-sentence summary and one opinion.
2. **Speaking:** record a **4-minute** voice note: introduce yourself and your career, give your opinion on a topic you care about, say what you'd change if you could, and retell a recent conversation.
3. **Writing:** write a formal email (120 words) and an opinion essay (180 words) on a topic of your choice.

## What next?

You can now learn Spanish **from Spanish**: watch series with Spanish subtitles, read news, and talk with native speakers (language exchanges and tutors). At this stage, hours of real listening and speaking matter more than more grammar. If you want an official certificate, look at the **DELE B1** or **SIELE** exams from the Instituto Cervantes.
