# Asking a friend for advice

Marta can't sleep because of stress and asks Karim for advice. The subjunctive appears after phrases of advice, feelings and doubt.

## Listen first: the conversation

**Stress at work**

| Spanish | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Marta:** Llevo dos semanas durmiendo fatal. Tengo demasiado trabajo. | I've been sleeping terribly for two weeks. I have too much work. |
| **Karim:** Lo siento mucho. ¿Has hablado con tu jefa? | I'm really sorry. Have you talked to your boss? |
| **Marta:** No, me da miedo que piense que no puedo hacerlo. | No, I'm afraid she'll think I can't do it. |
| **Karim:** Pues es importante que se lo digas. No creo que se enfade; es normal pedir ayuda. | Well, it's important that you tell her. I don't think she'll get angry; it's normal to ask for help. |
| **Marta:** Puede que tengas razón. ¿Y para dormir mejor? | You may be right. And to sleep better? |
| **Karim:** Te recomiendo que dejes el móvil una hora antes de acostarte y que salgas a caminar por la tarde. | I recommend you put your phone down an hour before bed and go for a walk in the afternoon. |
| **Marta:** Vale, lo voy a intentar. Me alegro de que seas tan buen amigo. | OK, I'll try. I'm glad you're such a good friend. |
| **Karim:** Para eso estamos. Y si necesitas hablar, aquí estoy. | That's what friends are for. And if you need to talk, I'm here. |

## Shadow it

Play the conversation in your head or read it aloud **three times**: first slowly, then at normal speed, then without looking at the meaning column. If you have the audio from the links below, pause after each line and copy the speaker's rhythm and melody, not just the words. This is called **shadowing**, and it trains your ear and your mouth together.

## Say it right

**Emphasis and emotion.** Spanish shows feeling through intonation more than through extra words. Try saying *Lo siento mucho* three ways: flat (polite), with a fall on *MU-cho* (sincere), and slowly with a higher start (very sympathetic). Then *¡Me alegro mucho!* with a rise-fall on *a-LE-gro*. Listen to how speakers on the videos do it and copy the melody.
