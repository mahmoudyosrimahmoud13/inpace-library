# Wishes and requests

Karim starts a new job tomorrow and his mother calls him. Notice the verbs after quiero que, espero que and ojalá: they're in a new form, the subjunctive.

## Listen first: the conversation

**The night before a new job**

| Spanish | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Madre:** Hijo, ¿estás nervioso por mañana? | Son, are you nervous about tomorrow? |
| **Karim:** Un poco. Espero que mis compañeros sean simpáticos. | A bit. I hope my colleagues are nice. |
| **Madre:** Seguro que sí. Quiero que me llames mañana por la noche y me lo cuentes todo. | I'm sure they will be. I want you to call me tomorrow evening and tell me everything. |
| **Karim:** Vale, mamá. Te llamo después de cenar. | OK, Mum. I'll call you after dinner. |
| **Madre:** Y acuéstate pronto. No quiero que llegues cansado el primer día. | And go to bed early. I don't want you to arrive tired on the first day. |
| **Karim:** Ya lo sé. Ojalá no haya mucho tráfico por la mañana. | I know. I hope there isn't much traffic in the morning. |
| **Madre:** Sal temprano, por si acaso. ¡Que tengas mucha suerte, cariño! | Leave early, just in case. Good luck, darling! |
| **Karim:** Gracias, mamá. Un beso. | Thanks, Mum. Love you. |

## Shadow it

Play the conversation in your head or read it aloud **three times**: first slowly, then at normal speed, then without looking at the meaning column. If you have the audio from the links below, pause after each line and copy the speaker's rhythm and melody, not just the words. This is called **shadowing**, and it trains your ear and your mouth together.

## Words and phrases to keep

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Espero que... | I hope that... |
| Quiero que me llames. | I want you to call me. |
| No quiero que llegues tarde. | I don't want you to be late. |
| ¡Ojalá! | I hope so! / If only! |
| ¡Que tengas suerte! | Good luck! |
| ¡Que te vaya bien! | Hope it goes well! |
| ¡Que lo pases bien! | Have a good time! |
| ¡Que aproveche! | Enjoy your meal! |
| por si acaso | just in case |
| Seguro que sí. | I'm sure it will. |
| Ya lo sé. | I know. |
| Te lo cuento todo. | I'll tell you everything. |

## Say it right

**Ojalá** comes from Arabic *law šā' Allāh* (if God wills) and is used by all Spanish speakers, religious or not, to mean "I hope". Spanish has around 4,000 words of Arabic origin, from eight centuries of al-Andalus: *aceite* (oil, from *az-zayt*), *azúcar* (sugar), *almohada* (pillow), *alcalde* (mayor), *hasta* (until). The stress in *ojalá* is on the last syllable: o-ja-LÁ.
