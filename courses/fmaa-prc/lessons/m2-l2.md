# Statement of Changes in Equity and Cash Flows

## Statement of Changes in Equity

**Question it answers:** Why did equity change during the period?

It reconciles the **opening** and **closing** balances of every equity account:

| Component | Opens at | + Additions | − Reductions | Closes at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common stock | $X | New shares issued | — | $X |
| Retained earnings | $X | Net income | Dividends declared | $X |
| Other components | $X | Comprehensive income | — | $X |

**Retained earnings** = Beginning retained earnings + Net income − Dividends

This is the only statement that links the income statement (net income) to the balance sheet (retained earnings).

## Statement of Cash Flows

**Question it answers:** Where did cash come from and where did it go?

### Three Sections

**1. Operating activities** — cash from the core business.

Under the **indirect method** (most common):
- Start with net income
- Add back non-cash expenses (depreciation, amortization)
- Adjust for changes in working capital:
  - Increase in current asset → subtract (uses cash)
  - Decrease in current asset → add (releases cash)
  - Increase in current liability → add (defers payment)
  - Decrease in current liability → subtract (paid out)

**2. Investing activities** — buying or selling long-term assets.

- **Outflows:** Purchase of equipment, buildings, long-term investments
- **Inflows:** Sale of equipment, receipt of principal on notes receivable

**3. Financing activities** — borrowing, repaying, and equity transactions.

- **Inflows:** Issuing stock, borrowing money
- **Outflows:** Repaying loans, paying dividends, buying back shares

### Direct vs. Indirect Method

Both methods produce the **same cash from operations number**.

| | Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|---|
| Starts with | Cash receipts from customers | Net income |
| Adjustments | Shows each cash flow | Reconciles accrual to cash |
| Use | Clearer for readers | More common in practice |

### Key Rules

- **Dividends paid** → financing outflow
- **Dividends received** → operating inflow (under US GAAP)
- **Interest paid** → operating outflow (under US GAAP)
- **Purchase of fixed assets** → investing outflow
- **Depreciation** → added back in operating (non-cash expense, never a cash flow itself)

### What the Cash Flow Statement Does NOT Show

- Profitability (use the income statement)
- Asset values (use the balance sheet)
- Accrual-basis transactions that have no cash impact

> **Exam tip:** Depreciation itself is NOT a cash flow — it is a non-cash expense. It appears in the indirect method as an **add-back** because it reduced net income but did not use cash.
