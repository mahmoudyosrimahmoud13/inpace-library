# Vertical and Horizontal Common-Size Statements

## Purpose of Financial Statement Analysis

Financial statement analysis evaluates a company's **risk and financial performance**, identifies strengths and weaknesses, and allows comparison:
- With the company's own past performance (trend analysis).
- With competitors.
- With industry averages.

## Common-Size Analysis

**Common-size statements** express each line item as a percentage of a base, removing the effect of company size.

### Vertical Common-Size Analysis

Each item is expressed as a percentage of a **base amount for that same year**.

| Statement | Base Amount |
|---|---|
| Income statement | Net Sales (Revenue) |
| Balance sheet | Total Assets |

**Purpose:** Compare the structure of the statements — what % of sales is COGS? What % of assets are current?

**Example:**

| Item | $ Amount | % of Sales |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | $1,000,000 | 100% |
| COGS | $600,000 | 60% |
| Gross profit | $400,000 | 40% |
| Operating expenses | $200,000 | 20% |
| Net income | $120,000 | 12% |

If a competitor's net income margin is 18%, the company underperforms — but vertical analysis shows *where* the gap is (COGS, operating expenses, or both).

### Horizontal Common-Size Analysis (Common Base Year / Trend Analysis)

Each item is expressed as a percentage of its **base-year amount**.

> Formula: (Current year amount ÷ Base year amount) × 100

**Purpose:** Show the trend and growth rate of individual items over time.

**Example:**

| Item | Year 1 (Base) | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | 100% | 125% | 150% |
| COGS | 100% | 130% | 160% |

Interpretation: Sales grew 50% over two years but COGS grew 60% — costs are rising faster than revenue. This signals margin compression.

**Annual growth rate formula:**
> Growth rate = (New amount − Old amount) ÷ Old amount × 100%

## Trend Analysis

Trend analysis examines multiple periods to identify patterns:
- Is revenue growing consistently?
- Are expenses growing faster or slower than revenue?
- Are margins stable, improving, or deteriorating?

**Use case:** A company shows 5-year horizontal analysis. Sales grow 10% per year, but net income only grows 3% per year. This signals rising relative costs — dig into which expense category is the culprit.

## Dollar Change vs. Percentage Change

Always compute both:
- A large dollar change in a small account may be a small % — may not be significant.
- A large % change in a small account may not be significant in dollar terms.

> **Exam tip:** Vertical analysis = same year, all items as % of a single base. Horizontal analysis = same item across different years, as % of base year. Know which is which — the FMAA frequently tests this distinction.
