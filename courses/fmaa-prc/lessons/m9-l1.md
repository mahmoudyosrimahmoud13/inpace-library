# Fixed, Variable, and Mixed Costs

## Why Cost Behavior Matters

To plan and control, a manager must predict how costs will respond to changes in activity (output volume, units produced, hours worked). Costs are classified as **fixed, variable, or mixed** based on this behavior.

**Relevant range** — the band of activity within which cost relationships are assumed to hold. Outside this range, fixed costs may step up or variable cost ratios may change.

---

## Fixed Costs (FC)

**Definition:** Costs that do NOT change in total when output changes within the relevant range.

- Total fixed costs = constant regardless of volume.
- Fixed cost **per unit decreases** as volume increases (cost is spread over more units).

**Examples:** Rent, depreciation (straight-line), salaried managers, insurance, annual maintenance contracts.

| Units Produced | Total FC | FC per Unit |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | $20,000 | $20.00 |
| 2,000 | $20,000 | $10.00 |
| 4,000 | $20,000 | $5.00 |

---

## Variable Costs (VC)

**Definition:** Costs that change **proportionally** with output.

- Total variable costs change with volume.
- Variable cost **per unit remains constant** within the relevant range.

**Examples:** Direct materials, direct labor (piecework), sales commissions, variable utilities.

| Units Produced | VC per Unit | Total VC |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | $5.00 | $5,000 |
| 2,000 | $5.00 | $10,000 |
| 4,000 | $5.00 | $20,000 |

**Note:** Economies of scale can reduce VC per unit at high volumes in reality, but for managerial accounting purposes VC per unit is assumed constant within the relevant range.

---

## Mixed (Semi-Variable) Costs

**Definition:** Costs with both a **fixed component** and a **variable component**.

**Examples:** Electricity (base charge + per-unit charge), phone bills, maintenance contracts with an hourly labor component.

**Formula:**  
Total cost = Fixed cost + (Variable cost per unit × Number of units)  
Or: **Y = a + bX**  
Where Y = total cost, a = fixed component, b = variable rate, X = activity level.

### Separating Mixed Costs — High-Low Method

1. Identify the highest and lowest activity levels and their total costs.
2. Variable rate = (Cost at high − Cost at low) ÷ (High units − Low units).
3. Fixed cost = Total cost at either point − (Variable rate × Units at that point).

**Example:**

| Month | Units | Total Cost |
|---|---|---|
| High | 5,000 | $35,000 |
| Low | 2,000 | $20,000 |

Variable rate = ($35,000 − $20,000) ÷ (5,000 − 2,000) = $5 per unit  
Fixed cost = $35,000 − ($5 × 5,000) = $10,000

---

## Cost Objects

A **cost object** is anything for which a separate measurement of cost is desired:
- A product, a service, a customer, a department, a project.

**Direct costs** — can be traced directly to a cost object (e.g., direct materials in a product).  
**Indirect costs** — cannot be traced; must be allocated (e.g., factory rent allocated to products).

> **Exam tip:** The key distinction is **total vs. per-unit behavior**: total fixed costs stay the same, total variable costs change; fixed cost per unit changes, variable cost per unit stays the same. This is frequently tested in multiple-choice questions.
