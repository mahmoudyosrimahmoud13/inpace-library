# What Is a Budget and Why It Matters

## Definition

A **budget** is a financial expression of a business plan — a set of projected values (sales, costs, cash, resources) for a specific period, usually 12 months.

**Budgeting** is the full set of efforts involved in creating, monitoring, and evaluating a budget.

## Why Organizations Budget

Budgets serve four main purposes:

1. **Planning** — translate strategy into measurable financial targets.
2. **Coordination** — align different departments (sales, production, finance) around shared targets.
3. **Control** — compare actual results to the budget; investigate variances.
4. **Motivation** — give managers clear targets; performance is measured against the budget.

Since the 1950s, budgets have evolved from simple forecasts into performance management tools. Weekly, monthly, and quarterly budget reviews track actual vs. budgeted figures. Managers are **evaluated based on budget performance**.

## Characteristics of a Successful Budget

| Characteristic | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| **Management support** | The tone at the top drives commitment |
| **Participation** | Involving those responsible increases buy-in |
| **Realistic targets** | Stretch goals that are achievable motivate; impossible targets demoralize |
| **Timeliness** | Budget must be completed before the period it covers |
| **Flexibility** | Should be updated if external conditions change materially |
| **Communication** | Goals and results must be shared clearly across the organization |

## The Planning Hierarchy

```
Mission and Vision
      ↓
  Long-term Strategy (3–5 years)
      ↓
  Annual Budget (1 year)
      ↓
  Monthly/Quarterly Variance Reports
```

The budget converts the organization's **future plans into numbers** (dollars, volume, hours) and establishes measures for monitoring both business performance and employee performance.

## Variance Analysis

**Variance** = Actual result − Budgeted result

| Variance | Sales | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Favorable | Actual > Budget | Actual < Budget |
| Unfavorable | Actual < Budget | Actual > Budget |

**Purpose of variance analysis:**
- Identify where performance deviates from plan.
- Trigger investigation — is the variance a one-time event or a trend?
- Enable corrective action before problems compound.

## When to Revise the Budget

A budget may be revised (with management approval) when:
- Economic conditions change significantly.
- Industry factors shift materially.
- Major unexpected events occur.

If significant changes are not reflected, the budget becomes obsolete and misleading as a performance benchmark.

> **Exam tip:** Know the four purposes of budgeting (planning, coordination, control, motivation). Distinguish between a **favorable** variance (good news) and an **unfavorable** variance — the direction depends on whether it's a revenue or a cost item.
