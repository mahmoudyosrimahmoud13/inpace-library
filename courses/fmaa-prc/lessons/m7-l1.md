# Liquidity, Leverage, and Activity Ratios

## Why Ratios?

Ratios distill financial statements into comparable measures. Each ratio answers a specific question about liquidity, leverage, activity, or profitability.

**Unless otherwise stated:** Use end-of-year balance sheet data; full-year income statement/cash flow data.

---

## Liquidity Ratios

Measure the ability to meet **short-term obligations**.

| Ratio | Formula | What It Tells You |
|---|---|---|
| **Current ratio** | Current assets ÷ Current liabilities | General short-term liquidity |
| **Quick ratio (acid test)** | (Cash + Marketable securities + Accounts receivable) ÷ Current liabilities | Liquidity without relying on selling inventory |
| **Cash ratio** | (Cash + Marketable securities) ÷ Current liabilities | Most conservative — cash-only coverage |
| **Cash flow ratio** | Operating cash flow ÷ Current liabilities | Cash-based liquidity |
| **Net working capital ratio** | Net working capital ÷ Total assets | Working capital as a % of total assets |

**Quick ratio excludes inventory** because inventory may take time to sell and may not sell at full value.

**Example:** Current assets = $7,500,000. Current ratio before a purchase = 2.3×.
- If inventory is purchased on credit, both current assets (+inventory) and current liabilities (+payable) increase.
- The current ratio falls because: when CR > 1, adding equal amounts to numerator and denominator reduces the ratio.

## Leverage Ratios (Solvency)

Measure the ability to meet **long-term obligations** and the degree to which the company is financed by debt.

| Ratio | Formula | What It Tells You |
|---|---|---|
| **Debt to equity** | Total debt ÷ Equity | How much debt per dollar of equity |
| **Long-term debt to equity** | (Total debt − Current liabilities) ÷ Equity | Long-term leverage |
| **Debt to total assets** | Total debt ÷ Total assets | Proportion of assets financed by debt |
| **Times interest earned (TIE)** | EBIT ÷ Interest expense | How many times earnings cover interest |

**Times interest earned** is the **best indicator of long-term debt-paying ability** (debt coverage from operating earnings).

**Using debt in capital structure:**
- **Benefit:** Financial leverage — if ROA > interest rate, debt magnifies returns for equity holders.
- **Risk:** Increases fixed obligations; can lead to financial distress.

## Activity (Efficiency) Ratios

Measure how efficiently the company uses its assets.

| Ratio | Formula | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Accounts receivable turnover** | Credit sales ÷ Average gross A/R | Times A/R collected per year |
| **Days sales in receivables** | 365 ÷ A/R turnover (or Avg A/R ÷ Credit sales/365) | Average days to collect |
| **Inventory turnover** | COGS ÷ Average inventory | Times inventory sold per year |
| **Days sales in inventory** | 365 ÷ Inventory turnover | Average days to sell inventory |
| **Accounts payable turnover** | Credit purchases ÷ Average A/P | Times payables paid per year |
| **Days purchases in payables** | 365 ÷ A/P turnover | Average days to pay suppliers |
| **Total asset turnover** | Sales ÷ Average total assets | Revenue generated per dollar of assets |
| **Fixed asset turnover** | Sales ÷ Average net PP&E | Revenue from long-term assets |

**High A/R turnover** = fast collections (good). **Low inventory turnover** = slow-moving stock (potential problem).

> **Exam tip:** Know the difference between A/R turnover (uses credit sales) and inventory turnover (uses COGS). Days ratios = 365 ÷ turnover ratio.
