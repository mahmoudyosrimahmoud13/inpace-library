# Property, Plant, Equipment and Intangible Assets

## Property, Plant, and Equipment (PP&E)

PP&E are tangible long-lived assets used in operations — not held for sale.

**Recorded at cost**, which includes:
- Purchase price
- Freight and installation
- Sales taxes and import duties
- Testing costs before the asset is ready for use

### Depreciation

**Depreciation** = allocating the cost of a tangible asset over its useful life. It is a **non-cash** expense.

**Depreciable cost** = Cost − Salvage (residual) value

**Three common methods:**

**1. Straight-line (SL):**
> Annual depreciation = (Cost − Salvage) ÷ Useful life

Equal expense each year. Simplest method.

**2. Declining balance (accelerated):**
> Annual depreciation = Book value × Depreciation rate

Higher expense early in life, lower later. Rate is often double the straight-line rate (double-declining balance / DDB).

**3. Units of production (activity-based):**
> Depreciation per unit = (Cost − Salvage) ÷ Total expected units  
> Annual depreciation = Units produced × Depreciation per unit

Expense matches actual usage. Used for machinery where hours or output vary significantly.

### Disposal of PP&E

When an asset is sold or retired:

1. Record depreciation up to the disposal date.
2. Remove the asset and its accumulated depreciation.
3. Record any proceeds received.
4. Recognize a **gain** if proceeds > book value, or a **loss** if proceeds < book value.

**Book value** = Cost − Accumulated depreciation

> When a fixed asset is sold for **less** than book value, a **loss** is recorded. The loss decreases assets (asset is gone) and decreases equity (reduces net income).

## Intangible Assets

Intangible assets have no physical form but provide economic benefits.

| Asset | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Patents** | Legal protection for an invention. Amortized over useful life (max 20 years). |
| **Copyrights** | Protection for creative works. Amortized over useful life. |
| **Trademarks** | Brand identifiers. Indefinite life — no amortization, tested for impairment. |
| **Goodwill** | Excess paid over fair value of net assets in an acquisition. Indefinite life — no amortization, tested for impairment annually. |
| **Customer lists / franchise rights** | Finite life — amortized. |

### Amortization vs. Depreciation

- **Depreciation** — tangible assets (PP&E). Can use various methods.
- **Amortization** — intangible assets with finite lives. Usually straight-line.
- **Depletion** — natural resources (oil, timber, minerals). Based on units extracted.

### Impairment

If the carrying value (book value) of an asset exceeds its recoverable amount (or fair value), the asset is **impaired** and must be written down. The write-down is an expense on the income statement.

Goodwill and intangibles with indefinite lives are tested for impairment **at least annually**.

> **Exam tip:** Depreciation methods affect the timing of expense recognition but NOT the total depreciation taken over the asset's life (total depreciation = cost − salvage value, always).
