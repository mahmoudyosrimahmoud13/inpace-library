# Current and Long-Term Liabilities

## What Is a Liability?

A liability is a **probable future sacrifice of economic benefits** arising from a present obligation to transfer assets or provide services as a result of a past transaction.

Three criteria:
1. Present obligation exists.
2. The obligation will require a future sacrifice of resources.
3. The event that created the obligation has already occurred.

## Classification: Current vs. Non-Current

**Current liabilities** — due within one year (or the operating cycle, whichever is longer):
- Accounts payable
- Accrued expenses (wages, taxes, interest)
- Short-term notes payable
- Current portion of long-term debt
- Deferred (unearned) revenue — obligation to deliver goods/services

**Non-current liabilities** — due beyond one year:
- Long-term bonds payable
- Long-term bank loans
- Deferred tax liabilities
- Lease obligations (long-term)

## Short-Term Debt

Short-term debt typically finances:
- Temporary increases in inventory (seasonal businesses).
- Timing mismatches between receipts and payments.

It carries **refinancing risk** — if conditions change, the company may not be able to refinance.

## Long-Term Debt

**Bonds payable:**
- A bond is a written promise to pay a specified amount (face value) at maturity plus periodic interest (coupon).
- If the coupon rate = market rate → bond issued at **par**.
- Coupon rate > market rate → bond issued at a **premium** (above par).
- Coupon rate < market rate → bond issued at a **discount** (below par).

**Mortgage payable:**
- Secured by real property.
- Monthly payments include both principal repayment and interest expense.

**Lease obligations:**
- **Operating lease** — essentially a rental; no asset or liability recorded (under old rules).
- **Finance (capital) lease** — the asset and liability appear on the balance sheet.

## Accrued Liabilities

Expenses incurred but not yet paid. Examples:
- Wages earned by employees but not yet paid (Wages Payable).
- Interest accumulated on a loan (Interest Payable).
- Taxes owed but not yet remitted (Taxes Payable).

**Journal entry for an accrual:**  
Dr Expense / Cr Accrued Liability

## Contingent Liabilities

A potential liability that depends on a future event (e.g., a pending lawsuit).

| Likelihood | Action |
|---|---|
| Probable AND estimable | Record the liability |
| Probable but not estimable | Disclose in notes |
| Reasonably possible | Disclose in notes |
| Remote | Generally no disclosure needed |

> **Exam tip:** Know the difference between current and non-current liabilities. A long-term loan's portion due in the next 12 months is reclassified as a **current liability**.
