# Balance Sheet and Income Statement

## The Four Main Financial Statements

1. **Balance sheet** (Statement of Financial Position)
2. **Income statement** (Statement of Operations)
3. **Statement of changes in equity**
4. **Statement of cash flows**

Each answers a different question about the company.

---

## Balance Sheet

**Question it answers:** What does the company own and owe *at a specific point in time*?

**Structure:** Assets = Liabilities + Equity

### Assets (in order of liquidity)

**Current assets** (expected to be used within 1 year):
- Cash and cash equivalents
- Short-term investments / marketable securities
- Accounts receivable (net of allowance for bad debts)
- Inventory
- Prepaid expenses

**Non-current assets:**
- Property, plant, and equipment (net of accumulated depreciation)
- Intangible assets (patents, trademarks, goodwill)
- Long-term investments

### Liabilities

**Current liabilities** (due within 1 year):
- Accounts payable
- Accrued expenses (wages, taxes)
- Short-term debt
- Current portion of long-term debt

**Non-current liabilities:**
- Bonds payable
- Long-term loans
- Deferred tax liabilities

### Equity
- Common stock (at par value)
- Additional paid-in capital
- Retained earnings
- Treasury stock (subtracted)

### Limitations of the Balance Sheet

- Uses historical cost — assets may not reflect current market value.
- Omits valuable intangibles like brand strength or workforce talent.
- A snapshot — does not show how the company got to this position.

## Income Statement

**Question it answers:** How much did the company earn and spend *over a period of time*?

### Structure

```
Revenue (Sales)
− Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
= Gross Profit
− Operating Expenses (SG&A, R&D, depreciation)
= Operating Income (EBIT)
± Other income/expense (interest, gains, losses)
= Income Before Tax
− Income Tax Expense
= Net Income
```

### Key Terms

**Revenue** — inflows from the company's main operations (selling goods, rendering services).

**COGS** — the direct costs of the goods sold (materials, direct labor, manufacturing overhead).

**Gross profit** = Revenue − COGS. Measures production efficiency.

**Operating income (EBIT)** = Gross profit − Operating expenses. Measures core business performance.

**Net income** — the "bottom line." Goes into retained earnings unless paid as dividends.

**Gains and losses** — from peripheral (non-operating) activities, e.g., selling a building. Gains increase equity; losses decrease it.

> **Exam tip:** The income statement does NOT include owner investments or dividend payments — those appear on the statement of changes in equity or the cash flow statement.
