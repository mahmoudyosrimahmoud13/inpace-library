# Role of Accounting and Types of Business

## What Is Accounting?

Accounting is the process of **identifying, classifying, measuring, recording, and communicating** — in monetary terms — the transactions and events of an economic entity that have a financial character.

Its purpose: measure, interpret, and communicate the results of economic activity so that decisions can be made.

## Three Types of Accounting

| Type | Audience | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| **Financial accounting** | External users (investors, creditors, regulators) | GAAP / IFRS |
| **Management accounting** | Internal managers | No fixed standard — whatever is useful |
| **Tax accounting** | Government / tax authorities | Tax law |

**Key difference:** Financial accounting follows strict external standards and is published. Management accounting is flexible and confidential — it exists to help managers run the business.

## Users of Accounting Information

**Internal users** — managers, board members, employees — use accounting to plan, control, and make decisions.

**External users** include:

- Owners and investors — evaluate the business and decide whether to invest.
- Creditors and banks — assess creditworthiness before lending.
- Government agencies — for taxation and regulation.
- Customers and suppliers — to assess stability.

## Types of Business Organizations

| Type | Key Feature | Liability of Owners |
|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | One owner, simple | Unlimited personal liability |
| Partnership | Two or more owners | Generally unlimited |
| Corporation | Separate legal entity, shares | Limited to investment |
| Limited liability company (LLC) | Hybrid | Limited |

A **corporation** is the most common form for large businesses because owners (shareholders) are not personally liable for the company's debts.

## Accounting Principles (GAAP Foundations)

- **Going concern** — assume the business will continue operating.
- **Accrual basis** — record revenues when earned and expenses when incurred, not when cash moves.
- **Consistency** — use the same methods from period to period.
- **Materiality** — only information significant enough to affect decisions needs to be disclosed.
- **Conservatism** — when in doubt, choose the option that is least likely to overstate assets or income.

## Management vs. Financial Accounting — Quick Comparison

| | Financial | Management |
|---|---|---|
| Users | External | Internal |
| Rules | GAAP/IFRS | None required |
| Time focus | Historical | Future-oriented |
| Frequency | Periodic (annual, quarterly) | As needed |
| Verification | Audited | Not audited |

> **Exam tip:** The FMAA tests both sides. If a question says "for use by external stakeholders," think financial accounting. If it says "for decision-making" or "for managers," think management accounting.
