# Corporate Governance and Risk Management

## What Is Corporate Governance?

Corporate governance is the system by which organizations are **directed and controlled**. It includes the rules, practices, relationships, and processes that balance the interests of stakeholders: the board, management, shareholders, creditors, customers, suppliers, regulators, and the community.

**Primary goal:** Enhance the long-term value of the company for shareholders while accounting for other stakeholders' interests.

Key elements: monitoring, oversight, assurance, risk management, control, transparency, accountability, and an ethical environment.

## The Governance — Risk — Control Triangle

```
        Governance
       (sets direction)
           ▲
          / \
         /   \
  Risk Mgmt — Control
(identifies  (manages
  risks)      risks)
```

- **Governance** sets the broad framework and strategy.
- **Risk management** identifies risks and decides how to respond.
- **Internal control** implements the actions that manage those risks.

They are interdependent: good governance needs both risk management and control; and both of those rely on the governance framework to give them direction.

## Corporate Governance Principles

**OECD / best practice principles:**

1. **Transparency** — disclose accurate and timely information.
2. **Accountability** — the board is accountable to shareholders; management is accountable to the board.
3. **Fairness** — treat all shareholders equally, including minority holders.
4. **Responsibility** — recognize the rights of all stakeholders, not just shareholders.

## The Board of Directors

The board is elected by shareholders to oversee management on their behalf. Key board responsibilities:

- Approve strategy, major decisions, and budgets.
- Hire, evaluate, and (if necessary) remove the CEO.
- Oversee the financial reporting process.
- Set the "tone at the top" for ethical culture.

**Independent directors** (not employed by the company) are important because they can challenge management objectively.

## Audit Committee

A subcommittee of the board, ideally made up of independent directors, that oversees:

- Internal and external audit processes.
- Financial reporting integrity.
- Internal control systems.

## Risk Management

**Risk** = the possibility that an event will occur and adversely affect the achievement of objectives.

**Risk management process:**

1. **Identify** potential risks (operational, financial, compliance, strategic).
2. **Assess** likelihood and impact.
3. **Respond** — avoid, reduce, share, or accept the risk.
4. **Monitor** — continuously track risk levels and control effectiveness.

**Types of risk:**

- Inherent risk — risk before any controls are applied.
- Residual risk — risk remaining after controls are in place.
- Control risk — the risk that a control fails to prevent or detect an error.

> **Exam tip:** Governance, risk management, and internal control are three distinct but connected concepts. Know how each relates to the others.
