# Trend Analysis and Reading Financial Data

## What Makes Analysis Useful?

Analysis of financial statements is only useful when it has **context**:
- Compare to the company's own history (trend).
- Compare to industry benchmarks.
- Compare to specific competitors.

A single number means little. A ratio of 2.5 is good or bad depending on the industry and trend.

## Common-Size Income Statement — Detailed Look

For each period, every income statement item is expressed as a % of net sales:

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Gross profit % | Gross profit ÷ Net sales |
| Operating expense % | Operating expenses ÷ Net sales |
| Net profit margin % | Net income ÷ Net sales |

**Interpretation example:**
- Year 1: Gross margin = 42%, Net margin = 10%
- Year 2: Gross margin = 38%, Net margin = 7%

The drop in gross margin means higher production costs relative to sales price (pricing pressure or cost increases). The further drop in net margin means operating expenses also rose as a % of sales.

## Common-Size Balance Sheet — Detailed Look

Each balance sheet item as a % of total assets:

- Current assets % of total assets → shows liquidity orientation.
- PP&E % of total assets → shows capital intensity.
- Current liabilities % of total assets → shows short-term obligation weight.
- Long-term debt % of total assets → shows leverage level.

## Base Year Statements — Step by Step

1. Choose a base year (usually the earliest year of analysis).
2. Set all base-year values to 100%.
3. For each subsequent year: (Year N value ÷ Base year value) × 100.
4. Values above 100 show growth; below 100 show decline.

**Example:**

| Account | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accounts receivable | 100% | 118% | 142% |
| Sales | 100% | 110% | 125% |

Accounts receivable grew faster than sales → customers are taking longer to pay, or the company is extending more credit. This may signal collection problems.

## Linking Statements

Common-size analysis works best when you read all statements together:

- Income statement → profitability trends.
- Balance sheet → asset and liability structure.
- Cash flow statement → quality of earnings (is profit backed by cash?).

**Red flag:** A company showing growing net income but declining operating cash flow. Accrual-basis income may be real, or it may be driven by aggressive revenue recognition. Cash flow doesn't lie in the same way.

## Limitations of Common-Size and Trend Analysis

- Different accounting methods (FIFO vs. LIFO, depreciation methods) make comparisons across companies unreliable.
- Common-size ratios can be distorted by one-time items.
- Industry norms vary — a 50% gross margin is normal in software; it would be extraordinary in grocery retail.
- Inflation can distort historical comparisons.

> **Exam tip:** The FMAA may give you two years of data and ask you to calculate a growth rate, common-size %, or identify a trend. Practice the arithmetic: (new − old) ÷ old for growth rates, item ÷ base for common-size.
