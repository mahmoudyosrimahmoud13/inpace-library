# Fixed and Variable Factory Overhead

## What Is Factory Overhead?

**Factory overhead** (manufacturing overhead) = all indirect manufacturing costs — everything needed to manufacture products other than direct materials and direct labor.

Factory overhead is a **product cost** — it flows into inventory and eventually into Cost of Goods Sold.

## Components: Fixed vs. Variable

### Fixed Factory Overhead

Costs that do not change with production volume within the relevant range:

- Rent or depreciation of manufacturing building.
- Depreciation of manufacturing equipment (not usage-based).
- Salary of the manufacturing supervisor.
- Annual repair/maintenance contracts.
- Annual insurance (not volume-based).
- Wages of non-production plant workers (janitorial, security).

Fixed overhead per unit **decreases as production increases** because the total is spread over more units.

### Variable Factory Overhead

Costs that change proportionally with production volume:

- Indirect labor (production support workers).
- Indirect materials (lubricants, cleaning supplies, small tools).
- Insurance based on production volume.
- Individual repairs and maintenance.
- Utilities based on machine hours or volume.

Variable overhead per unit **stays constant** as production changes.

### Mixed Factory Overhead

Some overhead items are mixed (e.g., electricity — a base fixed charge plus a per-unit variable component). These must be separated into fixed and variable components for analysis.

## Predetermined Overhead Rate

Because actual overhead is only known at year-end, overhead is **applied to production** using a predetermined rate:

**Predetermined overhead rate** = Budgeted total overhead ÷ Budgeted activity base

**Common activity bases:**
- Direct labor hours (DLH) — traditional, works when labor is the main driver.
- Machine hours — better for automated production.
- Direct labor cost.
- Units of production.

**Applied overhead** = Predetermined rate × Actual activity used

### Over- and Under-Applied Overhead

At year-end, compare applied overhead to actual overhead:

- **Over-applied** = Applied > Actual → overhead was overstated → adjustment reduces COGS.
- **Under-applied** = Applied < Actual → overhead was understated → adjustment increases COGS.

The variance is usually small and closes to Cost of Goods Sold.

## Corporate-Wide vs. Departmental Overhead Rates

**Plant-wide (corporate-wide) rate:**
- One rate for the entire factory.
- Simple but inaccurate if departments have very different overhead structures.

**Departmental rates:**
- Separate rate per department (machining, assembly, finishing).
- More accurate — reflects that machining is machine-intensive, assembly is labor-intensive.
- Required when different products use departments in different proportions.

**Example:** Product A uses 10 hours in machining and 1 hour in assembly. Product B uses 1 hour in machining and 10 hours in assembly. A plant-wide rate will cost both products the same — which is wrong. Departmental rates give a more accurate cost.

> **Exam tip:** Know which costs are fixed vs. variable overhead. Fixed overhead per unit changes with volume (the total does not). Variable overhead per unit is constant.
