# Accounts Receivable and Inventory Valuation

## Accounts Receivable

**Receivables** are amounts owed to the company by customers and others for goods sold, services rendered, or advances made.

**Trade receivables** = amounts from normal business operations (customers).  
**Nontrade receivables** = amounts from other sources (loans to employees, tax refunds, etc.).

### Cash (Sales) Discounts

A cash discount incentivizes early payment. Example: **2/10, n/30** means:
- 2% discount if paid within 10 days
- Full amount due in 30 days

**Gross method** — record the full amount; recognize the discount if the customer pays early.  
**Net method** — record the net amount; recognize "Sales Discount Forfeited" revenue if the customer pays late.

### Uncollectible Accounts

Companies must estimate bad debts. Two methods:

**1. Allowance method (GAAP preferred):**
- Estimate uncollectible accounts in advance (matching principle).
- Record: Dr Bad Debt Expense / Cr Allowance for Doubtful Accounts.
- Balance sheet shows Accounts Receivable **net** of the allowance.

**2. Direct write-off method:**
- Write off when actually uncollectible.
- **Not GAAP** (violates matching principle) but simpler.

## Inventory

**Inventory** includes items held for sale or used in producing goods for sale.

**Three types (manufacturing):**
- **Raw materials** — inputs not yet used.
- **Work in progress (WIP)** — partially completed goods.
- **Finished goods** — completed products ready for sale.

### Inventory Costs (Product Costs)

Inventory is recorded at **cost**, which includes:
- Purchase price (net of discounts)
- Freight-in (costs to bring goods to location)
- Receiving and inspection costs

**Freight-out** (cost to deliver to customers) is a selling expense, NOT an inventory cost.

### Inventory Cost Flow Assumptions

When identical items are purchased at different costs, an assumption is needed:

| Method | Description | Effect in Rising Prices |
|---|---|---|
| **FIFO** (First-In, First-Out) | Oldest cost → COGS; newest cost stays in inventory | Lower COGS, higher profit, higher inventory |
| **LIFO** (Last-In, First-Out) | Newest cost → COGS; oldest cost stays in inventory | Higher COGS, lower profit, lower inventory |
| **Weighted Average** | Average cost per unit for all | Between FIFO and LIFO |
| **Specific Identification** | Track actual cost of each item | Used for unique/high-value items |

> LIFO is **not permitted under IFRS** but is allowed under US GAAP.

### Lower of Cost or Net Realizable Value (LCNRV)

Inventory is reported at the **lower of** its cost or its net realizable value (NRV = estimated selling price − costs to complete and sell).

If NRV falls below cost, write down the inventory and record an impairment loss.

### Inventory Management

**Safety stock** — extra inventory kept to guard against unexpected demand or supply delays. It does NOT protect against *predictable* demand increases.

**Just-in-time (JIT)** — minimize inventory by receiving goods only when needed. Reduces carrying costs but requires reliable suppliers.

> **Exam tip:** Know the effect of each cost flow method on COGS, net income, and inventory value — both in rising and falling price environments.
