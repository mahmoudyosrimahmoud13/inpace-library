# Profitability Analysis and Return on Investment

## Performance Measurement — Why It Matters

Most organizations divide into **business units** (BUs), each with specific responsibilities. Performance measurement:
- Communicates strategy and aligns employee goals with organizational goals.
- Tracks performance against targets.
- Identifies problem areas.
- Evaluates managers (basis for rewards and promotion).
- Guides future strategy.

---

## Profitability Analysis

Profitability analysis identifies which customers, products, or business units are **most and least profitable**, enabling management to:
- Optimize the product mix.
- Improve pricing.
- Focus sales efforts on profitable customers.
- Eliminate or improve unprofitable products.

### Product Profitability

**Product profitability** = Revenue from product − Total costs attributable to the product

All relevant costs (direct and allocated indirect) must be traced to the product.

### Business Unit (BU) Profitability

A **business unit** is typically an independent profit or investment center. BU profitability analysis traces all relevant revenues and costs to the BU, including:
- Traceable fixed costs (costs that disappear if the BU is eliminated).
- Allocated common costs (shared headquarters overhead — may or may not be included depending on the analysis purpose).

**Segment margin** = Revenue − Variable costs − Traceable fixed costs

Do NOT eliminate a business unit based solely on negative net income if its **segment margin is positive** — it still contributes to covering common fixed costs.

### Customer Profitability

**Customer profitability** = Revenue from customer − All customer-specific costs

Customer-specific costs include:
- Order processing.
- Shipping and returns.
- Sales calls and account management.
- Customer-specific product modifications.
- Special payment terms.

A high-volume customer may actually be less profitable than a smaller one who requires fewer services.

## Return on Investment (ROI)

**ROI** = Income of business unit ÷ Assets of business unit

(Where "income" = operating income unless otherwise stated)

Also written as: ROI = Profit margin × Asset turnover  
= (Net income / Sales) × (Sales / Assets)

### Interpreting ROI

- Compare to: the company's cost of capital, the return from alternative investments, prior periods, and peer BUs.
- **Higher ROI** = better use of assets to generate profit.

### Advantages of ROI
- Simple and widely understood.
- Comparable across BUs of different sizes.
- Encourages efficient asset management.

### Disadvantages of ROI
- May discourage good investments that would lower the BU's ROI even if they exceed the company's minimum required return.
- Can be manipulated by reducing the asset base (not investing in new assets, accelerating depreciation).
- Uses accounting income, not cash flows.

---

## Residual Income (RI)

**RI** = Income of business unit − (Assets × Required rate of return)

RI measures the **dollar amount of income earned above** the minimum required return.

**Advantage over ROI:** A BU will accept a project if RI > 0, even if the project would lower its ROI but exceeds the company's minimum required return. This aligns BU and company goals better.

**Disadvantage:** Cannot be compared across BUs of different sizes (a large BU will have larger RI in absolute terms).

> **Exam tip:** Know the ROI formula and its decomposition into margin × turnover. Know the difference between ROI and Residual Income — especially when each gives the "correct" investment decision.
