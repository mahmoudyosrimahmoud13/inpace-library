# The Accounting Equation and Key Terminology

## The Accounting Equation

**Assets = Liabilities + Equity**

This equation always balances. Every transaction affects at least two accounts and keeps the equation in balance.

**Assets** — what the company *owns* or controls: cash, inventory, equipment, buildings, receivables.

**Liabilities** — what the company *owes*: loans, accounts payable, wages payable, bonds.

**Equity** — the owners' residual claim: Assets − Liabilities = Equity.

Also written as: **Equity = Assets − Liabilities**

## Expanded Equation

Equity = Paid-in Capital + Retained Earnings

Retained Earnings = Prior Retained Earnings + Net Income − Dividends

Net Income = Revenues − Expenses

So: **Assets = Liabilities + Paid-in Capital + Retained Earnings**

## Debits and Credits

The double-entry system: every transaction has equal debits and credits.

| Account Type | Increases with | Decreases with |
|---|---|---|
| Assets | Debit (Dr) | Credit (Cr) |
| Liabilities | Credit (Cr) | Debit (Dr) |
| Equity | Credit (Cr) | Debit (Dr) |
| Revenue | Credit (Cr) | Debit (Dr) |
| Expenses | Debit (Dr) | Credit (Cr) |

Memory aid: **DEAL** — Dividends, Expenses, Assets, Losses → all increase with a Debit.

## Common Accounting Terms

**Chart of accounts** — a numbered list of all accounts a company uses.

**Journal entry** — the record of a transaction, showing accounts debited and credited.

**General ledger** — the master record of all accounts and their balances.

**Trial balance** — a list of all accounts and their balances at a point in time, used to verify that debits = credits.

**Accrual** — recognizing a revenue or expense before cash is received or paid.

**Deferral** — delaying recognition of cash already received or paid until it is earned or incurred.

## Working Capital and Daily Finances

**Net working capital** = Current assets − Current liabilities

Working capital represents the funds available for day-to-day operations. Positive working capital means the company can meet short-term obligations; negative working capital is a danger sign.

**Current assets** — cash, receivables, inventory, prepaid expenses — items expected to convert to cash within one year.

**Current liabilities** — accounts payable, short-term loans, accrued expenses — obligations due within one year.

## Example: The Equation in Action

A company starts with $50,000 cash from the owner (equity), then buys $20,000 of inventory on credit.

| Event | Assets | Liabilities | Equity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner invests $50,000 | +$50,000 cash | — | +$50,000 |
| Buy inventory on credit | +$20,000 inventory | +$20,000 payable | — |
| **Totals** | **$70,000** | **$20,000** | **$50,000** |

Check: $70,000 = $20,000 + $50,000 ✓

> **Exam tip:** When a question asks how a transaction affects the accounting equation, work through it step by step — what increases, what decreases, and by how much.
