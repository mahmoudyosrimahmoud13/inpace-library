# Business Ethics and Ethical Decision-Making

## What Is Business Ethics?

**Business ethics** (corporate ethics) are the principles and codes of conduct that guide business activities and decision-making.

As a field of study, it analyzes whether organizational behaviors and practices are appropriate or questionable.

## Key Concepts

**Ethics** — the philosophical understanding of what is right and wrong (good and bad). A framework for judgment.

**Morality** — the application of ethical principles on a *personal* level. Morals are person-specific.

**Virtue** — the character of an individual regarding conformity to ethical standards.

| | Moral employee | Virtuous employee |
|---|---|---|
| Does | Applies the organization's ethical principles | Goes further — does the right thing even when not required |
| Sets | — | New standards of behavior for others to follow |

## Organizational Ethics

An organization's ethics are largely **inseparable from management's ethics** ("tone at the top"). The ethical conduct of employees is shaped by:
1. Their own personal morals.
2. The guidelines and culture established by the board and management.

## Ethical Gray Areas

Where to draw the line is genuinely difficult. The FMAA presents scenarios like:
- When is a gift from a supplier a simple gesture vs. a bribe?
- When is accepting a supplier's entertainment appropriate vs. a conflict of interest?
- When is firing a poor performer acceptable despite their personal circumstances?
- Should a company sell products it knows are harmful?
- When should a bank extend a loan to someone who may not repay?

There are no perfect answers to these — the FMAA tests awareness of the **tensions**, not simple rules.

## Ethical Decision-Making Frameworks

**Utilitarian approach** — choose the action that produces the greatest good for the greatest number.

**Rights-based (deontological) approach** — some actions are right or wrong regardless of outcomes. Focus on duties and rights.

**Justice/fairness approach** — distribute benefits and burdens fairly. Apply rules consistently.

**Virtue approach** — ask what a person of virtuous character would do.

**Common good approach** — consider the welfare of the community and society, not just individuals.

## IMA Statement of Ethical Professional Practice

For management accountants, the IMA sets four standards:

1. **Competence** — develop knowledge and skills; perform with professional competence; comply with relevant laws and standards.

2. **Confidentiality** — keep information confidential; don't disclose information without authorization; don't use confidential information for personal advantage.

3. **Integrity** — avoid conflicts of interest; refuse gifts that might influence behavior; communicate unfavorable as well as favorable information.

4. **Credibility** — communicate information fairly and objectively; disclose all relevant information; disclose delays and deficiencies.

## When Faced with an Ethical Dilemma

The IMA recommends a structured approach:
1. Consult the organization's policies.
2. Consult the immediate supervisor (unless they are involved in the conflict).
3. If unresolved, escalate to the next level, audit committee, or board.
4. Consult legal counsel if needed.
5. If internal channels fail, consider reporting to regulatory authorities (whistleblowing).

Seek legal advice before resigning or disclosing externally — protections vary.

> **Exam tip:** Know the four IMA ethical standards (Competence, Confidentiality, Integrity, Credibility). The FMAA will test which standard is violated in a scenario.
