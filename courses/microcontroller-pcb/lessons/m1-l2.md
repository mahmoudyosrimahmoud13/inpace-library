# Pins, ports and the datasheet

## The pinout

The 28-pin DIP ATmega328P, with the names Arduino uses in brackets:

```text
                 ┌───U───┐
   (RESET) PC6  1│       │28  PC5 (A5 / SCL)
   (D0 RX) PD0  2│       │27  PC4 (A4 / SDA)
   (D1 TX) PD1  3│       │26  PC3 (A3)
      (D2) PD2  4│       │25  PC2 (A2)
      (D3) PD3  5│       │24  PC1 (A1)
      (D4) PD4  6│       │23  PC0 (A0)
           VCC  7│       │22  GND
           GND  8│       │21  AREF
  (XTAL1)  PB6  9│       │20  AVCC
  (XTAL2)  PB7 10│       │19  PB5 (D13 / SCK)
      (D5) PD5 11│       │18  PB4 (D12 / MISO)
      (D6) PD6 12│       │17  PB3 (D11 / MOSI)
      (D7) PD7 13│       │16  PB2 (D10 / SS)
      (D8) PB0 14│       │15  PB1 (D9)
                 └───────┘
```

Pins are grouped in **ports**: B, C and D. "PB5" is port B, bit 5. Arduino renames them as simple numbers (PB5 = digital pin 13).

## Pins that must always be connected

| Pin | Connect to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 7 VCC | +5 V, with a 100 nF capacitor to pin 8 | power for the digital part |
| 8 and 22 GND | ground (both!) | |
| 20 AVCC | +5 V, with a 100 nF capacitor to 22 | power for the ADC and port C; **must** be connected even if you don't use analog |
| 21 AREF | a 100 nF capacitor to ground (or nothing) | ADC reference; never connect directly to 5 V if you use the internal reference |
| 1 RESET | 10 kΩ pull-up to +5 V | a low level resets the chip; a floating reset causes random restarts |

## Pins with special jobs

Most pins can be plain digital inputs or outputs, but many also have a second job:

| Job | Pins |
|---|---|
| Crystal (clock) | 9, 10 (XTAL1, XTAL2) |
| Serial (UART) to a computer | 2 RX, 3 TX |
| Programming (ISP / SPI) | 17 MOSI, 18 MISO, 19 SCK, 1 RESET |
| I²C (sensors) | 27 SDA, 28 SCL |
| Analog inputs | 23 to 28 (A0 to A5) |
| PWM (dimming, motor speed) | 5, 11, 12, 15, 16, 17 (D3, D5, D6, D9, D10, D11) |
| External interrupts | 4, 5 (D2, D3) |

Plan your board around these: put the LED on a PWM pin if you want to dim it, sensors on the I²C pins, and keep 2/3 free for the USB-serial connection.

## Limits from the datasheet

The ATmega328P datasheet is about 300 pages. You don't read it front to back; you look things up. The parts you need most:

- **Pin configurations** (the pinout above)
- **Electrical characteristics**: the limits
- **Clock sources**, **Reset**, **I/O ports** chapters when designing

Key limits:

```python
limits = {
    "max current per I/O pin": "40 mA (absolute max); design for 20 mA or less",
    "max total current through VCC and GND": "200 mA",
    "supply for 16 MHz": "about 4.5 to 5.5 V",
    "input HIGH threshold at 5 V": "above about 3 V (0.6 x VCC)",
    "input LOW threshold at 5 V": "below about 1.5 V (0.3 x VCC)",
}
for k, v in limits.items():
    print(f"{k:40} {v}")
```

```output
max current per I/O pin                  40 mA (absolute max); design for 20 mA or less
max total current through VCC and GND    200 mA
supply for 16 MHz                        about 4.5 to 5.5 V
input HIGH threshold at 5 V              above about 3 V (0.6 x VCC)
input LOW threshold at 5 V               below about 1.5 V (0.3 x VCC)
```

So: an LED with a resistor directly on a pin is fine; a motor, relay or LED strip needs a transistor (from the electronics course). And a 3.3 V sensor's output (3.3 V) counts as HIGH for a 5 V ATmega.

## Summary

- 28 pins in ports B, C, D; Arduino numbers map to them (PB5 = D13).
- Always connect VCC, both GNDs, AVCC (each with 100 nF), and a 10 kΩ pull-up on RESET.
- Plan pins by their special jobs (crystal, UART, ISP, I²C, ADC, PWM); stay within 20 mA per pin.
