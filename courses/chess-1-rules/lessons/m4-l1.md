# How to start a game: opening principles

You don't need to memorize openings yet. Follow a few principles and you'll get a good position almost every time.

## The principles

1. **Fight for the center.** Start with a center pawn: **1. e4** or **1. d4**. Pawns and pieces in the center control more squares.
2. **Develop your pieces.** Bring knights and bishops off the back rank, usually knights first (to f3 and c3, or f6 and c6).
3. **Castle early**, usually by move 8 or 10. Your king is safer, and a rook joins the game.
4. **Don't move the same piece twice** in the opening without a good reason.
5. **Don't bring the queen out early.** Enemy pieces will chase it and gain time.
6. **Connect your rooks:** when nothing stands between them on the back rank, your development is complete.

## A good setup

1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 3. Bc4 Bc5 4. c3 Nf6 5. d3 d6 6. O-O O-O

```
8  r . b q . r k .
7  p p p . . p p p
6  . . n p . n . .
5  . . b . p . . .
4  . . B . P . . .
3  . . P P . N . .
2  P P . . . P P P
1  R N B Q . R K .
   a b c d e f g h
```

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/r1bq1rk1/ppp2ppp/2np1n2/2b1p3/2B1P3/2PP1N2/PP3PPP/RNBQ1RK1_w_-_-_2_7)

Both sides followed every principle: center pawns, four minor pieces out, kings castled. This is the **Italian Game**, and you'll study it properly in the third course.

## A bad example

1. e4 e5 2. Qh5 Nc6 3. Qf3 Nf6 4. Qb3 d5

```
8  r . b q k b . r
7  p p p . . p p p
6  . . n . . n . .
5  . . . p p . . .
4  . . . . P . . .
3  . Q . . . . . .
2  P P P P . P P P
1  R N B . K B N R
   a b c d e f g h
```

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/r1bqkb1r/ppp2ppp/2n2n2/3pp3/4P3/1Q6/PPPP1PPP/RNB1KBNR_w_KQkq_-_0_5)

White moved the queen four times and developed nothing else. Black has two knights out and a strong center. Every queen move gave Black a free move.

## When the opponent breaks the principles

If your opponent brings the queen out early, **develop with tempo**: bring out a piece that attacks the queen. You gain a move every time the queen has to run.

## Try it

After 1. e4 e5, which move fits the principles better: **2. Nf3** or **2. h4**?

**Answer:** **2. Nf3**: it develops a piece, attacks the pawn on e5 and prepares castling. 2. h4 does nothing for the center or development.

## Your practice

Play three games against the Lichess computer at level 1 or 2. In each game, check after move 10: are both your knights and bishops out, and have you castled? Write down the move you castled on.
