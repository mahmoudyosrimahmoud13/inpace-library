# Playing your first games

## A routine for every move

Most beginner games are lost by simply leaving a piece where it can be taken, or missing an enemy threat. Before **every** move, ask:

1. **What did my opponent's last move do?** What does that piece attack now? Is there a threat?
2. **Are any of my pieces undefended or attacked?**
3. **For me: checks, captures, threats.** Look at every check, every capture and every move that attacks something.
4. Choose a move, then do a **blunder check**: "If I play this, what is the best reply for my opponent?"

This takes ten seconds and will win you more games than any opening.

## A trap that teaches a lesson

The game below is famous. Black pins White's knight and thinks it can't move. White moves it anyway.

1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 d6 3. Bc4 Bg4 4. Nc3 g6 5. Nxe5 Bxd1 6. Bxf7+ Ke7 7. Nd5#

```
8  r n . q . b n r
7  p p p . k B . p
6  . . . p . . p .
5  . . . N N . . .
4  . . . . P . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  P P P P . P P P
1  R . B b K . . R
   a b c d e f g h
```

*Checkmate.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/rn1q1bnr/ppp1kB1p/3p2p1/3NN3/4P3/8/PPPP1PPP/R1BbK2R_b_KQ_-_2_7)

Black grabbed the queen, and White mated with three minor pieces. This is called **Légal's mate**. Black's 4...g6 wasted time, and 5...Bxd1?? lost on the spot. The lesson: before taking a big prize, ask what your opponent's move threatens. (Black had to play 5...dxe5, giving up a pawn.)

## Time controls

- **Bullet** (1–2 minutes) and **blitz** (3–5 minutes): fast and fun, but you won't learn much at first.
- **Rapid** (10–30 minutes): best for learning. Start with **15+10**: 15 minutes each, plus 10 seconds added after every move.
- **Classical** (an hour or more): club and tournament games.

## Where to play

**Lichess** (lichess.org) is free, with no ads, and has puzzles, lessons and analysis. **Chess.com** is also popular. Both give you a **rating**, a number that goes up when you win and down when you lose, and pair you with players of similar strength.

## Good habits

- Say "good game" and mean it, win or lose. Don't leave a lost game without resigning.
- After each game, look at it again with the analysis board. Find the moment where the evaluation changed the most, and ask: which question from the routine would have saved me?
- Don't play ten games in a row when you are losing. Take a break.

## Try it

Your opponent just moved a bishop to a square where it attacks your undefended knight. Which question of the routine catches this?

**Answer:** question 1 (what did their last move do?) and question 2 (are any of my pieces attacked or undefended?).

## Your practice

Play five rapid games (15+10) on Lichess. After each one, open the analysis and write down your biggest mistake and which step of the routine would have prevented it. Then move on to the next course: tactics.
