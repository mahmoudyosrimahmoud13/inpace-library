# Reading and writing moves

Chess players write games down using **algebraic notation**. You need it to read books, follow lessons, and study your own games.

## The rules

- Each move gives the **piece letter** and the **square it goes to**: **Nf3 (knight to f3)** means a knight moves to f3, **Bb5 (bishop to b5)** a bishop to b5.
- **Pawn moves have no letter**: **e4** means a pawn moves to e4.
- A **capture** adds **x**: **Bxf7 (bishop takes on f7)** (bishop takes on f7). A pawn capture names the file it came from: **exd5 (pawn takes on d5)**.
- **Check** adds **+**, **checkmate** adds **#**.
- **Castling:** **O-O (castles kingside)** kingside, **O-O-O (castles queenside)** queenside.
- **Promotion:** **e8=Q (pawn to e8 and becomes a queen)**.
- If two of the same piece can go to the same square, add the file (or rank) it comes from: **Nbd2 (knight to d2)** (the knight from the b-file goes to d2), **R1e2 (rook to e2)**.

A full move is a White move and a Black move with a number: **1. e4 e5**. When only Black's move is shown, write three dots: **1... e5**.

Symbols you will see in books: **!** good move, **?** mistake, **??** blunder, **!?** interesting.

Play these moves on a board (or the Lichess link). White opened with the king's pawn, both sides brought out a knight, White's bishop went to b5, and Black asked it a question with ...a6. This opening is called the **Ruy Lopez** (or Spanish Game), one of the oldest in chess.

## Why write your games

On Lichess and Chess.com your games are saved automatically. In a club or tournament you write each move on a score sheet. Either way, the notation lets you go back after the game and find where it turned.

## Try it

1. What do **Qxe7+ (queen takes on e7, check)** and **O-O-O** mean?
2. Write in notation: White's pawn on d4 captures a piece on e5.

**Answers:** 1. The queen captures on e7 with check; castling queenside. 2. **dxe5 (pawn takes on e5)**.
