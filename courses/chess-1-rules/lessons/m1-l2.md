# How the pieces move, and what they are worth

A piece **captures** by moving onto a square with an enemy piece and removing it. You can never move onto a square with your own piece, and only the knight can jump over other pieces.

## The rook

Moves any number of squares in a straight line: up, down, left or right.

```
8  . . . . . . . .
7  . . . . . . . .
6  . . . . . . . .
5  . . . . . . . .
4  . . . R . . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  . . . . . . . .
1  . . . . K . . k
   a b c d e f g h
```

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/8/8/8/8/3R4/8/8/4K2k_w_-_-_0_1)

The rook on d4 can reach every square on the d-file and the 4th rank: 14 squares.

## The bishop

Moves any number of squares diagonally. A bishop stays on one color for the whole game, so each side has a light-squared and a dark-squared bishop.

## The queen

The strongest piece. Moves like a rook **and** a bishop: any number of squares in any straight or diagonal line.

```
8  . . . . . . . .
7  . . . . . . . .
6  . . . . . . . .
5  . . . . . . . .
4  . . . Q . . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  . . . . . . . .
1  . . . . K . . k
   a b c d e f g h
```

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/8/8/8/8/3Q4/8/8/4K2k_w_-_-_0_1)

From d4 this queen controls 27 squares.

## The king

Moves one square in any direction. The king can never move to a square where it would be attacked. It is the most important piece, but in the endgame it also becomes a strong fighter.

## The knight

Moves in an **L**: two squares in one direction, then one square to the side. It always lands on a square of the other color, and it jumps over anything in the way.

```
8  . . . . . . . .
7  . . . . . . . .
6  . . . . . . . .
5  . . . . . . . .
4  . . . N . . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  . . . . . . . .
1  . . . . K . . k
   a b c d e f g h
```

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/8/8/8/8/3N4/8/8/4K2k_w_-_-_0_1)

The knight on d4 can go to c2, e2, b3, f3, b5, f5, c6 and e6. In a corner a knight has only 2 squares, so remember: **a knight on the rim is dim**.

## The pawn

- Moves **straight forward** one square. From its starting square it may move **one or two** squares.
- **Captures diagonally** forward, one square.
- Never moves backward.

```
8  . . . . k . . .
7  . . . . . . . .
6  . . . . . . . .
5  . . . p . p . .
4  . . . . P . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  . . . . . . . .
1  . . . . K . . .
   a b c d e f g h
```

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/4k3/8/8/3p1p2/4P3/8/8/4K3_w_-_-_0_1)

The white pawn on e4 cannot move forward if e5 is blocked, but it can capture on d5 or f5.

## What the pieces are worth

| Piece | Points |
|---|---|
| Pawn | 1 |
| Knight | 3 |
| Bishop | 3 |
| Rook | 5 |
| Queen | 9 |
| King | priceless (the game depends on it) |

Use these numbers when you trade. Giving a knight (3) for a rook (5) wins you 2 points, which players call **winning the exchange**. Giving a queen for a rook loses 4 points. The numbers are a guide, not a law: an active piece can be worth more than a passive one.

## Try it

White has a rook on a1. Black has pieces on a8 and h1. Which one can the rook capture?

**Answer:** both: a8 is on the same file, h1 is on the same rank, and nothing blocks either line (if nothing stands in between).

## Your practice

On a board, place a knight on e4 and count its moves (8). Place it on h1 and count again (2). Then place a queen on e4 with no other pieces and count (27).
