# Your first checkmates

Every player must be able to win with a big advantage. These patterns come up again and again.

## The back-rank mate

You saw it in the last module: a rook or queen checks the king on its back rank while its own pawns block the escape. Defend against it by giving your king a little room: a quiet pawn move like h3 (or ...h6) is often called **making luft** ("air").

## Queen and king against king

With a queen you can force mate. The method:

1. Use the queen to **shrink the king's box**, keeping a knight's move away from the king so you never give stalemate.
2. When the king is on the edge, bring **your own king** close.
3. Give mate with the queen, supported by the king.

![Chess board](board-b85e3a14d8.png)

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess to play it out](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/7k/8/6K1/8/8/8/8/1Q6_w_-_-_0_1)

The final picture: the black king on the edge, the white king two squares in front of it, the queen mating along the edge (**Qb8# (queen to b8, checkmate)**). Another common finish is the queen right next to the king, protected by your king: with the white king on g6, **Qg7# (queen to g7, checkmate)** works too.

## Rook and king against king

Slower, but also forced. Push the king to the edge with the rook and your king working together, then mate along the edge:

![Chess board](board-0da8cbe076.png)

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess to play it out](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/k7/8/1K6/8/8/8/8/7R_w_-_-_0_1)

The white king on b6 takes a7 and b7 away from the black king, and the rook checks along the 8th rank: **Rh8# (rook to h8, checkmate)**.

## The scholar's mate, and how to stop it

Beginners often try this four-move trap:

![Chess board](board-ca55f884d4.png)

*Checkmate (the last move is shown in yellow).* [Open on Lichess to play it out](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/r1bqkb1r/pppp1Qpp/2n2n2/4p3/2B1P3/8/PPPP1PPP/RNB1K1NR_b_KQkq_-_0_4)

The queen and bishop both aim at **f7**, the weakest square in Black's camp (only the king defends it). After 3...Nf6?? White plays **Qxf7# (queen takes on f7, checkmate)**.

Black stops it easily with **3...g6** (attacking the queen) or **3...Qe7 (queen to e7)** (defending f7). After 3...g6 4. Qf3 Nf6, Black is fine, and White's queen has wasted time. So: know the trap, but don't rely on it. Good opponents will punish your early queen.
