# Check, checkmate, stalemate and draws

## Check

When a piece attacks the enemy king, the king is **in check**. The player in check must get out of check **at once**, in one of three ways:

1. **Move** the king to a safe square.
2. **Block** the attack by putting a piece in between (not possible against a knight or a pawn).
3. **Capture** the attacking piece.

You may never make a move that leaves your own king in check.

## Checkmate

If the king is in check and **none** of those three ways works, it is **checkmate** and the game ends. The side giving mate wins.

```
8  . . . . . . k .
7  . . . . . p p p
6  . . . . . . . .
5  . . . . . . . .
4  . . . . . . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  . . . . . P P P
1  R . . . . . K .
   a b c d e f g h
```

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/6k1/5ppp/8/8/8/8/5PPP/R5K1_w_-_-_0_1)

After **Ra8**, the black king is in check. It cannot move (its own pawns block f7, g7 and h7, and the rook covers f8 and h8), nothing can block, and nothing can take the rook. Checkmate. This is the **back-rank mate**, one of the most common in real games.

## Stalemate

If the player to move is **not** in check but has **no legal move**, the game is a **draw** by stalemate. This is a trap for the winning side.

```
8  . . . . . . . k
7  . . . . . Q . .
6  . . . . . . K .
5  . . . . . . . .
4  . . . . . . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  . . . . . . . .
1  . . . . . . . .
   a b c d e f g h
```

*Stalemate: Black to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/7k/5Q2/6K1/8/8/8/8/8_b_-_-_0_1)

Black to move. The king on h8 is not in check, but g8, g7 and h7 are all covered. Black has no legal move, so it is stalemate and the game is a draw, even though White has a whole queen more. When you are winning, always check that your opponent has a move.

## Other draws

- **Insufficient material:** neither side can possibly mate, for example king against king, or king and bishop against king.
- **Threefold repetition:** the same position appears three times with the same player to move.
- **Fifty-move rule:** 50 moves by each side with no capture and no pawn move.
- **Agreement:** the players agree to a draw.
