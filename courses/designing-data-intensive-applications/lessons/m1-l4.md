Today's script is `p01_load_and_latency.py`.

## Read the code

**Part A: Chirp, a tiny Twitter.**

- `followers` gives each of 10,000 users a follower count from a **Pareto distribution**: most users have a handful
  of followers, a few have thousands. That's what real social graphs look like, and it's what makes the problem hard.
- A day of traffic: 20,000 posts and 600,000 timeline reads (reads are 30 times more common, as on Twitter).
- **Fan-out on read** costs one write per post, and one lookup per followed account on every read.
- **Fan-out on write** costs one write per follower on every post, and one lookup per read.
- The **hybrid** fans out on write except for accounts with 1,000 followers or more; their posts are fetched at read
  time.

**Part B: response times.**

- `one_call()` returns a time around 20 ms; 2% of calls hit a slow path of 200 to 1,200 ms.
- `pct()` computes a percentile the simple way: sort, then pick the value at that position.
- Tail latency amplification: a "page" makes N calls in parallel and waits for the slowest, `max(...)`.

## Run it

```bash
python p01_load_and_latency.py
```

```text
B. 100,000 backend calls (milliseconds)
   mean    35.2   median (p50)    20.2
   p95     38.6   p99   714.2   p999  1170.2
   The mean hides the slow requests; the high percentiles show what your unhappiest users wait.

   Tail latency amplification: a page that needs N backend calls in parallel waits for the slowest
     1 calls per page: median page    20.2 ms, p99   791.9 ms, pages slower than 200 ms:   2.1%
     5 calls per page: median page    30.7 ms, p99  1121.9 ms, pages slower than 200 ms:   9.7%
    20 calls per page: median page    43.0 ms, p99  1198.2 ms, pages slower than 200 ms:  32.9%
    50 calls per page: median page   530.2 ms, p99  1212.1 ms, pages slower than 200 ms:  63.6%
```

What it shows:

- Fan-out on read does about **75 times more work** than fan-out on write for this read-heavy load.
- But fan-out on write turns a single post by the biggest account into thousands of writes. The hybrid costs a bit
  more in total, but no single post is a burst of thousands of writes: it trades total work for **predictable**
  work.
- The **mean (35 ms) is almost twice the median (20 ms)**, yet tells you nothing about the 1% who wait about 700 ms.
- With 50 backend calls per page, the **median page** is slow. The rare slow call has become the normal experience.

## What to take away

- The distribution of the load (followers per user) decides the design, not the average.
- Measure percentiles, and remember that fan-out multiplies the tail.
