# Read: the Preface and Chapter 1, Reliable, Scalable, and Maintainable Applications

Read the **Preface** quickly: note who the book is for and its outline (three parts: foundations, distributed data,
derived data). Then **Chapter 1**, which defines the three words the whole book is about.

## Thinking about data systems

Why "data-intensive" rather than "compute-intensive": the hard problems are the amount of data, its complexity and how
fast it changes. Look at Figure 1-1: one application built from a database, a cache, a search index and a message
queue. Your job is increasingly to combine tools like these into one reliable system.

## Reliability

"Continuing to work correctly, even when things go wrong." Note the difference between a **fault** (one part going
wrong) and a **failure** (the whole system stops serving users), and the idea of deliberately causing faults to test
your tolerance (Netflix's Chaos Monkey). Then the three kinds of fault: **hardware**, **software** (often correlated,
so they hit many machines at once) and **human** (in one study of large internet services, operators' configuration
errors were the leading cause of outages). Read the list of
ways to reduce human error.

## Scalability

The most practical section. Read it slowly.

- **Describing load** with **load parameters**, and the **Twitter example**: posting versus reading the home
  timeline, and the two ways to build the timeline. The key insight: the distribution of followers per user decides
  which approach is cheaper. You'll rebuild this example in today's project.
- **Describing performance**: **throughput** versus **response time**; why response time is a **distribution**, not a
  number; **percentiles** (p50, p95, p99, p999); why the slowest requests often come from your most valuable customers;
  **head-of-line blocking**; and **tail latency amplification**.
- **Approaches for coping with load**: scaling up versus out, elastic systems, and why there's no generic "magic
  scaling sauce".

## Maintainability

Three design principles: **operability** (make it easy to run), **simplicity** (remove accidental complexity with good
abstractions) and **evolvability** (make change easy). Most of the cost of software is maintenance, not writing it.

## After reading

For an app you use every day, name two load parameters and one response time percentile you would want on its
dashboard. Why that percentile and not the average?

## What to take away

- Reliability: tolerate faults so they don't become failures.
- Scalability: describe load with numbers, measure performance as percentiles, then decide how to grow.
- Maintainability: operability, simplicity and evolvability.
