# Read: Chapter 8, The Trouble with Distributed Systems

A pessimistic chapter on purpose: "anything that can go wrong will go wrong". Its job is to make you distrust
networks, clocks and even your own process, so the next chapter's solutions make sense.

## Faults and partial failures

On one computer, things work or crash. In a distributed system, **some parts fail and others don't**, unpredictably:
**partial failure**. Read the comparison of cloud computing with supercomputing, and the idea of building a reliable
system from unreliable parts.

## Unreliable networks

- When you send a request and get no answer (Figure 8-1), you **can't tell** whether the request was lost, the node is
  down or slow, or the response was lost.
- **Network faults in practice**: they're common, even in well-run datacenters (and sharks bite undersea cables).
- **Detecting faults**: the few cases where you get explicit feedback, and why you usually need a **timeout**.
- **Timeouts and unbounded delays**: why there's no correct timeout; **network congestion and queueing** (Figure 8-2);
  why TCP's own retries and variable delays make things worse; choosing timeouts by measurement (the Phi Accrual
  failure detector).
- **Synchronous versus asynchronous networks**: why datacenter networks don't give the guarantees telephone circuits
  do (bursty traffic and cost).

## Unreliable clocks

- **Monotonic** clocks (for measuring durations) versus **time-of-day** clocks (which can jump back).
- **Clock synchronization and accuracy**: NTP's limits, drift, leap seconds, virtual machines.
- **Relying on synchronized clocks**: the **last write wins** example (Figure 8-3: a later write gets an earlier
  timestamp and is lost); **clock readings have a confidence interval**; Google Spanner's TrueTime.
- **Process pauses**: garbage collection, virtual machine suspension, paging, a laptop lid closing. The **lease**
  example: a process checks its lease, pauses, and acts after the lease expired.

## Knowledge, truth, and lies

- **The truth is defined by the majority**: a node can't trust its own judgment; the incorrect distributed lock of
  Figure 8-4, and **fencing tokens** (Figure 8-5).
- **Byzantine faults** (nodes that lie) and when you need to care.
- **System models**: synchronous, partially synchronous, asynchronous; crash-stop, crash-recovery, Byzantine; and
  **safety versus liveness** properties.

## After reading

Your service calls another service and gets no response after 2 seconds. List every possible explanation, and say
what you can safely do next in each case. (Hint: is the operation safe to retry?)

## What to take away

- No response means "I don't know", never "it didn't happen".
- Time-of-day clocks can't order events across machines reliably.
- A paused process can wake up with stale beliefs: storage must fence it off.
