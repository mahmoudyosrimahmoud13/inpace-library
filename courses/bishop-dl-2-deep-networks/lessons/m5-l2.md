# Watch: building micrograd

Andrej Karpathy builds a tiny reverse-mode automatic differentiation engine, **micrograd**, from an empty file, then
trains a small neural network with it. It's long (about two and a half hours), so watch it in pieces, or just the first
hour, which covers the engine itself.

It's the perfect companion to section 8.2, and to the next lesson:

- His `Value` class holds **one number**; the `Tensor` class you'll write next holds a whole **array**, so one operation
  can be a matrix product over a mini-batch. Same idea, much faster in NumPy.
- His `_backward` closures are the adjoint rules from section 8.2.2.
- Watch for the bug he hits when a value is used twice: it's why adjoints must be **added**, not overwritten.
