# Big project: a digit recognizer on your own library

Today you finish **minigrad** and use it for two real tasks. Nothing in it comes from a deep learning framework: every
gradient is computed by the reverse-mode automatic differentiation you wrote on day 5.

## The plan

1. **Finish the library.** Add the operations a classifier needs (exp, log, sums along an axis, indexing, a maximum),
   a numerically safe log-softmax and cross-entropy (section 6.4), `Linear` and `MLP` layers with He initialization
   (section 7.2.5), and the SGD-with-momentum and Adam optimizers (section 7.3). Then check every new gradient
   numerically.
2. **Train a digit recognizer.** scikit-learn's built-in handwritten digits: 1,797 images of 8 × 8 pixels, 10 classes. A
   two-layer network with mini-batches, Adam and early stopping should reach about 97% on images it has never seen.
   Then look at its mistakes, and compare it with a single-layer model.
3. **Fix the inverse problem.** Train the **mixture density network** from section 6.5 on the problem where squared
   error failed on day 2, and see it find both groups of answers.

## What you need

- Your `minigrad.py` from day 5.
- NumPy and scikit-learn (only for the data set).
