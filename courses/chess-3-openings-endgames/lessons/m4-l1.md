# Converting an advantage and minor-piece endgames

Winning a pawn or a piece is half the job. Turning it into a win, called **converting**, is the other half.

## How to convert

1. **Stay safe first.** Don't let the opponent get counterplay. Check his checks and threats every move.
2. **Trade pieces, not pawns.** With fewer pieces, the extra material counts more, and pawns are what you promote.
3. **Activate your king** in the endgame.
4. **Create a passed pawn** and push it, with your king and pieces supporting it.
5. **Don't hurry.** When the opponent can do nothing, improve your pieces before the final push.

## Minor pieces

- **Bishop vs knight:** bishops prefer open positions and pawns on both wings; knights prefer closed positions and outposts.
- **The wrong bishop:** king, bishop and a rook pawn against a lone king is a **draw** if the bishop can't control the promotion square and the defending king reaches the corner.

## Try it

You are a bishop up with pawns on both sides of the board, and your opponent offers to trade the last pair of rooks. Should you accept?

**Answer:** **yes**. Trading pieces (not pawns) makes the extra bishop count more, and without rooks your opponent has almost no counterplay. Then bring your king forward and create a passed pawn.

## Your practice

Find one of your games where you were ahead in material but didn't win. Replay it with the analysis board and mark the move where you should have traded pieces or activated your king.
