# King and pawn endgames

In the endgame, the **king becomes a fighting piece**. Bring it to the center. And learn these ideas: they decide many rook and minor-piece endgames too, because those often end in a king and pawn endgame after trades.

## The rule of the square

Can the king catch a passed pawn? Draw a **square** from the pawn to its promotion square. If the king can step **inside the square**, it catches the pawn.

```
8  . . . . . . . .
7  . . . . . . . .
6  . . . . . . . .
5  . . . . . k . .
4  P . . . . . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  . . . . . . . .
1  K . . . . . . .
   a b c d e f g h
```

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/8/8/8/5k2/P7/8/8/K7_w_-_-_0_1)

**White to move:** after **a5**, the square is a5–a8–d8–d5, and the black king on f5 can't get in. The pawn queens.

```

7  . . . . . . . .
6  . . . . . . . .
5  . . . . . k . .
4  P . . . . . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  . . . . . . . .

   a b c d e f g h
```

*Black to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/8/8/8/5k2/P7/8/8/K7_b_-_-_0_1)

**Black to move:** **...Ke5** steps into the square of the pawn on a4 (a4–a8–e8–e4) and catches it.

## Opposition

Two kings facing each other on the same file with **one square between** them are in **opposition**. The player who **does not** have to move "has the opposition", because the other king must step aside.

```
8  . . . . . . . .
7  . . . . k . . .
6  . . . . . . . .
5  . . . . K . . .
4  . . . . P . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  . . . . . . . .
1  . . . . . . . .
   a b c d e f g h
```

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/8/4k3/8/4K3/4P3/8/8/8_w_-_-_0_1)

White to move: Black has the opposition. Wherever the white king goes, the black king follows (Kd5 Kd7, Kf5 Kf7). If White pushes the pawn, Black blocks it and the game ends in **stalemate**: a draw.

With **Black to move** in the same position, Black must give way, for example ...Kd7, and **Kf6!** puts the white king on a key square. White wins.

## Key squares

For a pawn not on the edge, the **key squares** are the three squares **two ranks in front of the pawn** (for a pawn on e4: d6, e6 and f6). If your king reaches a key square, the pawn promotes, whatever the opponent does. That's why the attacking king should go **in front of** its pawn, not behind it.

## Rook pawns are different

A pawn on the a- or h-file is often a **draw** even with the king in front: if the defending king reaches the corner, it can't be driven out.

## Try it

White: king e1, pawn h4. Black: king c5, Black to move. Does the black king catch the pawn?

**Answer:** the square of the h4 pawn is h4–h8–d8–d4. **...Kd4** steps inside (d4 is a corner of the square), so **yes**, Black catches it: h5 Ke5 h6 Kf6 h7 Kg7.
