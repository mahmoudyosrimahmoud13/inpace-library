# An opening for White: the Italian Game

You don't need many openings. You need **one for White**, and **one answer each to 1. e4 and 1. d4 for Black**, that you understand well. Start with openings where the ideas are clear and the pieces go to natural squares.

## The Italian Game

**1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 (knight to f3) Nc6 (knight to c6) 3. Bc4 (bishop to c4)**

![Chess board](board-29ecf6d1ed.png)

The bishop aims at **f7**, Black's weakest square. White has developed two pieces and is ready to castle. Black's two main answers:

### 3... Bc5 (the Giuoco Piano, the "quiet game")

**4. c3 Nf6 (knight to f6) 5. d3 d6 6. O-O (castles kingside) O-O**

![Chess board](board-61a49c7d91.png)

*White to move (the last move is shown in yellow).* [Open on Lichess to play it out](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/r1bq1rk1/ppp2ppp/2np1n2/2b1p3/2B1P3/2PP1N2/PP3PPP/RNBQ1RK1_w_-_-_2_7)

White's plan:

- **c3 and d3** now, and later **d4** to take the center when it's well prepared.
- **Re1 (rook to e1)**, **h3** (stops ...Bg4 pinning the knight), **Nbd2–f1–g3** to bring the last knight toward the kingside.
- Sometimes **a4**, so the bishop on c4 can retreat to a2 if Black plays ...a6 and ...b5.

### 3... Nf6 (the Two Knights Defense)

Play the calm **4. d3**, with the same plan: c3, O-O, Re1, h3, Nbd2.

The sharp 4. Ng5 attacks f7 again. After 4... d5 5. exd5, Black must play **5... Na5! (knight to a5)**. The natural 5... Nxd5? allows **6. Nxf7! (knight takes on f7)**, the **Fried Liver Attack**, where the black king gets dragged into the open. Know the trap exists so you can use it, or avoid it as Black.

## When Black doesn't play 1... e5

- **1... c5 (Sicilian Defense):** play **2. c3** (the Alapin), aiming for d4 next, and develop normally.
- **1... e6 (French)** or **1... c6 (Caro-Kann):** play **2. d4**, then develop knights and bishops and castle. Keep it simple.
- Anything else: follow the opening principles: center, develop, castle.

## How to learn an opening

1. Learn the **ideas** (where pieces go, which pawn breaks matter), not long move lists.
2. Play it in **many games**. After each game, check where you left "theory" and what the best move was.
3. Look at a few **master games** in it. On Lichess, the **Opening Explorer** shows what masters play in any position.

## Try it

After 1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 3. Bc4 Nf6 4. Ng5 d5 5. exd5, why is 5... Nxd5 a mistake?

**Answer:** it allows **6. Nxf7!**: the knight forks the queen and rook, and after 6... Kxf7 7. Qf3+ the black king is stuck in the center under attack (the Fried Liver Attack). **5... Na5 (knight to a5)** is the right move.
