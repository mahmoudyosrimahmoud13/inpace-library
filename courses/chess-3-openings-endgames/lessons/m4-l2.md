# How to keep getting better

You now know the rules, the main tactics, an opening repertoire, the basic plans and the key endgames. Here's how to keep improving without a coach.

## A simple weekly plan

| Activity | Time each week | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Puzzles | 2 to 3 hours (20 a day) | Tactics decide most games |
| Rapid games (15+10 or longer) | 3 to 4 games | Practice what you learn |
| Analyzing your games | 1 hour | Find your own patterns of mistakes |
| Endgames | 30 minutes | Lichess Practice, then endgame books |
| Openings | 30 minutes | Only after a game, check where you left theory |

## Analyze your own games

This is the fastest way to improve:

1. **First on your own**, without an engine. Find the moments where you weren't sure what to do, and write what you thought.
2. **Then with the engine.** Look at the 2 or 3 biggest swings in the evaluation. Ask: *why* did I miss it? A tactic I didn't see? A plan I didn't know? Time trouble?
3. Keep a **mistake log**. After 20 games, you'll see your typical errors, and you can work on them.

## Use your time well in games

- Don't play the opening in 30 seconds and then panic in the middlegame. Spend time at **critical moments**: when the position changes (trades, pawn breaks, the opponent's surprising move).
- Always keep a little time for the end.

## Learn from stronger players

- Watch **annotated master games**. Lichess Studies have thousands of free ones.
- Play over classic games of players like Capablanca (endgames and simplicity), Morphy (fast development and attack) and Fischer (clear plans).
- Join a **club** or an online team, and play in **tournaments**, even small online ones.

## Ratings

Your rating will go up and down. Look at the trend over months, not days. Many players improve 200 to 400 points in their first year with steady practice.

## Try it

You lost three games this week, each time after missing a knight fork. What should you change in your training?

**Answer:** do extra **fork puzzles** (the Lichess "Fork" theme), and add "where can enemy knights jump next move?" to your blunder check.

## Your practice

Analyze your last 10 games following the steps above, start a mistake log, and set one specific goal for the next month (for example: "no undefended pieces lost in 10 games in a row").
