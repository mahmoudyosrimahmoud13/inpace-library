# Rook endgames

Rook endgames are the most common endgames in chess: about half of all endgames have rooks in them. Three ideas will save and win you many points.

## Rooks belong behind passed pawns

The most common endgame. One rule beats most others: **put your rook behind passed pawns**, your own (to push them) or the opponent's (to stop them). A rook behind a pawn gets more active as the pawn advances; a rook in front of it gets more passive.

## Lucena: building a bridge

The winning method with rook and pawn against rook, when your pawn is on the 7th rank and your king is in front of it.

```
8  . K . k . . . .
7  . P . . . . . .
6  . . . . . . . .
5  . . . . . . . .
4  . . . . . . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  r . . . . . . .
1  . . R . . . . .
   a b c d e f g h
```

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/1K1k4/1P6/8/8/8/8/r7/2R5_w_-_-_0_1)

**1. Rd1+** pushes the black king away. **2. Rd4!** prepares the "bridge". Then the white king walks out, and when the checks come, **Rb4** blocks them. The pawn queens.

## Philidor: the drawing method

When you defend with rook against rook and pawn, and your king is in front of the pawn, keep your rook on your **third rank** (the 6th rank for Black) so the enemy king can't come forward.

```
8  . . . . k . . .
7  R . . . . . . .
6  . r . . . . . .
5  . . . . P K . .
4  . . . . . . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  . . . . . . . .
1  . . . . . . . .
   a b c d e f g h
```

*Black to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/4k3/R7/1r6/4PK2/8/8/8/8_b_-_-_0_1)

Black keeps the rook on the 6th rank. As soon as White pushes **e6** (so the white king has no shelter in front of the pawn), Black's rook goes to the **back** (...Rb1) and gives checks from behind. White can't escape the checks: a draw.

## Activity is everything

In rook endgames an **active rook** (attacking pawns, cutting off the king, on the 7th rank) is often worth a pawn. When in doubt, choose the active move, even at the cost of a pawn.

## Try it

You have a rook and a passed pawn on the a-file; your opponent has a rook. Where should your rook go?

**Answer:** **behind the pawn**, on the a-file below it, so it supports the pawn all the way to a8.
