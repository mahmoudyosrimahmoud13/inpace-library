# Middlegame plans

After the opening, many players don't know what to do. These questions give you a plan almost every time.

## The four questions

1. **Is there a tactic?** (Checks, captures, threats, for both sides.) Always first.
2. **What is my worst piece?** Improve it. A bishop blocked by its own pawns, a knight on the rim, a rook that hasn't moved: bring it into play.
3. **Where should I play?** Usually on the side where your **pawns point** and where you have **more space**. If your pawn chain points to the kingside (e.g., pawns on d4–e5), attack on the kingside.
4. **What does my opponent want?** Stop his plan if it is faster or more dangerous than yours. This is called **prophylaxis**.

## Attacking the king

Attack the king when:

- you have **more pieces near it** than the opponent has defenders;
- there are **weaknesses** in front of it (a pawn moved, like ...g6 or ...h6, gives hooks and holes);
- the kings have **castled on opposite sides**: then both sides throw their pawns at the enemy king, and **speed** is everything.

Don't attack with one or two pieces. Bring the others first.

## When to trade

- **Ahead in material:** trade pieces (not pawns). The endgame will be easy.
- **Your opponent's strong piece:** trade it (a knight on an outpost, an active bishop).
- **Behind or under pressure:** avoid trades that don't help you, and look for activity.
- **Cramped:** trades free your position.

## Space

When you control more space, avoid trades: the opponent's pieces get in each other's way. When you have less space, trade to breathe.

## An example plan, in words

In the Italian Game with **c3, d3, O-O, Re1, h3**, a typical plan is: bring the queen's knight to **g3** via d2 and f1, then prepare **d4** with **Bg5 (bishop to g5)** or **Be3 (bishop to e3)** first. If Black plays ...a6 and ...b5, retreat the bishop to **b3** or **a2** and keep it pointing at f7. If Black castles kingside and allows **Nf5 (knight to f5)**, the knight on f5 is very strong.

## Try it

You are a knight up, it's move 30, and there are still queens, two rooks each and some minor pieces on the board. What should your plan be?

**Answer:** **trade pieces**, especially queens, while keeping your pawns. Each trade makes your extra knight count for more, and reduces your opponent's chances to attack.

## Your practice

In your next five games, at move 15 stop and answer the four questions in writing (or in your head). After the game, check whether your plan was a good one with the analysis board.
