# Read: the dual SVM, kernels and numerical solutions (12.3–12.6)

**The dual (12.3)**: use Lagrange multipliers αₙ ≥ 0 (course 3) for the margin constraints. Then:

- **w = Σₙ αₙyₙxₙ**: the solution is a combination of training points.
- Only points with **αₙ > 0** count: the **support vectors**, on or inside the margin.
- The dual problem depends on the data **only through inner products** ⟨xᵢ, xⱼ⟩.
- The **convex hull view** (12.3.2): the hard-margin SVM finds the closest points between the convex hulls of the two
  classes.

**Kernels (12.4)**: replace ⟨xᵢ, xⱼ⟩ by k(xᵢ, xⱼ) = ⟨φ(xᵢ), φ(xⱼ)⟩. You get a linear classifier in a (possibly
infinite-dimensional) feature space without ever computing φ: **the kernel trick**. Common kernels: polynomial
(⟨x, y⟩ + c)ᵈ and RBF exp(−‖x − y‖²/(2σ²)). A kernel must give a symmetric positive semidefinite **Gram matrix**.

**Numerical solution (12.5)**: the primal and dual are both quadratic programs; the book shows how to write them in
standard QP form. Libraries (libsvm, scikit-learn) use specialised methods.

12.6 points to Gaussian processes and other kernel methods.
