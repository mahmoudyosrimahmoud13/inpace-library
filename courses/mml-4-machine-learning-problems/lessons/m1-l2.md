# Read: data, models and learning (8.1)

Part II opens with a short chapter about how the math meets practice. Read it as a framework for the next four.

- **Data as vectors**: every example becomes a vector xₙ (features), often with a label yₙ. Choices here (scaling,
  encoding categories, building features) matter as much as the model.
- **Models as functions** (a predictor f(x, θ)) or **as probability distributions** (p(y | x, θ)). The book uses both
  views and shows when they agree.
- **Learning is finding parameters** that work on **unseen** data. Training, validation and test sets keep you honest.
- The three algorithmic ingredients you'll meet again and again: **prediction/inference**, **training/parameter
  estimation**, and **hyperparameter tuning/model selection**.
