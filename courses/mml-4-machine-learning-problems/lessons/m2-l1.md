# Read: parameter estimation (8.3)

The "probabilistic" view of learning.

- **Maximum likelihood (MLE, 8.3.1)**: choose θ to make the observed data most probable. In practice, minimize the
  **negative log-likelihood** −Σ log p(yₙ | xₙ, θ) (logs turn products into sums and avoid tiny numbers).
- With **Gaussian noise**, the negative log-likelihood is the **squared loss** (up to constants). So least squares is
  maximum likelihood. Different noise models give different losses.
- **MAP (8.3.2)**: put a prior p(θ) on the parameters and maximize the posterior. That adds −log p(θ) to the loss: a
  **Gaussian prior gives an L2 penalty** (ridge regression), a Laplace prior gives L1 (lasso).
- **Model fitting (8.3.3)**: the book's figure shows overfitting, underfitting and a good fit. MLE can overfit when
  there's little data; MAP helps.
