# Read: Bayesian linear regression (9.3)

Instead of one θ, keep the whole posterior and average predictions over it.

![Bayesian linear regression: plausible functions and a predictive band that widens away from the data](bayes_regression.png)

- **Prior** θ ~ N(m₀, S₀) and Gaussian likelihood → **Gaussian posterior** (conjugacy):
  S_N = (S₀⁻¹ + σ⁻²ΦᵀΦ)⁻¹ and m_N = S_N(S₀⁻¹m₀ + σ⁻²Φᵀy). Precisions add.
- **Posterior predictive** at x*: N(φ*ᵀm_N, φ*ᵀS_Nφ* + σ²). The first part of the variance is our uncertainty about θ
  (it grows away from the data), the second is the noise (it never goes away).
- **Marginal likelihood (9.3.5)**: p(y | X) is Gaussian too, so models (say, polynomial degrees) can be compared by
  their evidence, as on day 3.
- The figures with samples of functions drawn from the prior and the posterior are the best way to understand it.
