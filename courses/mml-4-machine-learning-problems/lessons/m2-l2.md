# Read: probabilistic modeling and inference (8.4)

- A **probabilistic model** is the joint distribution of everything: data, parameters and any hidden (latent)
  variables.
- **Bayesian inference (8.4.2)** keeps the **whole posterior** p(θ | data) instead of one best θ. Predictions average
  over all plausible parameters: p(y* | x*, data) = ∫ p(y* | x*, θ) p(θ | data) dθ. That integral is often hard; that's
  why conjugate priors (course 3) and approximations matter.
- **Latent variables (8.4.3)**: unobserved quantities that make modeling easier (which cluster a point came from, the
  low-dimensional code in PCA). Chapters 10 and 11 use them.
