# Section F: the IMA Statement of Ethical Professional Practice

Section F is 15% of the exam, and CBQs often include an ethics question. Learn the IMA Statement's structure well enough to name the principle and standard involved in any scenario, and the steps to resolve a conflict.

## Four overarching principles

**Honesty, Fairness, Objectivity, Responsibility.**

## Four standards (what members must do)

**1. Competence**
- Maintain professional knowledge and skills
- Follow laws, regulations and technical standards
- Provide accurate, clear, concise and timely decision support
- Recognize and help manage risk

**2. Confidentiality**
- Keep information confidential unless disclosure is authorized or legally required
- Inform others (e.g., subordinates) of their confidentiality obligations
- Never use confidential information for unethical or illegal advantage (insider trading)

**3. Integrity**
- Avoid actual conflicts of interest; disclose potential ones
- Refrain from conduct that would prejudice carrying out duties ethically
- Abstain from activities that might discredit the profession
- Contribute to a positive ethical culture

**4. Credibility**
- Communicate information fairly and objectively
- Provide all relevant information that could influence users' understanding
- Report delays or deficiencies in information, timeliness, processing or internal controls

## Resolving an ethical conflict

The Statement suggests:

1. **Follow the organization's established policies** on resolving such conflicts.
2. If policies don't resolve it, **discuss with your immediate supervisor**, unless the supervisor appears to be involved; then go to the **next level** of management. Escalation can continue up to the audit committee or the board.
3. **Clarify the issues** confidentially with an IMA ethics counselor or another impartial adviser.
4. **Consult your own attorney** about your legal obligations and rights.
5. If the conflict still isn't resolved, consider **resigning**.

Communicating the problem to outside authorities or individuals is generally **not appropriate** unless required by law or regulation (some laws require or protect reporting, e.g., whistleblower protections).

## Applying it: exam-style cases

**Case 1.** A controller is told by the CFO to delay recording a large expense until next quarter so the company meets analysts' expectations.
- Standards: **Credibility** (communicate fairly; disclose all relevant information), **Integrity** (refrain from conduct that prejudices ethical duties), **Competence** (follow accounting standards).
- Steps: follow company policy; since the CFO is involved, go above the CFO (CEO, audit committee); clarify with an ethics counselor; consult an attorney; consider resigning.

**Case 2.** A cost accountant learns that the company will announce a large contract next week and considers buying shares.
- Standard: **Confidentiality** (never use confidential information for personal advantage) and **Integrity**. Also illegal insider trading.

**Case 3.** A budget analyst's spouse owns the supplier being evaluated.
- Standard: **Integrity**: disclose the potential conflict and step away from the evaluation.

**Case 4.** An analyst realizes the cost model she sent management last month has an error.
- Standards: **Competence** (accurate decision support) and **Credibility** (report deficiencies): correct and inform promptly.

## Try it

A senior manager asks you to classify a personal trip as a business expense "just this once". Which standards apply, and what are your first two steps?

**Answer:** **Integrity** (avoid conduct that prejudices ethical duties) and **Credibility** (fair, objective information). First, follow the company's policy on such issues; second, raise it with your supervisor, or the next level if the supervisor is the one asking.
