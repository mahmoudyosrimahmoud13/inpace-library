# Section B: working capital management

Working capital management is about keeping just enough cash, receivables and inventory: too little stops the business, too much ties up money that costs the WACC.

## Cash

Reasons to hold cash: **transactions**, **precaution**, **speculation**, and compensating balances banks require.

Speeding up collections and slowing payments (legally) frees cash:
- **Lockbox systems** and electronic payments shorten collection float.
- **Zero-balance accounts** keep idle cash in one interest-earning place.
- Pay on the due date, not before (unless taking a discount).

**Value of shortening float:** cutting collection time by 2 days on daily collections of 500,000 frees **1,000,000**. At 6% interest, that's worth 60,000 a year: the most a lockbox service should cost.

## Marketable securities

Surplus cash goes into safe, liquid, short-term instruments: treasury bills, commercial paper, certificates of deposit, money market funds. Priorities: **safety, liquidity, then yield**.

## Receivables: credit policy

Looser credit raises sales but also bad debts, collection costs and the money tied up in receivables. Evaluate a change with incremental analysis:

| | |
|---|---|
| Extra contribution margin from new sales | + |
| Extra bad debts | − |
| Cost of extra investment in receivables (avg receivables × variable cost % × required return) | − |
| Change in discount costs, collection costs | ± |

Example: new policy adds sales of 600,000 with a 30% CM ratio (CM 180,000); bad debts 3% of new sales (18,000); DSO for new sales 60 days → average receivables 600,000 × 60/360 = 100,000; investment at variable cost 70% = 70,000; at 10% required return = 7,000. Net benefit = 180,000 − 18,000 − 7,000 = **155,000**: adopt.

## Inventory: EOQ

> **EOQ = √(2 × Annual demand × Cost per order ÷ Carrying cost per unit per year)**

Demand 36,000 units; ordering cost 200 per order; carrying cost 4 per unit per year. EOQ = √(2 × 36,000 × 200 ÷ 4) = √3,600,000 = **1,897 units**.

At the EOQ, total ordering cost = total carrying cost.

**Reorder point** = Daily usage × Lead time (+ safety stock). 100 units a day, 5-day lead time, safety stock 150: reorder at **650 units**.

Safety stock trades carrying cost against stockout cost. **JIT** cuts inventory to a minimum but needs reliable suppliers.

## The cost of trade credit

Terms **2/10, net 30**: 2% off if paid in 10 days, otherwise pay in full by day 30. Not taking the discount means borrowing 98 for 20 days at a cost of 2.

> **Annual cost = [Discount % ÷ (100% − Discount %)] × [365 ÷ (Days paid − Discount days)]**

= (2 ÷ 98) × (365 ÷ 20) = **37.2%** (effective annual rate, compounded, is even higher: about 44.6%).

If the bank lends at 10%, borrow and take the discount.

## Try it

Terms 1/15, net 45. (a) Nominal annual cost of skipping the discount (365 days)? (b) Demand 10,000 units, order cost 50, carrying cost 2.50 per unit: EOQ?

**Answers:** (a) (1/99) × (365/30) = **12.3%**. (b) √(2 × 10,000 × 50 ÷ 2.5) = √400,000 = **632 units**.
