# Section C: marginal analysis and short-term decisions

The rule for every short-term decision: **compare only relevant (incremental) revenues and costs**: future amounts that differ between the options. Sunk costs and unavoidable fixed costs are irrelevant. Opportunity costs are relevant.

## Special order

Capacity 10,000 units; current sales 8,000 at 50. Variable cost 30; fixed costs 100,000. Offer: 1,500 units at 36, special packaging 1 per unit.

- Incremental revenue 1,500 × 36 = 54,000
- Incremental cost 1,500 × (30 + 1) = 46,500
- **Gain 7,500** → accept (spare capacity exists; no effect on regular prices)

If the order were **3,000 units**, it would exceed capacity by 1,000: regular sales lost = 1,000 × (50 − 30) = 20,000 opportunity cost. Gain = 3,000 × (36 − 31) − 20,000 = **−5,000** → reject (or accept only 2,000).

## Make or buy

Making a part: direct materials 6, direct labor 4, variable overhead 2, fixed overhead allocated 5 (of which 2 is avoidable). Supplier price 13.

| Per unit | Make | Buy |
|---|---|---|
| Variable costs | 12 | |
| Avoidable fixed | 2 | |
| Purchase price | | 13 |
| **Relevant cost** | **14** | **13** |

Buy, unless the freed capacity has no other use and quality or supply risk argue otherwise. If the freed space could be rented for income, that rent is an **opportunity cost of making**.

## Sell or process further

Joint costs (before the split-off point) are **sunk** for this decision. Process further only if **incremental revenue > incremental processing cost**.

Product J sells for 20 at split-off; processed further it sells for 28 with extra cost 6 per unit. Gain 2 per unit → process further.

## Keep or drop a segment

Drop only if the **avoidable fixed costs** saved exceed the **contribution margin lost** (and consider effects on other products' sales). Allocated common costs don't disappear.

## Constrained resources

With one binding constraint, rank products by **CM per unit of the constraint**.

| | P | Q |
|---|---|---|
| CM per unit | 24 | 30 |
| Machine hours per unit | 2 | 3 |
| CM per machine hour | **12** | **10** |

Make P first up to its demand, then Q. With several constraints, the exam may describe **linear programming** (maximize CM subject to constraints; solution at a corner point) or **theory of constraints** (throughput = sales − direct materials; manage the bottleneck).

## Qualitative factors (CBQ favorites)

Quality, supplier reliability, employee morale, customer reactions to price discrimination, legal limits (selling below cost, price discrimination laws), strategic fit, the long-term effect of outsourcing on skills.

## Try it

A company makes 20,000 units of a part: variable cost 9; allocated fixed 6 (avoidable 1.50). A supplier offers 10.25. The freed space could be rented for 12,000 a year. Make or buy?

**Answer:** make = 9 + 1.50 = 10.50 per unit plus 12,000 lost rent = 210,000 + 12,000 = 222,000. Buy = 205,000. **Buy** (saves 17,000).
