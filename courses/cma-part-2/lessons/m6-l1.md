# Section C: CVP analysis at exam depth

Section C is the biggest (25%). You met CVP in Part 1 and in *Cost and management accounting*; the exam adds multi-product, tax and sensitivity twists.

## The core

> **Break-even units = Fixed costs ÷ Contribution margin per unit**
> **Break-even sales = Fixed costs ÷ CM ratio**
> **Units for target profit = (Fixed costs + Target pre-tax profit) ÷ CM per unit**
> **Target pre-tax profit = Target after-tax profit ÷ (1 − tax rate)**

## Multi-product break-even

Two products: A (price 50, VC 30, CM 20) and B (price 80, VC 50, CM 30). Sales mix in **units**: 3 A for every 2 B. Fixed costs 120,000.

- CM of a "bundle" (3A + 2B) = 3 × 20 + 2 × 30 = **120**
- Break-even bundles = 120,000 ÷ 120 = **1,000** → **3,000 A and 2,000 B**

Using a **sales-dollar mix**, use the weighted average CM ratio instead:
Sales of a bundle = 3 × 50 + 2 × 80 = 310; weighted CM ratio = 120 ÷ 310 = 38.7%; break-even sales = 120,000 ÷ 0.387 = **310,000**.

## Sensitivity: what moves the result

| Change | CM per unit | Break-even |
|---|---|---|
| Price +10% | Up | Down |
| Variable cost +10% | Down | Up |
| Fixed costs +10% | Same | Up 10% |
| Mix shifts to lower-CM product | Weighted CM down | Up |

## Margin of safety and leverage

- **Margin of safety** = Actual sales − Break-even sales (or ÷ actual sales for a %)
- **Degree of operating leverage** = CM ÷ Operating income = 1 ÷ margin of safety %
- A company with high fixed costs and low variable costs has a **high DOL**: profit swings more with sales.

## Indifference point between two cost structures

Machine X: fixed 50,000, VC 12 per unit. Machine Y: fixed 80,000, VC 9. Price 20.

Indifference: 50,000 + 12Q = 80,000 + 9Q → **Q = 10,000 units**. Above 10,000, Y (lower variable cost) is more profitable.

## CVP with tax, an exam-style example

Price 40, VC 24, fixed costs 160,000, tax 25%, target **after-tax** profit 60,000.
- Pre-tax target = 60,000 ÷ 0.75 = 80,000
- Units = (160,000 + 80,000) ÷ 16 = **15,000**

## Assumptions (asked as theory)

Linear revenue and costs within the relevant range; fixed costs fixed; constant sales mix; units produced = units sold; all costs split into fixed and variable.

## Try it

Fixed costs 90,000; products C (CM 15) and D (CM 25) sold in a 2:1 unit mix. (a) Break-even units of each? (b) If the mix became 1:1, would break-even total units rise or fall?

**Answers:** (a) bundle CM = 2 × 15 + 25 = 55; 90,000 ÷ 55 = 1,636.4 bundles → about **3,273 C and 1,637 D** (round up). (b) bundle CM per unit rises (average 20 vs 18.33), so total break-even units **fall**.
