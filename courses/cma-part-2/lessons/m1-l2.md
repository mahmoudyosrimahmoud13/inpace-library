# Time value of money: the tool behind Part 2

Bonds, stocks, the cost of capital, NPV and IRR all rest on one idea: **money today is worth more than money later**, because it can earn a return.

## Future value and present value

> **FV = PV × (1 + r)ⁿ**
> **PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)ⁿ**

- 10,000 invested at 8% for 3 years: FV = 10,000 × 1.08³ = **12,597**
- 12,597 received in 3 years, at 8%: PV = 12,597 ÷ 1.08³ = **10,000**

The factor 1 ÷ (1 + r)ⁿ is the **present value factor**. At 8%: year 1 = 0.926, year 2 = 0.857, year 3 = 0.794.

## Annuities: equal payments

An **ordinary annuity** pays at the **end** of each period.

> **PV of an annuity = Payment × [1 − (1 + r)⁻ⁿ] ÷ r**

5,000 a year for 4 years at 10%: factor = (1 − 1.1⁻⁴) ÷ 0.1 = **3.170**. PV = 5,000 × 3.170 = **15,850**.

An **annuity due** pays at the **start** of each period: multiply the ordinary annuity value by (1 + r). Rent paid in advance is an annuity due.

A **perpetuity** pays forever: PV = Payment ÷ r. A preferred share paying 6 a year, at 8%: 6 ÷ 0.08 = **75**.

A **growing perpetuity**: PV = Next payment ÷ (r − g). This is the Gordon growth model for stocks (Module 4).

On the exam, PV tables may be given, and you're allowed a basic financial calculator (check the IMA's list). Practice both ways.

## Compounding more often

12% a year compounded monthly: the effective annual rate = (1 + 0.12/12)¹² − 1 = **12.68%**.

> **Effective annual rate = (1 + nominal ÷ m)ᵐ − 1**

Exam questions on the cost of trade credit and loan offers use this.

## Exam traps

- Match the **rate and the period**: monthly payments need a monthly rate.
- Watch **beginning vs end** of period (annuity due vs ordinary).
- In NPV, Excel's `NPV` function assumes the first cash flow is at the **end of year 1**: add the year-0 outflow separately.
- "Discount rate", "required return", "hurdle rate", "cost of capital": in capital budgeting questions they all mean the rate you discount at.

## Try it

1. PV of 20,000 received in 5 years at 6%?
2. A loan of 50,000 at 9% repaid in 5 equal yearly payments: the payment?
3. A share pays a dividend of 2.10 next year, growing 5% a year forever; required return 12%. Its value?

**Answers:** 1. 20,000 ÷ 1.06⁵ = **14,945**. 2. 50,000 ÷ 3.890 = **12,855**. 3. 2.10 ÷ (0.12 − 0.05) = **30**.
