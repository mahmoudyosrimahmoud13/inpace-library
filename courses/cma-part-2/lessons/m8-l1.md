# Section D: enterprise risk management

**Risk** is the chance that events will affect objectives, for better or worse. **Enterprise risk management (ERM)** manages all risks together, at the level of the whole organization, linked to strategy.

## Types of risk

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Strategic** | Wrong strategy, new competitors, technology shifts, reputation |
| **Operational** | Process failures, fraud, cyber attacks, supply chain breaks, key people leaving, natural disasters |
| **Financial** | Market (interest rates, exchange rates, commodity prices), credit, liquidity |
| **Hazard** | Fire, injury, liability (often insurable) |
| **Compliance** | Laws, regulations, sanctions |

## COSO ERM framework (2017)

Five components, 20 principles. The five components:

1. **Governance and culture:** board oversight, values, the people who manage risk.
2. **Strategy and objective-setting:** risk appetite set alongside strategy.
3. **Performance:** identify, assess and prioritize risks; choose responses.
4. **Review and revision:** check how well ERM works as things change.
5. **Information, communication and reporting.**

Key terms:
- **Risk appetite:** how much risk the organization is willing to accept overall to pursue value.
- **Risk tolerance:** acceptable variation around a specific objective.
- **Inherent risk:** before any response. **Residual risk:** what remains after responses.

## Assessing risks

Rate each risk on **likelihood** and **impact**, often on a 1–5 scale, and plot a **heat map**. Focus on high-likelihood, high-impact risks first.

Quantitative tools:
- **Expected loss** = probability × impact
- **Value at risk (VaR):** the maximum loss over a period at a given confidence level (e.g., "95% one-day VaR of 2 million": on 95% of days, losses won't exceed 2 million)
- **Cash flow at risk**, **earnings at risk**, **stress testing**, **scenario analysis**

## Responding to risk

| Response | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Avoid** | Stop the activity | Exit a politically unstable market |
| **Reduce (mitigate)** | Lower likelihood or impact | Controls, backups, diversification of suppliers |
| **Share (transfer)** | Shift to others | Insurance, hedging, outsourcing |
| **Accept** | Live with it | Small risks within appetite |

Choose by comparing the cost of the response with the reduction in expected loss.

## Business continuity

A **business continuity plan** keeps critical operations running after a disaster; a **disaster recovery plan** restores IT systems. Test them regularly.

## Try it

A supplier failure has a 10% annual probability and would cost 2,000,000. A second supplier would cut the probability to 2% at an extra cost of 120,000 a year. Is it worth it?

**Answer:** expected loss now 200,000; with the second supplier 40,000 + 120,000 = 160,000. **Yes**, it reduces expected cost by 40,000 (and also cuts the chance of a disastrous year).
