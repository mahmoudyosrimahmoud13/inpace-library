# Section B: international finance

## Exchange rates

- **Direct quote:** home currency per unit of foreign currency (EGP 50 per USD, from an Egyptian view).
- **Indirect quote:** foreign currency per unit of home currency (USD 0.02 per EGP).
- If the USD/EGP rate goes from 48 to 50, the dollar has **appreciated** and the pound **depreciated**.

**Cross rate:** if 1 USD = 0.92 EUR and 1 USD = 3.75 SAR, then 1 EUR = 3.75 ÷ 0.92 = **4.08 SAR**.

## What moves exchange rates

- **Relative inflation (purchasing power parity):** the currency with higher inflation tends to weaken.
- **Relative interest rates (interest rate parity):** higher interest rates attract capital, but the forward rate adjusts so you can't earn a riskless profit.
- **Trade balances, capital flows, political stability, central bank intervention.**

**Interest rate parity:** Forward rate = Spot × (1 + home rate) ÷ (1 + foreign rate). If the currency with the higher interest rate trades at a **forward discount**, parity holds.

## Three kinds of exchange rate risk

| Risk | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Transaction** | A specific foreign-currency receivable or payable changes value before settlement | An invoice of EUR 100,000 due in 90 days |
| **Translation (accounting)** | Consolidated statements change when foreign subsidiaries are translated | A German subsidiary's assets shrink in USD terms |
| **Economic (operating)** | Long-term competitiveness changes | A strong home currency makes exports less competitive |

## Hedging transaction risk

A US company will receive **EUR 100,000** in 90 days. Spot 1.10 USD/EUR; 90-day forward 1.08.

- **Forward contract:** lock in 100,000 × 1.08 = **108,000 USD**, whatever happens. No upfront cost, no upside.
- **Money market hedge:** borrow the PV of EUR 100,000 now, convert to USD at spot, invest; repay the EUR loan with the receivable.
- **Currency option (put on EUR):** pay a premium for the right to sell EUR at a set rate; protects against a fall while keeping the upside if EUR rises.
- **Futures:** like forwards but standardized and traded on exchanges, settled daily.
- **Natural hedge:** match foreign revenues with foreign costs (borrow in EUR, pay suppliers in EUR).

## International trade finance

- **Letter of credit:** a bank guarantees payment to the exporter if documents are presented: reduces credit risk across borders.
- **Bill of lading**, **drafts (sight vs time)**, **countertrade** (barter), **export credit agencies**.

## Transfer pricing across borders

Multinationals set prices between their own subsidiaries in different countries. Tax authorities require **arm's-length** prices; aggressive transfer pricing to shift profit to low-tax countries risks penalties.

## Try it

An Egyptian importer must pay **USD 50,000** in 60 days. Spot EGP 48.50; 60-day forward EGP 49.20. (a) Cost in EGP with a forward hedge? (b) If it doesn't hedge and the spot rate in 60 days is 50.00, how much more does it pay compared with the hedge?

**Answers:** (a) 50,000 × 49.20 = **EGP 2,460,000**. (b) 50,000 × 50.00 = 2,500,000: **EGP 40,000 more**.
