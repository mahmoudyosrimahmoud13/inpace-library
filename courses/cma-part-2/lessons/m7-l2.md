# Section C: decisions under uncertainty

## Expected value

> **Expected value = Σ (Outcome × Probability)**

A new product: profit 300,000 (probability 0.3), 100,000 (0.5), −150,000 (0.2). EV = 90,000 + 50,000 − 30,000 = **110,000**.

EV is the long-run average; it ignores risk. A risk-averse manager might reject a project with a positive EV but a chance of a catastrophic loss.

## Payoff tables

Rows = your actions, columns = states of nature with probabilities.

| Order | Demand 100 (0.4) | Demand 200 (0.6) | EV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy 100 | 1,000 | 1,000 | **1,000** |
| Buy 200 | 200 | 2,000 | **1,280** |

Choose **Buy 200** (highest EV).

## Expected value of perfect information (EVPI)

The most you should pay for a perfect forecast:

> **EVPI = EV with perfect information − EV without it**

With perfect information you'd buy 100 when demand is 100 (1,000) and 200 when it's 200 (2,000): EV = 0.4 × 1,000 + 0.6 × 2,000 = **1,600**. EVPI = 1,600 − 1,280 = **320**.

## Decision trees

Draw decisions (squares) and chance events (circles) left to right; then **roll back** from the right, computing EV at chance nodes and choosing the best branch at decision nodes. Good for sequential decisions (test-market first, then launch or not).

## Other approaches

- **Sensitivity analysis:** how much can an input change before the decision flips?
- **Scenario analysis:** best, base and worst case.
- **Monte Carlo simulation:** thousands of random draws of uncertain inputs, giving a distribution of outcomes.
- **Without probabilities:** maximax (optimist), maximin (pessimist), minimax regret.

## Probability basics you may need

- Standard deviation measures spread around the EV; coefficient of variation = σ ÷ EV for comparing projects of different size.
- Joint probability of independent events = product of probabilities.

## Try it

A company can launch now (EV 400,000) or run a test market costing 50,000. With the test, it expects EV 480,000 (before the test cost). Should it test?

**Answer:** net EV with test = 480,000 − 50,000 = 430,000 > 400,000. **Test** (the information is worth 80,000, more than it costs).
