# Section B: cost of capital and capital structure

## Cost of each source

**Debt (after tax):**
> **kd after tax = Pre-tax yield × (1 − tax rate)**

Interest is tax-deductible. A bond yielding 8% with a 25% tax rate costs **6%** after tax. Use the **market yield** (yield to maturity), not the coupon.

**Preferred stock:**
> **kp = Preferred dividend ÷ Net issue price**

Dividend 6, issue price 80 less 2 of flotation costs: 6 ÷ 78 = **7.7%**. No tax adjustment (dividends aren't deductible).

**Common equity (retained earnings):** use **CAPM** or the **dividend growth model**: ke = D₁ ÷ P₀ + g. For **new** common stock, use the net price after flotation costs: it costs more than retained earnings.

## WACC

> **WACC = wd × kd(1 − t) + wp × kp + we × ke**

Use **market-value** weights where possible, and the **target** capital structure.

Example: target 40% debt, 10% preferred, 50% equity; pre-tax debt 8%, tax 25%; preferred 7.7%; equity 12%.

WACC = 0.4 × 6% + 0.1 × 7.7% + 0.5 × 12% = 2.4% + 0.77% + 6% = **9.17%**

WACC is the hurdle rate for projects of **average risk**. Riskier projects need a higher rate.

## Marginal cost of capital

As a company raises more money, costs rise (retained earnings run out, new stock costs more, lenders charge more). The **marginal cost of capital** schedule steps up at **break points**:

> **Break point = Amount of the cheaper source available ÷ Its weight**

Retained earnings 1,000,000 and equity weight 50%: break point = **2,000,000** of total new capital. Beyond that, new stock replaces retained earnings and WACC rises.

## Capital structure

How much debt is right?

- Debt is **cheaper** (lower required return, plus the tax shield).
- But more debt raises **financial risk**: equity holders demand more, and bankruptcy costs grow.
- **Trade-off theory:** WACC falls as debt is added, then rises when financial distress costs outweigh the tax benefit. The optimum minimizes WACC (and maximizes firm value).
- **Pecking order:** managers prefer internal funds first, then debt, then new equity (issuing stock signals that management thinks shares are overvalued).
- **Modigliani–Miller:** without taxes, structure doesn't matter; with taxes, debt adds value through the tax shield (in theory, up to bankrupt-proof limits).

## EPS-EBIT analysis

Compare financing plans by EPS at different EBIT levels. The **indifference point** is where both plans give the same EPS:

Plan A: all equity, 100,000 shares. Plan B: 50,000 shares + 1,000,000 debt at 10% (interest 100,000). Tax 25%.

(EBIT × 0.75) ÷ 100,000 = ((EBIT − 100,000) × 0.75) ÷ 50,000 → EBIT = **200,000**.

Above 200,000 of EBIT, debt gives higher EPS; below it, equity is better.

## Try it

Debt 30% at 9% pre-tax, tax 20%; equity 70% at 13%. (a) WACC? (b) A project of average risk returns 11%: accept?

**Answers:** (a) 0.3 × 7.2% + 0.7 × 13% = 2.16% + 9.1% = **11.26%**. (b) 11% < 11.26%: **reject**.
