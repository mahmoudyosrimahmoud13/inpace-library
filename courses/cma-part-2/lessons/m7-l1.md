# Section C: pricing

## Market structures

| Structure | Pricing power |
|---|---|
| Perfect competition | None: price taker |
| Monopolistic competition | Some, through differentiation |
| Oligopoly | Interdependent: watch competitors' reactions |
| Monopoly | High, limited by demand and regulation |

## Price elasticity of demand

> **Elasticity = % change in quantity ÷ % change in price** (use absolute value)

Price rises from 20 to 22 (+10%), quantity falls from 1,000 to 850 (−15%): elasticity = 1.5 → **elastic** (> 1): total revenue **falls** when price rises.

| Elasticity | Demand | Price up → total revenue |
|---|---|---|
| > 1 | Elastic | Falls |
| = 1 | Unit elastic | Same |
| < 1 | Inelastic | Rises |

The exam may use the **arc (midpoint) method**: % change = change ÷ average of the two values.

## Cost-based pricing

> **Price = Cost base × (1 + Markup %)**

- Variable cost base 40, markup 75% → **70**
- Full (absorption) cost base 56, markup 25% → **70**

The markup must cover all costs not in the base, plus profit. Simple and defensible, but ignores customers and competitors.

## Target costing (market-based)

> **Target cost = Target price − Target profit**

The market will pay 120; the company wants 25% of price as profit (30). Target cost = **90**. If current cost is 100, engineers must cut 10 through value engineering before launch. Common in competitive markets (cars, electronics).

## Other pricing ideas the exam names

- **Peak-load pricing:** higher prices when demand peaks (electricity, hotels).
- **Price skimming:** start high for early adopters, then lower. **Penetration pricing:** start low to win market share.
- **Life-cycle costing:** include research, design, marketing, service and disposal costs over the whole life, not just production.
- **Price discrimination** (different prices to different customers): legal limits apply (in the US, the Robinson-Patman Act).
- **Predatory pricing** (below cost to drive out competitors) and **collusion** (price fixing) are illegal.

## Profit-maximizing price (economics)

A firm maximizes profit where **marginal revenue = marginal cost**. In exam tables, find the quantity where the extra revenue from one more unit (or batch) just equals its extra cost.

| Price | Units | Total revenue | Total cost | Profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 1,000 | 30,000 | 22,000 | 8,000 |
| 28 | 1,200 | 33,600 | 24,500 | **9,100** |
| 26 | 1,400 | 36,400 | 27,500 | 8,900 |

Best price: **28**.

## Try it

(a) Price falls from 50 to 45, quantity rises from 2,000 to 2,300. Elastic or inelastic (simple % method)? (b) Market price 80, target margin 20% of price, current cost 70: target cost and required cost reduction?

**Answers:** (a) +15% ÷ −10% → 1.5, **elastic** (revenue rises from 100,000 to 103,500). (b) Target cost **64**; cut **6**.
