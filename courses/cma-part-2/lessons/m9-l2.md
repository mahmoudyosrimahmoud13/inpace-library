# Section E: payback, PI and risk in capital budgeting

## Payback period

Years to recover the investment from cash flows (undiscounted).

Investment 50,000; flows 15,000, 20,000, 25,000, 10,000. After 2 years: 35,000 recovered; 15,000 left of year 3's 25,000 → 0.6 year. Payback = **2.6 years**.

Pros: simple, a rough measure of liquidity and risk. Cons: ignores the time value of money and everything after payback.

**Discounted payback:** the same with discounted cash flows. Better, but still ignores later flows.

## Profitability index

> **PI = PV of future cash flows ÷ Initial investment**

From the last lesson: (118,469 + 13,973) ÷ 115,000 = **1.15**. Accept if PI > 1 (same as NPV > 0).

**Capital rationing:** with a limited budget, rank projects by PI (or choose the combination with the highest total NPV within the budget).

| Project | Investment | NPV | PI |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 100,000 | 30,000 | 1.30 |
| B | 60,000 | 24,000 | 1.40 |
| C | 40,000 | 10,000 | 1.25 |

Budget 160,000: B + A = 160,000, total NPV **54,000** (better than A + C = 40,000 or B + C = 34,000).

## Accounting rate of return

> **ARR = Average annual accounting profit ÷ Initial (or average) investment**

Easy to compute from accounting data, but ignores cash flows and timing.

## Risk in capital budgeting

- **Risk-adjusted discount rate:** use a higher rate for riskier projects (higher beta, new markets).
- **Certainty equivalents:** convert risky cash flows to certain amounts, discount at the risk-free rate.
- **Sensitivity analysis:** how far can sales or costs fall before NPV hits zero?
- **Scenario analysis** and **Monte Carlo simulation.**
- **Real options:** flexibility has value and traditional NPV ignores it.
  - Option to **expand** if things go well
  - Option to **abandon** if they don't
  - Option to **wait** for more information
  - Option to **switch** inputs or outputs

  NPV with options = traditional NPV + value of the options. A project with a slightly negative NPV may still be worth doing if it opens valuable options.

## Behavioral issues

Post-audits compare actual results with the forecast and discourage over-optimism. Managers may favor projects with quick payback if they're judged on short-term results.

## Try it

Investment 80,000; cash flows 30,000, 30,000, 30,000, 30,000. (a) Payback? (b) At 10% (annuity factor 3.170), NPV and PI?

**Answers:** (a) **2.67 years**. (b) PV 95,100; NPV **15,100**; PI **1.19**.
