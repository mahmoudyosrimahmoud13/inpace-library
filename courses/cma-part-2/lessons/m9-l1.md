# Section E: capital budgeting cash flows, NPV and IRR

## Relevant cash flows

Capital budgeting uses **incremental, after-tax cash flows**, not accounting profit.

**Initial investment (year 0):**
- Cost of the asset + shipping + installation
- + Increase in net working capital (inventory, receivables needed)
- − After-tax proceeds from selling an old asset being replaced

After-tax proceeds = Sale price − Tax on gain (or + tax saving on a loss), where gain = sale price − tax book value.

**Operating cash flows (each year):**

> **Operating cash flow = (Revenue − Cash costs) × (1 − t) + Depreciation × t**

Depreciation × t is the **depreciation tax shield**.

**Terminal year:**
- After-tax salvage value
- + Recovery of net working capital

Ignore: sunk costs, allocated overhead that doesn't change, and **interest** (financing is reflected in the discount rate).

## Example

New machine: cost 100,000; 5-year life; straight-line, no salvage for tax; actual salvage 10,000. Working capital 15,000. Annual cash savings 35,000. Tax 25%. Required return 10%.

- Year 0: −100,000 − 15,000 = **−115,000**
- Years 1–5: 35,000 × 0.75 + 20,000 × 0.25 = 26,250 + 5,000 = **31,250**
- Year 5 extra: salvage 10,000 × 0.75 = 7,500 + working capital back 15,000 = **22,500**

## NPV

> **NPV = PV of future cash flows − Initial investment**

PV of 31,250 for 5 years at 10% = 31,250 × 3.791 = 118,469. PV of 22,500 in year 5 = 22,500 × 0.621 = 13,973.

NPV = 118,469 + 13,973 − 115,000 = **17,442** → **accept** (NPV > 0).

NPV is the best method: it measures value added, uses all cash flows and the time value of money, and assumes reinvestment at the cost of capital.

## IRR

The **internal rate of return** is the discount rate that makes NPV = 0. Accept if IRR > required return. Here IRR ≈ 15%.

With even cash flows and no terminal amount: find the annuity factor = investment ÷ annual flow, then look it up in the table. Otherwise use `=IRR()` or trial and error.

**IRR problems:**
- Mutually exclusive projects of different sizes or timing: NPV and IRR can rank them differently. **Follow NPV.**
- Non-conventional cash flows (signs change more than once) can give **multiple IRRs**.
- IRR assumes reinvestment at the IRR itself. **MIRR** fixes this by assuming reinvestment at the cost of capital.

## Try it

Investment 60,000; after-tax cash flows of 18,000 a year for 5 years; no salvage; required return 12% (annuity factor 3.605). NPV? Accept?

**Answer:** 18,000 × 3.605 = 64,890 − 60,000 = **4,890** → accept (IRR ≈ 15.2%).
