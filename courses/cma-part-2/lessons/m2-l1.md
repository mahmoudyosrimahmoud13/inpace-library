# Section A: ratios for liquidity, leverage and activity

Section A tests whether you can **compute** ratios fast and **interpret** them. Learn the formulas cold.

## Liquidity: can the company pay short-term obligations?

| Ratio | Formula |
|---|---|
| Current ratio | Current assets ÷ Current liabilities |
| Quick (acid-test) ratio | (Cash + Marketable securities + Receivables) ÷ Current liabilities |
| Cash ratio | (Cash + Marketable securities) ÷ Current liabilities |
| Cash flow ratio | Operating cash flow ÷ Current liabilities |
| Net working capital | Current assets − Current liabilities |

**Exam favorite:** how does a transaction affect the current ratio? If the ratio is **above 1**, paying a current liability with cash **raises** it (e.g., 200/100 = 2.0 → pay 50 → 150/50 = 3.0). If below 1, the same payment lowers it. Buying inventory on credit raises both sides by the same amount: the ratio moves toward 1.

## Leverage and solvency: is long-term debt safe?

| Ratio | Formula |
|---|---|
| Debt to equity | Total debt (liabilities) ÷ Total equity |
| Debt to total assets | Total liabilities ÷ Total assets |
| Equity multiplier (financial leverage) | Total assets ÷ Total equity |
| Times interest earned | EBIT ÷ Interest expense |
| Fixed charge coverage | (EBIT + Lease payments) ÷ (Interest + Lease payments) |
| Degree of financial leverage | EBIT ÷ (EBIT − Interest) |

**Degree of financial leverage (DFL):** a 10% rise in EBIT raises EPS by DFL × 10%. Combine with operating leverage (DOL, from Part 1): **DTL = DOL × DFL**.

Example: EBIT 400, interest 100. DFL = 400 ÷ 300 = **1.33**. If DOL = 2.0, DTL = **2.67**: a 10% rise in sales raises EPS by 26.7%.

## Activity: how efficiently are assets used?

| Ratio | Formula |
|---|---|
| Receivables turnover | Net credit sales ÷ Average receivables |
| Days sales outstanding | 365 ÷ Receivables turnover |
| Inventory turnover | COGS ÷ Average inventory |
| Days in inventory | 365 ÷ Inventory turnover |
| Payables turnover | Purchases (or COGS) ÷ Average payables |
| Days payables outstanding | 365 ÷ Payables turnover |
| Operating cycle | Days in inventory + DSO |
| Cash conversion cycle | Operating cycle − DPO |
| Total asset turnover | Net sales ÷ Average total assets |
| Fixed asset turnover | Net sales ÷ Average net fixed assets |

Some questions use 360 days: use what the question says.

## Worked example

Sales 1,200,000 (all credit); COGS 720,000; average receivables 100,000; average inventory 90,000; average payables 60,000.

- Receivables turnover = 12; DSO = 365 ÷ 12 = **30.4 days**
- Inventory turnover = 8; days in inventory = **45.6 days**
- Payables turnover (using COGS) = 12; DPO = **30.4 days**
- Operating cycle = 76.0 days; cash conversion cycle = **45.6 days**

## Interpretation, the way the exam asks

- A **falling** inventory turnover can mean slow-moving or obsolete stock, or a deliberate build-up before a busy season.
- A **rising** DSO can mean looser credit terms or collection problems.
- High leverage magnifies ROE in good years and losses in bad ones.
- Ratios must be compared with the company's history and its **industry**.

## Try it

Current assets 600, current liabilities 400. (a) Current ratio? (b) After paying 100 of payables with cash? (c) After buying 100 of inventory on credit instead?

**Answers:** (a) 1.5. (b) 500 ÷ 300 = **1.67** (rises). (c) 700 ÷ 500 = **1.4** (falls, toward 1).
