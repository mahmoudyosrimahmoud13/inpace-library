# Section F: fraud, ethical culture and governance

## The fraud triangle

Fraud tends to happen when three things come together:

| Element | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Pressure (incentive)** | A need or motive | Debts, bonus targets, analysts' expectations |
| **Opportunity** | Weak controls let it happen and stay hidden | No separation of duties, poor oversight, management override |
| **Rationalization** | Justifying it to oneself | "I'll pay it back", "Everyone does it", "They owe me" |

Organizations can mainly reduce **opportunity** (internal controls) and influence rationalization (culture). Some models add **capability** (the fraud diamond).

## Types of occupational fraud

- **Asset misappropriation** (most common, smaller losses): theft of cash, fake vendors, expense reimbursement fraud, payroll ghosts.
- **Corruption:** bribery, kickbacks, conflicts of interest.
- **Financial statement fraud** (least common, biggest losses): fictitious revenue, hidden liabilities, improper asset valuation, timing manipulation.

Red flags: employees living beyond their means, refusing vacations, unusually close vendor relationships, missing documents, management overriding controls.

## Building an ethical culture

- **Tone at the top:** leaders' behavior matters more than any written code.
- A **code of conduct**, training, and clear consequences, applied equally to senior people.
- **Whistleblower hotlines**, anonymous and free from retaliation.
- Incentive plans that don't push people to cheat (balance financial and non-financial measures; avoid all-or-nothing targets).
- **Ethical leadership** and modeling: rewarding ethical behavior, not just results.

## Corporate governance and responsibility

- **Board of directors:** independent directors, separate chair and CEO where possible, an **audit committee** of independent members with financial expertise.
- **Sarbanes-Oxley (US):** CEO and CFO certify financial statements; management assesses internal control over financial reporting (Section 404); auditor independence rules.
- **Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA):** forbids bribing foreign officials; requires accurate books and adequate internal controls. Facilitating ("grease") payments for routine actions are a narrow exception under the FCPA but illegal in many countries (e.g., under the UK Bribery Act).
- **Corporate social responsibility and sustainability:** environmental, social and governance (ESG) factors; integrated reporting; the IMA expects management accountants to support ethical, sustainable decisions.

## Ethics and other people's decisions

Management accountants often face ethics through **others**: pressure to bend a forecast, a request to hide a control weakness, a vendor offering gifts. The safest habits: document, disclose conflicts, escalate through proper channels, and never let a deadline override accuracy.

## Try it

An accounts payable clerk can create vendors, approve invoices and issue payments. She is behind on her mortgage and has said the company "underpays everyone". Name the three fraud triangle elements in this case and one control to fix it.

**Answer:** pressure: the mortgage. Opportunity: she controls the whole payment cycle. Rationalization: "the company underpays everyone". Control: **separate duties**, so different people create vendors, approve invoices and release payments, with independent review of new vendors.
