# Section D: financial risk and hedging

## Financial risks

- **Interest rate risk:** rising rates raise floating-rate borrowing costs and cut bond values.
- **Exchange rate risk:** transaction, translation and economic (Module 5).
- **Commodity price risk:** fuel, metals, grain, energy.
- **Credit risk:** customers or counterparties may not pay.
- **Liquidity risk:** not being able to meet obligations or sell assets without a big loss.

## Derivatives: the hedging tools

| Tool | What it is | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Forward** | Private contract to buy or sell at a fixed price on a future date | No upfront cost; locks in the price; counterparty risk |
| **Future** | Standardized forward traded on an exchange | Marked to market daily; margin required; little counterparty risk |
| **Option** | The **right** (not obligation) to buy (call) or sell (put) at a strike price | Costs a premium; protects the downside, keeps the upside |
| **Swap** | Exchange of cash flows (e.g., fixed for floating interest) | Changes the type of exposure |

## Interest rate swap example

A company has a 10,000,000 floating-rate loan at SOFR + 1%. Fearing rising rates, it enters a swap: it pays a fixed 4% and receives SOFR.

Net cost = (SOFR + 1%) − SOFR + 4% = **5% fixed**, whatever SOFR does.

## Option basics

- **Call:** profits when the price rises above the strike. Value at expiry = max(0, price − strike).
- **Put:** profits when the price falls below the strike. Value at expiry = max(0, strike − price).
- An airline buying **call options on fuel** caps its fuel cost; if fuel prices fall, it lets the options expire and buys cheaply.

Example: a call with strike 50 and premium 3. At expiry the price is 58: payoff 8, net profit **5**. At 49: let it expire, loss **3** (the premium).

Option value rises with **volatility** and time to expiry. Black-Scholes and binomial models are named in the exam outline at a conceptual level.

## Hedging vs speculation

- A **hedge** offsets an existing exposure: losses on one side are offset by gains on the other.
- **Speculation** creates new exposure to profit from price moves. Board policy should forbid it in non-financial companies.

## Credit risk management

Credit scoring, credit limits, collateral, letters of credit, credit insurance, factoring without recourse, and diversifying customers.

## Try it

A company will buy 1,000 tonnes of copper in 6 months. Current price 9,000 per tonne. Which hedges protect it from a price rise, and which keeps the benefit if prices fall?

**Answer:** a **long forward or futures** contract locks in the price (no benefit from a fall). A **call option** caps the price at the strike plus premium and keeps the benefit if prices fall.
