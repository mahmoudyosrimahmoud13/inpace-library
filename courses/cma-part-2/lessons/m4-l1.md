# Section B: risk, return and CAPM

## Return and risk

- **Expected return:** the probability-weighted average of possible returns.
- **Risk:** how much returns vary around that average, measured by the **standard deviation**.
- **Coefficient of variation** = Standard deviation ÷ Expected return: risk per unit of return, for comparing investments of different size.

Example: a stock returns 20% (probability 0.3), 10% (0.5) or −5% (0.2). Expected return = 6 + 5 − 1 = **10%**.

## Diversification

Combining assets whose returns don't move perfectly together lowers portfolio risk. Risk that disappears with diversification is **unsystematic** (company-specific: a strike, a lawsuit). Risk that remains is **systematic** (market-wide: interest rates, recessions). Investors are only paid for bearing **systematic** risk.

**Correlation** ranges from −1 to +1: the lower, the bigger the diversification benefit.

## Beta

**Beta (β)** measures a stock's systematic risk relative to the market (β of the market = 1.0).
- β = 1.5: tends to move 1.5 times as much as the market
- β = 0.5: half as much
- A portfolio's beta is the weighted average of its stocks' betas

## CAPM

> **Required return = Risk-free rate + β × (Market return − Risk-free rate)**

The term (Market return − Risk-free rate) is the **market risk premium**.

Example: risk-free 4%, market return 10%, β = 1.3. Required return = 4% + 1.3 × 6% = **11.8%**.

If a stock's expected return is above its CAPM required return, it's **undervalued** (it plots above the security market line).

## Valuing bonds

A bond's price = PV of coupons + PV of face value, at the market yield.

Example: face 1,000, 6% annual coupon, 3 years, market yield 8%.
Price = 60 × 2.577 + 1,000 × 0.794 = 154.6 + 793.8 = **948.4** (a **discount**, because the coupon is below the market rate).

Rules the exam tests:
- Market rate **up** → bond price **down** (and vice versa).
- Coupon > yield → premium; coupon < yield → discount.
- Longer maturity and lower coupon → more price sensitivity (**duration**).

## Valuing stocks

**Preferred stock:** Value = Dividend ÷ Required return.

**Common stock, constant growth (Gordon model):**

> **P₀ = D₁ ÷ (k − g)**

D₀ = 2.00 (just paid), g = 5%, k = 11.8%. D₁ = 2.10. P₀ = 2.10 ÷ 0.068 = **30.88**.

Trap: the formula needs **next year's** dividend D₁ = D₀ × (1 + g).

Other approaches: **P/E multiples** (value = EPS × comparable P/E) and **discounted free cash flow**.

## Try it

Risk-free 3%, market return 9%, β = 0.8. (a) Required return? (b) A stock just paid 1.50, dividends grow 4%. Its value? (c) A 5-year 1,000 bond with a 7% coupon when yields are 5%: premium or discount?

**Answers:** (a) 3% + 0.8 × 6% = **7.8%**. (b) 1.56 ÷ (0.078 − 0.04) = **41.05**. (c) **Premium** (coupon above yield).
