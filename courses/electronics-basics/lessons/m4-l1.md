# Transistors as switches

A microcontroller pin can supply only about 20 mA at 5 V or 3.3 V. Motors, relays, LED strips and buzzers need more. A **transistor** lets a small signal switch a big current, like a tap controlled by a fingertip.

## The NPN bipolar transistor (BJT)

An NPN transistor has three legs: **base** (the control), **collector** and **emitter**. A small current into the base allows a much larger current from collector to emitter. The ratio is the **current gain** (hFE or β), often 100 or more.

![An MCU pin switches an LED from 9 V through an NPN transistor](el-npn.png)

**How to read it:** the circle with three legs is the transistor. The microcontroller pin (left) feeds the **base** through a 1 kΩ resistor. The LED and its 220 Ω resistor hang from +9 V down to the **collector** (top leg). The **emitter** (bottom leg, with the arrow) goes to ground. When the base gets a little current, the big current can flow from +9 V, through the LED, through the transistor, to ground.

- Pin high (5 V) → base current flows → the transistor turns on → current flows through the LED to ground.
- Pin low (0 V) → no base current → off.

This is a **low-side switch**: the transistor sits between the load and ground.

## Choosing the base resistor

To be fully on ("saturated"), give the base about 1/10 of the load current, which is more than the gain strictly needs. That guarantees it's fully switched even with a weak transistor.

```python
v_pin, v_be = 5.0, 0.7          # the base-emitter junction uses about 0.7 V, like a diode
load_ma = 30                     # LED or small relay current
i_base_ma = load_ma / 10         # rule of thumb for a hard switch
r_base = (v_pin - v_be) / (i_base_ma / 1000)
print(f"base current {i_base_ma:.1f} mA -> base resistor about {r_base:.0f} Ω (use 1 kΩ)")
```

```output
base current 3.0 mA -> base resistor about 1433 Ω (use 1 kΩ)
```

A 2N2222 or BC547 handles a few hundred mA at most. Check the datasheet's maximum collector current and power.

## The N-channel MOSFET

A **MOSFET** is controlled by **voltage** instead of current: the **gate** draws (almost) no current at all. When the gate is high enough above the **source**, the path from **drain** to source opens with very low resistance. They switch amps with little heat, which makes them the usual choice for motors and LED strips.

![An MCU pin switches a 12 V motor through an N-MOSFET with a 100 kΩ pull-down](el-mosfet.png)

**How to read it:** same idea as the NPN switch. The motor hangs from +12 V down to the MOSFET's **drain** (top). The **source** (bottom) goes to ground. The microcontroller pin drives the **gate** (left), and the 100 kΩ resistor from the gate to ground keeps it off until the pin says otherwise.

Two details that matter:

- **Logic-level MOSFET**: choose one that turns fully on at 3.3 V or 5 V on the gate. Check "R_DS(on)" in the datasheet at V_GS = 4.5 V (or 2.5 V for 3.3 V boards). Common examples: IRLZ44N, AO3400.
- **Pull-down resistor** (100 kΩ from gate to ground): while the microcontroller is starting up, its pin floats; the resistor keeps the MOSFET off so the motor doesn't twitch.

```python
r_ds_on = 0.022          # ohms, from the datasheet at Vgs = 4.5 V
for amps in [1, 3, 5]:
    print(f"{amps} A through the MOSFET -> {amps ** 2 * r_ds_on:.2f} W of heat")
```

```output
1 A through the MOSFET -> 0.02 W of heat
3 A through the MOSFET -> 0.20 W of heat
5 A through the MOSFET -> 0.55 W of heat
```

Heat = I² × R_DS(on): tiny, which is why MOSFETs are so good at this.

## Motors and relays need a flyback diode

Motors, relays and solenoids contain coils. When you switch a coil off, it produces a sudden high voltage spike in the opposite direction, which can destroy the transistor. Put a diode **across the coil**, pointing toward + (the cathode to the + supply), so the spike circulates harmlessly. A 1N4007 or 1N4148 works for small loads.

## NPN or MOSFET?

| | NPN BJT | N-MOSFET |
|---|---|---|
| Controlled by | base current | gate voltage |
| Good for | small loads, up to ~200 mA | anything from mA to many amps |
| Needs | a base resistor | a logic-level part, a gate pull-down |
| Heat | ~0.2 V × I | I² × R_DS(on), usually lower |

## Summary

- A transistor lets a weak pin switch a strong load; low-side: transistor between load and ground.
- NPN: base resistor for about 1/10 of the load current; MOSFET: logic-level type plus gate pull-down.
- Always add a flyback diode across motors, relays and other coils.
