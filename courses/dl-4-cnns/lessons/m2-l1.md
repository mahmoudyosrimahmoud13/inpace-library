# Convolutions over volumes, and one layer

An RGB image is `n×n×3`, so a filter is `f×f×3`: it spans every channel and gives **one** 2D output. Use 10 filters and
stack their outputs: the result has 10 channels. Add a bias per filter and a ReLU: that's one convolution layer.

**Parameters don't depend on the image size:** 10 filters of 3×3×3 have `10·(27 + 1) = 280` parameters, whether the
image is 64×64 or 1000×1000.

Notation for layer `l`: input `n_H × n_W × n_c[l−1]`, filters `f × f × n_c[l−1]`, output `n_H[l] × n_W[l] × n_c[l]`
where `n_c[l]` is the number of filters. PyTorch keeps channels first: `(batch, channels, height, width)`; Keras last:
`(batch, height, width, channels)`.
