# Siamese networks and triplet loss

A **Siamese network** runs both images through the same CNN to get embeddings `f(x)` (128 numbers, say), and compares
them: `d = ||f(x1) − f(x2)||²`.

**Triplet loss** (FaceNet) trains it with an anchor A, a positive P (same person), a negative N:
`L = max(||f(A) − f(P)||² − ||f(A) − f(N)||² + α, 0)`. The margin `α` stops the trivial solution of all embeddings
being equal.

Choose **hard** triplets (N looks like A) or the network learns nothing: in the notebook, easy negatives give a loss of
0.0, hard ones 0.188. Alternatives: train verification as binary classification on pairs of embeddings.

Today, face systems use better losses (ArcFace, a margin on the angle) and huge datasets. They also raise real
questions about **consent, bias and privacy**: many places regulate face recognition.
