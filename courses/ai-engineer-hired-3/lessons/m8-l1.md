# The framework and the rubric

You drive the conversation for 45 to 60 minutes. The [field guide](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/ai-engineering-field-guide) suggests five steps:

1. **Frame the problem** (5 to 10 min): users, scale, quality bar, latency and cost budgets, data, privacy, what "good" means and how you'd measure it.
2. **High-level architecture** (10 to 15 min): boxes and arrows from input to answer.
3. **Deep dive** (20 to 30 min): retrieval, tools, memory, evaluation, safety: the parts that make it work.
4. **Trade-offs** (10 to 15 min): what breaks, how you'd detect it, how it degrades gracefully.
5. **Wrap up:** summary, risks, the next iteration.

## Mistakes the guide lists as weak signals

- Jumping to a solution without requirements and success criteria.
- Treating the LLM as a source of truth instead of grounding it.
- Only the happy path: no failure modes, monitoring or evaluation.
- Naming tools ("I'd use LangChain") without explaining why.
- Ignoring cost and latency (tokens, tiering, caching).
- Ignoring safety (injection, data leaks, unsafe tools).
- Control flow in prompts instead of the orchestrator.
- Choosing agents for novelty.

**Senior signals:** reasoning under uncertainty, fluent trade-offs (retrieval speed vs context length, fine-tuning vs prompting, GPU cost vs latency), and clear narration of how data flows.
