## 1. Impairment of long-lived assets

When an asset can no longer earn its carrying value, it must be written down.

| 🌍 Framework | 🔎 Test | 🧮 Write-down to | ↩️ Reversal |
|---|---|---|---|
| US GAAP | Are undiscounted future cash flows ≥ carrying amount? | Fair value | **Never** |
| IFRS | Compare carrying amount with recoverable amount (higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use) | Recoverable amount | Allowed, except goodwill |

**Worked example:** machine carrying amount 200,000; expected undiscounted cash flows 180,000; fair
value 150,000. Under US GAAP the recoverability test fails (180,000 < 200,000), so the asset is
written down to fair value: loss of **50,000**. Under IFRS you compare 200,000 with the recoverable
amount directly and write down to that figure.

Goodwill is tested at least annually under both frameworks, and goodwill impairment is never reversed.

## 2. Intangible assets

| 💡 Type | 🧾 Treatment |
|---|---|
| Purchased intangible | Capitalise at cost |
| Internally generated brand, customer list, goodwill | **Never** capitalised |
| Research phase | Expensed as incurred |
| Development phase | IFRS: capitalise if criteria met. US GAAP: expense, with limited software exceptions |
| Finite life | Amortise over useful life |
| Indefinite life | No amortisation; test for impairment annually |

The IFRS development criteria, in plain words: the product is technically possible, you intend to
finish it, you can sell or use it, it will bring future benefits, you have the resources, and you can
measure the cost reliably.

**Worked example:** a company spends 300,000 on research and 200,000 on development that meets the
IFRS criteria. Under IFRS, profit falls by 300,000 now and the 200,000 sits as an asset to amortise.
Under US GAAP both are usually expensed, so profit falls by 500,000 — same spend, very different
statements.

## 3. Section A asset rules in one table

| 🧾 Asset | 🧮 Measure at | ⚠️ Classic trap |
|---|---|---|
| Receivables | NRV (receivables − allowance) | Write-offs do not change NRV |
| Inventory | Lower of cost and market/NRV | LIFO not allowed under IFRS |
| Investments | Fair value or amortised cost by class | Where the gain goes: profit or OCI |
| PP&E | Cost − accumulated depreciation | Depreciation is allocation, not valuation |
| Impaired assets | Fair value (US GAAP) or recoverable amount (IFRS) | Reversal rules differ |
| Intangibles | Cost, amortised if finite | Research is always expensed |

**A judgement to practise:** a manager wants to raise this year's profit. Levers include extending
useful lives, switching inventory method, delaying a write-down and capitalising development. Each
lever has a rule that limits it — that tension is what Section A is really testing.
