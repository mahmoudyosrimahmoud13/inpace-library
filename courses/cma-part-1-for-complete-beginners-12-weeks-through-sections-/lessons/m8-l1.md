## 1. Responsibility accounting

| 🎯 Centre | 👤 Responsible for | 📏 Measured by | 💡 Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost | Costs only | Cost per unit, cost variances | Production department |
| Revenue | Revenue only | Revenue vs quota | Sales branch |
| Profit | Revenue and costs | Segment profit | A product line |
| Investment | Profit and assets used | ROI, residual income, EVA | A division with capital authority |

The golden rule: **judge managers only on what they control.** Allocating head-office costs to a cost
centre and then judging that manager on profit is both unfair and useless.

## 2. The contribution approach

| Line | $ |
|---|---|
| Sales | 500,000 |
| − Variable costs | (300,000) |
| **Contribution margin** | **200,000** |
| − Traceable fixed costs (caused by this segment) | (120,000) |
| **Segment margin** | **80,000** |
| − Allocated common fixed costs | (50,000) |
| Profit after allocation | 30,000 |

Two decisions follow:

1. A **positive segment margin** means the segment helps pay for common costs — keep it, even if it
   looks loss-making after allocation.
2. Closing a segment removes its traceable costs and its contribution, but **not** the common costs;
   those simply land on the other segments.

**Worked decision:** closing this segment would lose 80,000 of margin and save 120,000 of traceable
cost, so company profit falls by 80,000 unless the freed capacity is used for something better.

## 3. Allocating common costs

| 🔧 Method | 🧮 Base | 👍 / 👎 |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | Only to operating departments | Simple, ignores support-to-support service |
| Step-down | Allocate support departments one at a time | Recognises some internal service |
| Reciprocal | Solve for mutual services | Most accurate, most complex |
| Single rate | One rate per unit used | Easy, but distorts capacity decisions |
| Dual rate | Fixed capacity charge + variable usage charge | Encourages honest capacity requests |

Allocations move profit between units without changing group profit. Use them for awareness and
pricing discipline — never as the sole basis for judging a manager.
