## 1. When one company controls another

Control normally means more than 50% of the voting shares. The parent then presents **one set of
statements for the group**.

| 🧩 Step | 🧠 What happens |
|---|---|
| Combine | Add parent and subsidiary line by line |
| Eliminate | Remove intercompany sales, balances and the parent's investment in the subsidiary |
| Goodwill | Purchase price + non-controlling interest − fair value of net identifiable assets |
| Non-controlling interest | The slice the parent does not own, shown inside equity |

**Worked example:** Parent pays $800,000 for 80% of Sub; fair value of Sub's net assets is $900,000.
NCI = 20% × 900,000 = $180,000. Goodwill = 800,000 + 180,000 − 900,000 = **$80,000**.

Why eliminate intercompany items: if the parent sells to the subsidiary, the group cannot owe itself
money or make profit by selling to itself. Removing those lines stops the group figures being
inflated.

## 2. Integrated reporting and the six capitals

Integrated reporting explains how an organisation creates value over time using six capitals:

| 🏦 Capital | 🧠 Meaning | 💡 Example |
|---|---|---|
| Financial | Money available | Cash, retained profit |
| Manufactured | Physical assets | Factories, trucks, IT |
| Intellectual | Knowledge and systems | Patents, brand, processes |
| Human | People's skills and health | Trained engineers |
| Social and relationship | Trust with others | Supplier relationships, licence to operate |
| Natural | Resources and environment | Water, energy, land |

Its guiding principles: strategic focus and future orientation, connectivity of information,
stakeholder relationships, materiality, conciseness, reliability and completeness, and consistency
and comparability.

**Memory hook:** "**F**inance, **M**achines, **I**deas, **H**earts, **S**ociety, **N**ature."
