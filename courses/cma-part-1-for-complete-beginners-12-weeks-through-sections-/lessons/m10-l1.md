## 1. The predetermined rate

> **Predetermined rate = budgeted overhead ÷ budgeted activity base**

**Worked example:** budgeted overhead $240,000 on 20,000 direct labour hours → **$12 per hour**.
During the year actual overhead is $251,000 and actual hours are 20,500, so applied overhead =
20,500 × $12 = **$246,000**.

| 📊 Result | Amount | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Applied | 246,000 | Charged into work in process |
| Actual | 251,000 | Really incurred |
| **Under-applied** | **5,000** | Products were charged too little |

Applied above actual = **over**-applied; applied below actual = **under**-applied.

## 2. One rate or many?

| 🏭 Approach | 🧮 How | 👍 / 👎 |
|---|---|---|
| Plant-wide | One base for the whole factory | Simple but distorts when departments differ |
| Departmental | A rate per department | More accurate |
| Activity-based | A rate per activity | Most accurate, most effort |

**Worked example:** Department A, labour intensive: $180,000 ÷ 10,000 labour hours = $18/hour.
Department B, machine intensive: $90,000 ÷ 6,000 machine hours = $15/hour. A product using 2 hours in A
and 3 machine hours in B absorbs 36 + 45 = **$81**. A plant-wide labour-hour rate would misprice it
badly.

## 3. Allocating support departments

| 🔧 Method | 🧠 How it works | 👍 / 👎 |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | Support costs go only to operating departments | Simple, ignores support-to-support |
| Step-down | Allocate one support department at a time | A reasonable compromise |
| Reciprocal | Solve simultaneous equations | Most accurate, most work |

**Worked example (direct method):** Maintenance $60,000 (70% to P1, 30% to P2) and IT $40,000 (40% to
P1, 60% to P2).

| Department | Maintenance | IT | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | 42,000 | 16,000 | 58,000 |
| P2 | 18,000 | 24,000 | 42,000 |

## 4. Single rate versus dual rate

| 💰 Method | 🧮 Structure | 💡 Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Single | One rate per unit used | Heavy users subsidise light users |
| Dual | Fixed capacity charge + variable usage charge | Users face the cost of the capacity they reserve |

**Worked example:** $120,000 fixed cost for 10,000 hours of capacity plus $5 per hour variable. A
department reserves 2,000 hours and uses 1,500.

- Single rate: ($120,000 ÷ 10,000) + $5 = $17 per hour → 1,500 × 17 = **$25,500**.
- Dual rate: 2,000 × $12 = $24,000 plus 1,500 × $5 = $7,500 → **$31,500**.

The dual rate charges for the capacity that was held in reserve, which is why it encourages honest
capacity requests.
