## 1. Receivables and the allowance method

Not every customer pays. Instead of waiting for a disaster, we estimate the loss each period and book
it against profit.

| 🧾 Event | 📝 Entry | 💥 Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Estimate bad debts | Dr Bad debt expense / Cr Allowance | Profit falls, net receivables fall |
| Write off a specific account | Dr Allowance / Cr Receivables | **No effect on profit or NRV** |
| Later recovery | Dr Receivables / Cr Allowance, then Dr Cash / Cr Receivables | Restores what was written off |

**Net realisable value (NRV) = receivables − allowance.** A write-off reduces both sides equally, so
NRV is unchanged — the classic exam point.

| 📐 Method | 🧮 Basis | 🎯 What it sets |
|---|---|---|
| % of sales | Sales × % | This period's expense |
| % of receivables | Receivables × % | The ending allowance balance |
| Aging | Each age bucket × its % | The ending allowance, most accurately |

**Worked example (aging):** current 100,000 at 1% = 1,000; 31-60 days 40,000 at 5% = 2,000; 61-90 days
20,000 at 15% = 3,000; over 90 days 10,000 at 40% = 4,000. Required allowance = **10,000**. If the
allowance already has a credit balance of 2,500, the expense is **7,500**; if it has a debit balance of
1,000, the expense is **11,000**. NRV = 170,000 − 10,000 = 160,000 in both cases.

**Getting cash from receivables:** *pledging* keeps them on your balance sheet and adds a loan;
*factoring* sells them for cash less a fee (sold without recourse → remove them).

## 2. Inventory cost

**Include:** purchase price, freight in, duties, conversion cost. **Exclude:** abnormal waste, storage
after production, selling costs, most interest.

Buy 100 units at $10 then 100 at $14, and sell 150:

| 🧮 Method | COGS | Ending inventory | 🧠 Story |
|---|---|---|---|
| FIFO | 1,500 | 700 | Oldest costs leave first |
| LIFO | 1,900 | 500 | Newest costs leave first |
| Weighted average | 1,800 | 600 | One blended price ($2,400 ÷ 200 = $12) |

| 📈 When prices rise | FIFO | LIFO |
|---|---|---|
| Profit | Higher | Lower |
| Inventory value | Higher | Lower |
| Tax | More | Less (US only) |
| IFRS | Allowed | **Not allowed** |

**Write-downs:** if inventory's value falls, write it down at once. US GAAP uses *lower of cost or
market*; IFRS uses *lower of cost or NRV*, and IFRS allows a later reversal up to original cost.

**Error direction:** if ending inventory is overstated, COGS is understated and profit is overstated
this year, then the error reverses next year. Two-year total profit is unchanged.
