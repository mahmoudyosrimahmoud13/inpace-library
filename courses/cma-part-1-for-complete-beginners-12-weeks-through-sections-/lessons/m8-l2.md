## 1. Transfer pricing methods

| 🎯 Method | 🧮 Price | 👍 / 👎 |
|---|---|---|
| Market price | Outside market price | Objective, but may not exist |
| Negotiated | Agreed between divisions | Shares the gain, but slow |
| Variable cost | Variable cost per unit | Fine with spare capacity, bad without |
| Full cost | Variable + allocated fixed | Simple, passes inefficiency on |
| Cost plus | Full cost + mark-up | Seller earns profit, mark-up arbitrary |
| Dual pricing | Seller gets market, buyer pays cost | Encourages trade, needs an adjustment |

**The rule to memorise:**

| 🔎 Situation | ✅ Transfer price range |
|---|---|
| Seller has spare capacity | Variable cost ≤ price ≤ market price |
| Seller has no spare capacity | Market price (lost contribution must be covered) |

**Worked example:** Division A's variable cost is $30 and the market price is $50; B would pay up to
$48. With spare capacity, a transfer at $40 adds 40 − 30 = **$10 per unit** to group profit. With no
spare capacity, the same transfer **costs** the group $10 per unit, because A gives up a $20
contribution on the outside sale.

## 2. Multinational transfer pricing

| 🌍 Motive | 🧠 Direction | ⚠️ Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Lower tax | Low price into a high-tax country | Arm's-length rules |
| Avoid tariffs | Low price into a high-tariff country | Customs valuation |
| Repatriate cash | High price out of a restricted country | Local regulation |
| Motivate managers | Any defensible price | Internal fairness |

Tax authorities require **arm's-length** prices — what two independent parties would agree.

## 3. Reportable segments (ASC 280)

| 🧪 Test | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Revenue | ≥ 10% of combined revenue |
| Profit or loss | ≥ 10% of the greater of total segment profits or total segment losses |
| Assets | ≥ 10% of combined assets |

Plus: reportable segments must cover at least **75% of external revenue**, and around ten segments is
usually the practical maximum.

**Worked example:** combined revenue $2,000,000 → threshold $200,000. A segment at $250,000 (12.5%)
passes; a segment at $180,000 (9%) fails the revenue test but may still qualify through the profit or
asset tests.
