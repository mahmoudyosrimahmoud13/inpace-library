## 2. Fixed overhead: two very different variances

> **Budget variance** = actual fixed overhead − budgeted fixed overhead
>
> **Volume variance** = budgeted fixed overhead − applied fixed overhead

| Variance | Calculation | Result | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Budget | 31,000 − 30,000 | **$1,000 U** | Overspent against the fixed budget |
| Volume | 30,000 − (1,000 × 20) | **$10,000 U** | Produced below normal capacity |
| **Under-applied** | 31,000 − 20,000 | **$11,000 U** | |

**Only the budget variance is controllable by the manager.** The volume variance is a capacity and
demand consequence — a favourite MCQ distinction.

## 3. Mix and yield

When inputs can be substituted, the usage variance splits into mix (right recipe?) and yield (enough
output from the input used?).

Standard mix for 1,000 litres: 600 litres mango at $2 and 400 litres filler at $1. Actual: 700 litres
mango and 300 litres filler.

| Input | Actual | Standard mix for 1,000 L | Difference | × price | Mix variance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mango | 700 | 600 | +100 | $2.00 | $200 U |
| Filler | 300 | 400 | −100 | $1.00 | $100 F |
| **Total** | | | | | **$100 U** |

The yield variance compares the standard cost of input actually used with the standard cost of output
actually produced: waste above the normal allowance is unfavourable.

## 4. Sales variances

> **Price variance** = (AP − SP) × actual units · **Volume variance** = (actual units − budget units) ×
> standard contribution margin

| Variance | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Selling price | (21 − 20) × 9,500 | **$9,500 F** |
| Sales volume | (9,500 − 10,000) × 8 | **$4,000 U** |
| **Net** | | **$5,500 F** |

With several products the volume variance splits again into **mix** (did we sell the right products?)
and **quantity** (did we sell enough in total?).
