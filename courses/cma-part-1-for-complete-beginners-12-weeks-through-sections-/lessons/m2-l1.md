## 1. Who the statements are for

General purpose financial statements exist for outsiders who cannot ask for their own reports:
**present and potential investors, lenders and other creditors** above all.

| 👥 User | 🔍 Their question | 📊 First statement they read |
|---|---|---|
| Investor | Will I get growth and dividends? | Income statement |
| Bank | Can they repay me? | Balance sheet and cash flow |
| Supplier | Will I be paid on time? | Cash flow |
| Tax authority | Is the tax right? | All of them |

Two assumptions sit underneath everything: **going concern** (the business will keep trading) and
**accrual basis** (record when earned or incurred, not when cash moves). The qualities that make
information useful are **relevance** and **faithful representation**, supported by comparability,
verifiability, timeliness and understandability.

## 2. The balance sheet: a photo at one moment

| 🧾 Group | 📌 Typical items | 🧠 Beginner reading |
|---|---|---|
| Current assets | Cash, receivables, inventory, prepaid expenses | Turns into cash within a year |
| Non-current assets | PP&E, intangibles, goodwill, right-of-use assets | Held for the long term |
| Current liabilities | Payables, accruals, unearned revenue, short-term loans | Due within a year |
| Non-current liabilities | Bonds, long leases, pensions | Due later |
| Equity | Share capital, share premium, retained earnings, accumulated OCI | Owners' claim |

**The operating cycle** is buy → hold → sell → collect. If it is longer than a year, the cycle — not
the calendar — decides what is current.

**Worked example of classification:** a five-year loan with $50,000 payable in eight months shows
$50,000 as a current liability and the rest as non-current. Treasury shares are a deduction inside
equity, never an asset.

## 3. The income statement: a film of the period

| ➗ Step | 💡 Line | 💡 Example ($000) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sales − cost of goods sold = gross profit | 500 − 300 = 200 |
| 2 | − selling, general and admin = operating income | 200 − 120 = 80 |
| 3 | ± other income and interest = income before tax | 80 − 10 = 70 |
| 4 | − tax = income from continuing operations | 70 − 21 = 49 |
| 5 | ± discontinued operations, net of tax = net income | 49 + 0 = 49 |

Three details that earn marks:

- **Discontinued operations** sit below the tax line, net of tax, because they will not repeat.
- **Extraordinary items no longer exist** under US GAAP; unusual items stay inside continuing
  operations, before tax.
- **EPS** = (net income − preferred dividends) ÷ weighted-average common shares. **Diluted EPS** also
  assumes convertibles and options are exercised, so it is always ≤ basic EPS.

**Worked example:** net income 49,000, preferred dividends 4,000, weighted shares 15,000 →
basic EPS = 45,000 ÷ 15,000 = **$3.00**.
