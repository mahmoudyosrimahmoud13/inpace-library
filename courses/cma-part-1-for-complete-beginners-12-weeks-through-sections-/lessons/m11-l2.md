## 1. Enterprise risk management

ERM extends control across the whole risk universe: strategic, operational, financial, compliance and
reputational. The COSO ERM (2017) components are governance and culture, strategy and objective-setting,
performance, review and revision, and information, communication and reporting.

| 🎯 Response | 🧠 Meaning | 💡 Example |
|---|---|---|
| Accept | Live with it | Small theft risk |
| Avoid | Stop the activity | Leave a market |
| Reduce | Lower likelihood or impact | Controls, training, insurance |
| Share | Pass it on | Insurance, outsourcing, hedging |

Four words to keep apart: **inherent risk** (before controls), **residual risk** (after controls),
**risk appetite** (how much the board accepts) and **risk tolerance** (acceptable variation).

## 2. External auditors and the PCAOB

The external auditor gives an independent opinion on the financial statements; the **PCAOB** regulates
auditors of US public companies. Reports can be unmodified (clean), qualified, adverse or a disclaimer.
An audit gives reasonable, not absolute, assurance, and it targets material misstatement rather than
every fraud.

## 3. Sarbanes-Oxley (SOX, 2002)

| 📜 Provision | 🧠 Requirement |
|---|---|
| Section 302 | CEO and CFO certify the statements and disclosure controls each quarter |
| Section 404 | Management reports on internal control over financial reporting; the auditor attests |
| Audit committee | Must be independent and handle auditor appointment and complaints |
| Partner rotation | Lead audit partner rotates (five years) |
| Non-audit services | Restrictions on consulting for audit clients |
| Whistleblowers | Retaliation is a crime |
| Records | Retention requirements, including electronic records |

The chain to remember: **certification → accountability → independence → protection for the person who
reports the problem.**

## 4. The Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (1977)

| 🧩 Part | 🧠 Meaning |
|---|---|
| Anti-bribery | No payments of anything of value to foreign officials to win or keep business |
| Books and records | Accurate records and an adequate system of internal controls |
| Who it covers | US companies and foreign firms with US connections |
| Penalties | Fines, disgorgement, personal liability |

The exam point: the FCPA is not only about bribes — weak internal controls can themselves be a
violation.

## 5. Internal audit and the three lines

Internal audit is an **independent, objective assurance and consulting** activity.

| 🛡️ Line | 👤 Who | 🧠 Role |
|---|---|---|
| First | Operational management | Owns and manages risk daily |
| Second | Risk, compliance, quality, controllership | Sets frameworks, monitors, advises |
| Third | Internal audit | Independent assurance to the board |

**Worked scenario:** a warehouse manager both counts stock and posts the inventory records. The failing
control is **segregation of duties** (control activities), sitting inside a weak control environment.
The fix is to separate counting from recording and add independent cycle counts — a preventive control
backed by a detective one.
