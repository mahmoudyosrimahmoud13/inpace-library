# SLIs, SLOs and error budgets

| Term | Is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **SLI** (indicator) | A measurement of good events / valid events | Share of checkout requests that succeed in under 500 ms |
| **SLO** (objective) | Your target for the SLI over a window | 99.5% over 28 days |
| **SLA** (agreement) | A contract with penalties | 99.0%, with credits if missed (looser than the SLO) |
| **Error budget** | 100% minus the SLO | 0.5% of requests may fail or be slow: about 3.4 hours of full outage per 28 days |

## Good SLIs

- Measured **as close to the user as possible** (load balancer or client, not CPU).
- Ratio of good to total: `good / valid`.
- Few: 1-3 per user journey (availability, latency, maybe freshness or correctness).

```promql
# Availability SLI over 28 days
sum(rate(http_requests_total{route="/checkout",status!~"5.."}[28d]))
  / sum(rate(http_requests_total{route="/checkout"}[28d]))

# Latency SLI: share faster than 500 ms (needs a 0.5 bucket)
sum(rate(http_request_duration_seconds_bucket{route="/checkout",le="0.5"}[28d]))
  / sum(rate(http_request_duration_seconds_count{route="/checkout"}[28d]))
```

## Error budget policy: the important part

Write down, with product owners, what happens as the budget is used:

| Budget left | Team does |
|---|---|
| Plenty | Ship freely; experiments welcome |
| Under 25% | Extra care: smaller releases, more canarying |
| Exhausted | Freeze risky launches; spend the time on reliability work until it recovers |

This turns "devs want speed, ops want stability" into one shared number. If you never use your budget, the SLO may be too strict, or you could ship faster.

Read the SRE book chapter "Service Level Objectives" and the SRE Workbook chapter "Implementing SLOs" for depth.
