# What reliability means

Running a service well is a skill of its own. **Site Reliability Engineering (SRE)**, from Google, treats operations as an engineering problem: measure reliability, set targets, automate the toil, learn from failures.

## The three ideas this course is built on

1. **Users define reliability.** Your CPU graph doesn't matter; whether checkout works fast does.
2. **100% is the wrong target.** Every extra nine costs much more and slows you down, and users can't tell 99.99% from 100% through their own Wi-Fi. Pick a target, then *spend* the remaining failure budget on shipping.
3. **You can't fix what you can't see.** Good telemetry turns "it's slow, no idea why" into "p99 latency of /checkout doubled after the 14:02 deploy because of a new query".

## Nines in real time

| Availability | Downtime per 30 days |
|---|---|
| 99% | 7.2 hours |
| 99.9% | 43 minutes |
| 99.95% | 22 minutes |
| 99.99% | 4.3 minutes |

## Observability vs monitoring

| Monitoring | Observability |
|---|---|
| Checks for problems you predicted ("disk > 90%") | Lets you ask new questions about problems you didn't predict |
| Dashboards and alerts | Rich, correlated **metrics, logs and traces** you can slice by any field |

You need both. This course builds them with open-source tools you can run anywhere: **Prometheus**, **Grafana**, **Loki** and **OpenTelemetry**, with CloudWatch notes for AWS.

The free **Google SRE books** (link in downloads) are the source of most ideas here; read the chapters named in each lesson if you want depth.
