# Precision, recall, F1 and thresholds

When 0.5% of patients have a rare disease, "always say no" is 99.5% accurate and useless. Use a **confusion matrix**:

| | Actual 1 | Actual 0 |
|---|---|---|
| Predicted 1 | true positive (TP) | false positive (FP) |
| Predicted 0 | false negative (FN) | true negative (TN) |

```text
precision = TP / (TP + FP)     of those we flagged, how many were right
recall    = TP / (TP + FN)     of the real positives, how many we found
F1        = 2PR / (P + R)      the harmonic mean: low if either is low
```

**Threshold trade-off:** raising the threshold from 0.5 to 0.7 gives higher precision, lower recall; lowering it
gives the opposite. Choose by the **cost of each mistake**: missing cancer (FN) is worse than a follow-up test (FP).

## Going further

- **ROC curve** (TPR vs FPR) and **AUC**: threshold-free ranking quality. With heavy imbalance, the
  **precision-recall curve** and average precision are more informative.
- Fixes for imbalance: class weights in the loss, resampling, focal loss, and better metrics, not just more tricks.
