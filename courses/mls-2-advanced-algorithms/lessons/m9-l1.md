# Decision trees: purity and information gain

A tree asks feature questions from the **root** to **leaves**. Learning: at each node, pick the split that most
increases **purity**; stop when a node is pure, a depth limit is hit, gains are tiny, or too few examples remain.

**Entropy** (for a node with a fraction `p₁` of positives):

```text
H(p₁) = −p₁ log₂(p₁) − (1 − p₁) log₂(1 − p₁)       H(0.5) = 1, H(0) = H(1) = 0
```

**Information gain** of a split:

```text
gain = H(p₁ at root) − ( w_left · H(p₁ left) + w_right · H(p₁ right) )
```

where `w_left` is the fraction of examples going left. Pick the feature (and threshold) with the highest gain.

- **Categorical features** with many values: **one-hot encode** into several 0/1 features.
- **Continuous features**: try thresholds (midpoints between sorted values) and take the best.
- **Regression trees**: split to reduce **variance** of `y`; a leaf predicts the mean of its examples.
- Gini impurity (`1 − Σ pₖ²`) is the common alternative to entropy (scikit-learn's default).
