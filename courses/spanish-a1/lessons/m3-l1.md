# A normal day

Lucía asks Omar about his day. Notice how many verbs end in -o when Omar talks about himself.

## Listen first: the conversation

**What's your day like?**

| Spanish | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Lucía:** ¿A qué hora te levantas? | What time do you get up? |
| **Omar:** Me levanto a las siete. Desayuno café con pan y salgo de casa a las ocho. | I get up at seven. I have coffee and bread for breakfast and leave home at eight. |
| **Lucía:** ¿Trabajas cerca de casa? | Do you work near home? |
| **Omar:** No, trabajo en el centro. Tomo el metro. ¿Y tú? | No, I work in the centre. I take the metro. And you? |
| **Lucía:** Yo doy clases por la mañana y por la tarde estudio francés. | I teach in the morning and in the afternoon I study French. |
| **Omar:** ¿Y qué haces por la noche? | And what do you do in the evening? |
| **Lucía:** Ceno con mis amigos, leo un poco y me acuesto a las once. | I have dinner with my friends, read a bit and go to bed at eleven. |
| **Omar:** ¡Qué día tan completo! | What a full day! |

## Shadow it

Play the conversation in your head or read it aloud **three times**: first slowly, then at normal speed, then without looking at the meaning column. If you have the audio from the links below, pause after each line and copy the speaker's rhythm and melody, not just the words. This is called **shadowing**, and it trains your ear and your mouth together.

## Words and phrases to keep

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ¿A qué hora...? | At what time...? |
| Me levanto a las siete. | I get up at seven. |
| Desayuno / como / ceno | I have breakfast / lunch / dinner |
| Salgo de casa. | I leave home. |
| Trabajo en... / Estudio... | I work in... / I study... |
| Tomo el metro / el autobús. | I take the metro / the bus. |
| por la mañana / por la tarde / por la noche | in the morning / afternoon / evening |
| ¿Qué haces...? | What do you do...? |
| Me acuesto a las once. | I go to bed at eleven. |
| todos los días / a veces / nunca | every day / sometimes / never |
| ¿Qué hora es? Son las tres. | What time is it? It's three. |
| Es la una y media. | It's half past one. |

## Say it right

**Where's the stress?** Spanish has simple rules:

1. Words ending in a **vowel, n or s**: stress the **second-to-last** syllable: *tra-BA-jo*, *TO-mas*, *CO-men*.
2. Words ending in **another consonant**: stress the **last** syllable: *co-MER*, *ciu-DAD*, *es-pa-ÑOL*.
3. A written **accent** breaks the rule and shows the stress: *ca-FÉ*, *es-TÁ*, *MÉ-di-co*, *ár-bol*.

Say: *trabajo, trabajar, médico, café, desayuno, francés*. The stress can change meaning: *trabajo* (I work) vs *trabajó* (he worked).
