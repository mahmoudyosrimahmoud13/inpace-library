# Ser and me llamo: who are you?

## The grammar of this unit: ser (to be) and llamarse

Look at these lines from the conversation: *Soy de Egipto. Soy ingeniero. ¿De dónde eres?* They all use **ser**, "to be". You use *ser* for **who or what someone is**: name, origin, job, nationality.

| | ser | llamarse |
|---|---|---|
| yo (I) | **soy** | **me llamo** |
| tú (you, friendly) | **eres** | **te llamas** |
| él / ella / usted (he / she / you, polite) | **es** | **se llama** |
| nosotros / nosotras (we) | **somos** | **nos llamamos** |
| vosotros / vosotras (you all, Spain) | **sois** | **os llamáis** |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes (they / you all) | **son** | **se llaman** |

Three things to notice:

1. **You usually drop the pronoun.** The verb already says who: *Soy de México* (not *Yo soy*), unless you want to stress it: *Yo también*.
2. **Tú or usted?** Use *tú* with friends, people your age and children; *usted* with older people and in formal situations. *Usted* uses the same verb form as *él/ella*: *¿De dónde es usted?* In Latin America, *ustedes* is used for "you all" in every situation instead of *vosotros*.
3. **Jobs and nationalities change for women:** *ingeniero → ingeniera*, *profesor → profesora*, *egipcio → egipcia*. And there's no *un/una* before a job: *Soy médico* (I'm a doctor).

## Speak: your voice note

Record a 30-second voice note introducing yourself to a new classmate: greet them, say your name, where you're from, where you live and what you do, ask their name, and say goodbye.

Record it in inPace as a voice note (or on your phone). Listen back once, fix one thing, and record it again. Saying the same task twice makes the second try faster and more accurate.

A model answer (yours can be different):

> ¡Hola! ¿Qué tal? Me llamo Sara. Soy de Jordania, de Amán, pero vivo en Valencia. Soy enfermera. ¿Y tú? ¿Cómo te llamas? Encantada. ¡Hasta luego!
