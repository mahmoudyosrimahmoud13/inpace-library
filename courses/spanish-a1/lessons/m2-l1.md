# Talking about your family

Lucía and Omar meet again for coffee. Omar shows her some photos on his phone.

## Listen first: the conversation

**Photos on the phone**

| Spanish | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Lucía:** ¿Quién es ella? | Who is she? |
| **Omar:** Es mi hermana, Nour. Tiene veinte años. | She's my sister, Nour. She's twenty. |
| **Lucía:** ¡Qué guapa! ¿Y él? | She's beautiful! And him? |
| **Omar:** Es mi padre. Es médico y es muy simpático. | That's my father. He's a doctor and he's very nice. |
| **Lucía:** ¿Tienes más hermanos? | Do you have more brothers and sisters? |
| **Omar:** Sí, tengo dos hermanos. Son altos, como mi padre. ¿Y tú? | Yes, I have two brothers. They're tall, like my father. And you? |
| **Lucía:** Yo no tengo hermanos, soy hija única. Pero tengo un perro. | I don't have any brothers or sisters, I'm an only child. But I have a dog. |
| **Omar:** ¿Cómo se llama? | What's its name? |
| **Lucía:** Se llama Toby. Es pequeño y un poco loco. | His name is Toby. He's small and a bit crazy. |

## Shadow it

Play the conversation in your head or read it aloud **three times**: first slowly, then at normal speed, then without looking at the meaning column. If you have the audio from the links below, pause after each line and copy the speaker's rhythm and melody, not just the words. This is called **shadowing**, and it trains your ear and your mouth together.

## Words and phrases to keep

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| mi padre / mi madre | my father / my mother |
| mis padres | my parents |
| mi hermano / mi hermana | my brother / my sister |
| mi hijo / mi hija | my son / my daughter |
| mi marido / mi mujer | my husband / my wife |
| ¿Tienes hermanos? | Do you have brothers and sisters? |
| Tengo dos hermanos. | I have two brothers (or siblings). |
| ¿Cuántos años tienes? | How old are you? |
| Tengo treinta años. | I'm thirty. |
| Es alto / baja / simpático | He's tall / she's short / he's nice |
| hijo único / hija única | only child |
| ¿Quién es? | Who is it? / Who's that? |

## Say it right

Spanish has two **r** sounds, and they change the meaning: *pero* (but) vs *perro* (dog).

- A single **r** between vowels is a quick tap of the tongue, like the *tt* in American *butter*: *pero*, *Lara*, *caro*.
- **rr**, and an **r** at the start of a word, is a trill: the tongue vibrates several times: *perro*, *Roma*, *rojo*.

If you can't trill yet, practise saying *butter* fast, then *pero*, then hold the tap longer: *perrrro*. It takes weeks for many learners. Keep trying, people will understand you meanwhile.
