# At the café

Omar orders breakfast in a café. Waiters in Spain often use usted with customers, and customers say quería (I'd like) to be polite.

## Listen first: the conversation

**Ordering breakfast**

| Spanish | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Camarero:** Buenos días. ¿Qué le pongo? | Good morning. What can I get you? |
| **Omar:** Quería un café con leche y una tostada con tomate, por favor. | I'd like a white coffee and toast with tomato, please. |
| **Camarero:** Muy bien. ¿Algo más? | Very good. Anything else? |
| **Omar:** Sí, un zumo de naranja. ¿Cuánto es todo? | Yes, an orange juice. How much is it all? |
| **Camarero:** Son seis euros con cincuenta. | That's six euros fifty. |
| **Omar:** Aquí tiene. | Here you are. |
| **Camarero:** Gracias. ¿Le gusta el café de aquí? | Thanks. Do you like the coffee here? |
| **Omar:** Sí, me gusta mucho. ¡Y me encantan las tostadas! | Yes, I like it a lot. And I love the toast! |

## Shadow it

Play the conversation in your head or read it aloud **three times**: first slowly, then at normal speed, then without looking at the meaning column. If you have the audio from the links below, pause after each line and copy the speaker's rhythm and melody, not just the words. This is called **shadowing**, and it trains your ear and your mouth together.

## Words and phrases to keep

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ¿Qué le pongo? / ¿Qué desea? | What can I get you? |
| Quería... / Quiero... | I'd like... / I want... |
| ¿Algo más? | Anything else? |
| Nada más, gracias. | Nothing else, thanks. |
| ¿Cuánto es? / ¿Cuánto cuesta? | How much is it? / How much does it cost? |
| Aquí tiene. | Here you are. |
| La cuenta, por favor. | The bill, please. |
| un zumo (Spain) / un jugo (Latin America) | a juice |
| Me gusta el café. | I like coffee. |
| No me gusta el pescado. | I don't like fish. |
| Me encantan las frutas. | I love fruit. |
| ¿Tiene...? / ¿Hay...? | Do you have...? / Is there...? |

## Say it right

**b and v** sound **the same** in Spanish. At the start of a phrase or after *m/n* they're like English *b*: *vino*, *bien*, *un vaso*. Between vowels they're softer, with the lips almost touching: *uva*, *Cuba*, *la verdad*. Don't make an English *v* sound with your teeth.

**d** between vowels is soft, like *th* in *the*: *nada*, *todo*, *helado*.

Say: *vino, bebida, uva, nada, todo, helado, ¿Cuánto vale?*
