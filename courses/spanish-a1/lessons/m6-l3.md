# Level check: can you do A1?

The CEFR (the European framework that defines A1, A2, B1...) describes each level by what you **can do**. Read each line and answer honestly: *yes*, *almost*, or *not yet*.

## I can...

- understand and use everyday phrases to introduce myself and others;
- ask and answer simple questions about where I live, people I know and things I have;
- talk about my day with times, and understand someone else's;
- ask the way and understand simple directions;
- order food and drink, ask prices and understand numbers to 100;
- say what I like and don't like;
- make simple plans with someone: what, when, where;
- understand slow, clear speech on these topics, especially with pictures or context.

For every *not yet*, go back to that unit: listen to the conversation again, shadow it, and redo the voice note.

## Final tasks

1. **Listening:** watch three Superbeginner or Beginner videos on [Dreaming Spanish](https://www.dreaming.com/) on topics from this course. Write down five words you understood that you didn't know before.
2. **Speaking:** record a **2-minute** voice note about yourself: who you are, your family, your day, your town, what you like and your plans for the weekend. Don't read a script; use notes with key words only.
3. **Writing:** write an email of about 80 words to a new friend introducing yourself.

## What next?

Keep listening every day (aim for 20 minutes), and move on to **Spanish A2**, where you'll tell stories in the past, compare things, and handle travel and health situations. People who keep up daily listening often find A2 much easier than A1.
