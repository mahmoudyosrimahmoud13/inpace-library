# Learning-rate decay and schedules

Big steps early, small steps late. The slides' formulas (`α = α₀ / (1 + decay·epoch)`, exponential, step) still work;
today you mostly use a scheduler object:

![Learning-rate schedules](lr_schedules.png)

```python
sched = torch.optim.lr_scheduler.OneCycleLR(opt, max_lr=1e-2, total_steps=epochs * len(loader))
# call sched.step() after every optimizer step
lr = keras.optimizers.schedules.CosineDecay(1e-4, decay_steps, warmup_target=3e-3, warmup_steps=500)
```

**Warmup then cosine** is the standard for transformers: a short warmup avoids big unstable steps while Adam's
statistics are still empty.
