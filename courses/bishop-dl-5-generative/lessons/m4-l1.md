# Read: diffusion models, noising and denoising (20.1–20.2)

Read the opening of chapter 20, and sections 20.1 and 20.2 (pages 581 to 593).

## The idea (opening)

Take a training image and add a little Gaussian noise, again and again, for hundreds of steps, until nothing is left but
noise. Then train a network to **reverse one step**. To generate, start from pure noise and apply the reverse step
hundreds of times. These **denoising diffusion probabilistic models** (DDPMs) are behind most of today's image, video and
audio generators.

## The forward encoder (20.1)

Each step shrinks the previous value slightly and adds noise of variance `β_t` (20.1 to 20.3). Two facts make the whole
method practical:

- **The diffusion kernel** (20.1.1): you can jump from `x` straight to any step `t` with one formula,
  `z_t = √α_t x + √(1 − α_t) ε`, where in this book `α_t` is the **product** of `(1 − β_τ)` up to `t` (many papers write
  it `ᾱ_t`; don't mix the notations up). After many steps, `z_T` is a standard Gaussian, whatever `x` was.
- **The conditional distribution** (20.1.2): the reverse of one step, `q(z_{t−1} | z_t)`, is intractable in general,
  but **given the clean `x`** it's a simple Gaussian. That's what training exploits.

## The reverse decoder (20.2)

A network learns `p(z_{t−1} | z_t, w)`, a Gaussian whose mean it predicts. The derivation is long; read it for the
structure, and know the destination:

- **Training** (20.2.1 to 20.2.3) maximizes an ELBO, the same bound as in VAEs: a diffusion model is a very deep VAE
  whose encoder is fixed noise and whose latent variables have the same size as the image.
- **Predicting the noise** (20.2.4): the ELBO simplifies to a remarkably simple loss: noise an image to a random step,
  and train the network `g(z_t, w, t)` to predict the noise `ε` that was added, with squared error (Algorithm 20.1).
- **Generating** (20.2.5): Algorithm 20.2. Start from noise, and at each step remove a fraction of the predicted noise
  and add a little fresh noise. Hundreds of network passes per sample: diffusion models are slow to sample.

## What to take from the section

- The forward process has no parameters and a closed form, so training examples at any noise level are free.
- All the network has to do is predict noise. The Build lesson shows that a **perfect** noise predictor rebuilds the data
  exactly; the project trains a real one on digits.
