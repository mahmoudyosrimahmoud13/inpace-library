# Read: score matching and guided diffusion (20.3–20.4)

Read sections 20.3 and 20.4 (pages 594 to 603).

## Score matching (20.3)

The **score** of a distribution is the gradient of its log density, `∇ ln p(x)`: an arrow pointing towards more
probable data. Part 4's Langevin dynamics showed that knowing the score is enough to **sample**, even without the
density itself.

- **The score loss** (20.3.1) would fit a network to the true score, which we don't know.
- **The modified loss** (20.3.2), denoising score matching: add noise to data and fit the network to the score of the
  noise distribution, which we do know. On average this gives the score of the noisy data. And that target is the added
  noise, rescaled: a noise-predicting diffusion network **is** a score estimator. The Build lesson checks this.
- **Noise variance** (20.3.3): use many noise levels, from large (to explore) to tiny (to be precise), and sample with
  **annealed Langevin dynamics**, the noise-conditional score networks.
- **Stochastic differential equations** (20.3.4): in the limit of infinitely many steps, both diffusion and its reverse
  become SDEs (20.55, 20.56), and there's an equivalent ODE (20.57) that allows faster, deterministic sampling.

## Guided diffusion (20.4)

How do you ask for a particular kind of image (a dog, a digit 7, a picture described by a sentence)?

- **Classifier guidance** (20.4.1): Bayes' theorem splits the conditional score into the unconditional score plus the
  gradient of a classifier's log probability (20.58). Scale the classifier term by λ (20.59). It works, but needs a
  separate classifier trained on noisy images.
- **Classifier-free guidance** (20.4.2): mix a conditional and an unconditional score (20.60):
  `λ ∇ ln p(x | c) + (1 − λ) ∇ ln p(x)`. One network can provide both: during training, replace the label with a "null"
  token 10 to 20% of the time. λ = 1 is plain conditioning; λ > 1 actively pushes away from images that ignore the label.
  This is what text-to-image systems use, with the label replaced by an encoded text prompt.

The trade-off to remember: strong guidance gives samples that match the condition clearly, at the cost of **diversity**.
You'll measure it twice: exactly, in the Build lesson, and on real digits in the project.

## What to take from the section

- Diffusion models and score-based models are two views of the same thing.
- Guidance steers generation with a single knob, and that knob trades variety for faithfulness to the request.
