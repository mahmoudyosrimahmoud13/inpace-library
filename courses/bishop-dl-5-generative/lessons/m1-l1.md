# Start here

This is part 5 of 5, the last part of the course series on **Deep Learning: Foundations and Concepts** by Christopher M.
Bishop and Hugh Bishop (Springer, 2024). Part 5 covers chapters 17 to 20: the four families of **deep generative
models** that make new images, sounds and molecules today.

| Chapter | Model | The idea in one line |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | Generative adversarial networks (GANs) | a generator learns to fool a discriminator |
| 18 | Normalizing flows | an invertible network turns noise into data, with an exact likelihood |
| 19 | Autoencoders and VAEs | squeeze data into a small code, then decode new data from random codes |
| 20 | Diffusion models | learn to remove noise one small step at a time |

## Get the book

Each **Read** lesson opens the right pages of *your own copy* (the Springer PDF, or read free online at
[bishopbook.com](https://www.bishopbook.com) using the section numbers). On this course's page, press **Find it for me**
in the box **"This course reads a book you get yourself"**.

## Before you start

You need parts 1 and 2 (probability, likelihood, KL divergence, deep networks and backpropagation), and part 4's
evidence lower bound (ELBO) and Langevin dynamics: VAEs and diffusion models are built directly on them. Part 3's
convolutional networks help for the image models.

## Module projects for your CV

**Every module ends with a real project in PyTorch** that you build and publish on GitHub:

- a **GAN that draws clothes**, with a separate judge network that measures quality and catches mode collapse;
- **Real NVP from scratch**, a normalizing flow, compared with Gaussian mixtures on three 2-D shapes;
- a **variational autoencoder** on handwritten digits, with a map of its latent space;
- a **diffusion model** that draws digits from pure noise;
- **classifier-free guidance**: the same diffusion model drawing the digit you ask for, and what guidance costs.

Day 6 is the **big project**: the generators side by side, judged the same way. Each project lesson comes with a
notebook (attached to the lesson and saved on your device; on the website it opens in Google Colab) and the finished
code under `projects/` in the course files. For the projects: `pip install torch pandas pyarrow pillow matplotlib
scikit-learn`. A GPU helps but isn't needed: everything trains on a laptop CPU, the diffusion model in under an hour.

## How each module works

**Read** lessons open the book with notes underneath. **Build** lessons are small NumPy programs you run, with the real
output printed under every block. **Watch** lessons are optional videos. The **module project** turns the day's model
into a real project of your own. Each module ends with a quiz (pass with 70%), an optional task (publishing your project)
and a short explanation in your own words.
