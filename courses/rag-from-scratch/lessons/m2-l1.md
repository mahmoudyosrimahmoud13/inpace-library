# Chunking: how to cut documents

Search returns pieces of text, and the prompt can only hold a few. So documents are cut into **chunks** before
they're indexed. How you cut decides what can be found and what the model gets to read.

## Why not whole documents?

- **Long documents dilute.** A 6,000-character module overview mentions fifty things. It matches many questions a
  little and few questions well, and if it's retrieved it fills the prompt with text that's mostly off topic.
- **The prompt has a budget.** Five sources of 300 characters fit easily; five of 6,000 don't.

## Why not tiny chunks?

- A sentence on its own loses its subject. "It returns None if the file doesn't exist" — what returns None?
- The answer may need two sentences that end up in different chunks.

## Three ways to cut

| Way | How | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed size | Every 400 characters, with 80 repeated (overlap) | Simple, even sizes | Cuts mid-sentence and mid-list |
| By structure | At blank lines, headings or sections, glued up to a target size | Keeps ideas together | Sizes vary |
| Semantic | Where the meaning changes (embeddings of nearby sentences drift apart) | Clean topics | Slower, more parameters |

**Overlap** repeats the end of one chunk at the start of the next, so a sentence cut in two still appears whole in
one of them.

## Give every chunk its context

A chunk from the middle of a document no longer says what it's about. The simple fix is a **chunk header**: start
every chunk with its document's title (here, the function name and arguments). It costs a few characters and helps
both keyword and meaning search, because the most important words, the names, are now in every chunk.

## Measure two things

Recall tells you whether the right chunk is found. **Context size**, the characters in the top 5 chunks, tells you
what the prompt will cost and how much noise the model has to read past. Good chunking raises the first and lowers
the second.
