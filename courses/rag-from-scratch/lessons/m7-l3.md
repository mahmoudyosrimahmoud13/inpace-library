# Capstone: Ask Python, your way

You have every part of a RAG system, built and measured by you. The capstone makes it yours: either point it at
documents you care about, or take Ask Python to the next level.

The whole project folder is above this text.

## Choose one

**A. Your own documents.** Your team's wiki pages, a library's docs, your course notes, a product manual. Write
a `load_docs()` for them that returns the same fields (`id`, `module` or another category, `kind`, `title`, `text`),
then:

1. Write at least 25 test questions with their right documents, the way a real user would ask.
2. Run projects 1 to 4 on them and pick the chunking and retrieval that win **on your data**. They may not be the
   ones that won here.
3. Run project 6's checks on 15 questions with 3 out-of-scope ones.

**B. A better Ask Python.** Beat this course's numbers on the full 45 questions:

1. Raise recall@5 above 70% (a reranker, a "questions it answers" view, query rewriting with the model, better
   chunk headers).
2. Raise citations above 50% and keep all out-of-scope questions refused (a bigger model, a better format).

## Either way, hand in

- The repository, with a README that has: what it does, how to run it, your results table before and after, and
  what didn't work.
- One example answer with its sources, and one failure you understand.

## Looking back

- Where did your biggest improvement come from: search, prompt, or model?
- Which of your test questions would you never have thought of without reading the failures?
- What would you need before letting colleagues rely on it every day?

That last question is a good one for interviews: it shows you know a demo from a system.
