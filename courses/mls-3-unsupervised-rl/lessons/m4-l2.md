# Project 4: content-based filtering, and retrieve then rank

You'll recommend by genres, compare three recommenders on what people really loved, and build a two-stage
recommender.

The code is above this text. Save it as `p4_content.py`.

## Read the code

- Each movie's genres become a vector of length 1. A **person's profile** adds up the genre vectors of what they rated,
  weighted by how much more (or less) than usual they liked each one.
- **The test**: for each person with a held-out 5-star rating, is one of those movies in their top 10 (never
  counting movies they already rated)?
- Three recommenders: **most popular**, **content-based** (genre fit plus a little popularity), and **collaborative
  filtering** from project 3.
- **Retrieval, then ranking**: a cheap step keeps the 100 movies whose genres fit best; the expensive model ranks
  only those.

## Run it

```bash
python p4_content.py
```

The real output:

```text
1. 652 people have a held-out 5-star rating. How often is one of them in the top 10?
   most popular                36.8%
   content-based (genres)      15.2%
   collaborative filtering     33.1%

2. Retrieval, then ranking: for one person, keep the 100 movies whose genres fit best (cheap), then
   rank only those 100 with the collaborative model (expensive at scale).
   person 312: For the Moment (1994); Chasing Amy (1997); Bound (1996); Like Water For Chocolate (Como agua para chocolate) (1992); Affair to Remember, An (1957)
   scored 70 movies instead of 1,682: with millions of items, that's the difference that matters
```

What it shows:

- **"Most popular" is hard to beat** (36.8%): people who love movies often love the famous ones. Always include this
  baseline.
- **Collaborative filtering comes close** (33.1%). It was trained to predict ratings, not to rank a top 10. Systems
  trained directly for ranking do better.
- **Genres alone are weak** (15.2%): 19 flags say little about taste. Content-based shines where collaborative
  filtering can't: new movies with no ratings, and new users who tell you a few likes.
- **Two stages** scored 70 movies instead of 1,682 for this person. With millions of items, that's how recommenders
  stay fast.

## Make it yours

1. Add the release year (scaled) to each movie's vector. Does content-based improve?
2. Blend: rank by `0.7 x collaborative + 0.3 x popularity`. Can you beat 36.8%?

## What to take away

- Compare recommenders on a ranking task against popularity.
- Content-based solves cold start; retrieve-then-rank solves scale.
