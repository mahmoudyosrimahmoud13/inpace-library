# Two towers, retrieval and ranking

**Content-based filtering** uses features of users (`x_u`: age, country, past genres) and items (`x_m`: genre, year,
critic score):

```text
v_u = NN_u(x_u)        v_m = NN_m(x_m)        prediction = v_u · v_m   (or g(v_u · v_m) for clicks)
```

Two networks (a **two-tower** model) are trained together, with the cost on the rated pairs. Similar items: small
`‖v_m⁽ᵏ⁾ − v_m⁽ⁱ⁾‖²`, and **item vectors can be computed ahead of time**.

## Large catalogues: retrieval, then ranking

1. **Retrieval** (fast, broad): gather a few hundred candidates: similar items to recent views, top items in the
   user's favourite genres, popular in their country. Remove already seen.
2. **Ranking** (slower, accurate): score the candidates with the full model and sort.

More candidates means better results but slower; tune it with offline experiments. In industry, retrieval uses
**approximate nearest neighbour** search over embeddings (FAISS, ScaNN).

## Ethics

Recommenders optimize something: clicks, watch time, profit. That can amplify harmful or exploitative content,
create filter bubbles, or push ads from bad actors. Be transparent with users, and choose what you optimize carefully.
