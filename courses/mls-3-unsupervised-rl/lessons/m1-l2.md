# The k-means algorithm and its objective

**Clustering** groups unlabelled points that are similar: market segments, similar news, DNA patterns, astronomical
data.

## K-means

```text
pick K centroids μ₁..μ_K (randomly, from the training points)
repeat:
    # 1. assign
    for each point i: c⁽ⁱ⁾ = index of the closest centroid  (min ‖x⁽ⁱ⁾ − μ_k‖²)
    # 2. move
    for each k: μ_k = mean of the points assigned to k
```

(If a cluster gets no points, drop it or re-initialize it.)

## The cost it minimizes (distortion)

```text
J(c, μ) = (1/m) Σᵢ ‖x⁽ⁱ⁾ − μ_c⁽ⁱ⁾‖²
```

Step 1 minimizes `J` over the assignments, step 2 over the centroids, so `J` **never goes up**. If it does, there's a
bug. It converges, but to a **local** minimum.

## Initialization and choosing K

- Run k-means 50 to 1,000 times with different random starts; keep the lowest `J`. (k-means++ picks spread-out
  starts and is the scikit-learn default.)
- **Choosing K**: the elbow method is often unclear. Better: choose by the **downstream purpose** (T-shirt sizes S/M/L
  vs XS-XL: what does the business need?).

## Going further

- K-means assumes round, similar-size clusters and needs **scaled** features. For odd shapes use DBSCAN; for soft
  assignments, Gaussian mixture models.
- Silhouette score is a common way to compare K when there's no downstream measure.
