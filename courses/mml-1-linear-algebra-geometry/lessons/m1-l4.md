# Read: systems of linear equations (2.1)

Section 2.1 motivates the whole chapter with one question: **does a system of linear equations have no solution,
exactly one, or infinitely many?**

![Three systems of two equations: one crossing, two parallel lines, the same line twice](systems.png)

**Key ideas**

- Each equation in two unknowns is a **line**; a solution must lie on all lines at once. In three unknowns each
  equation is a **plane**.
- Write the system compactly as **Ax = b**. The matrix A holds the coefficients, x the unknowns, b the right-hand side.
- The book's "columns view" is the important one: Ax = b asks **which combination of the columns of A gives b**:
  x₁·(column 1) + x₂·(column 2) + … = b. Hold on to this; it's the idea behind span, rank and least squares.

**Do on paper:** the book's example with three equations where adding two of them contradicts the third (no
solution), and the one with a free variable (infinitely many solutions).
