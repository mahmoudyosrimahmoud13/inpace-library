# Read: norms (3.1) and inner products (3.2)

Chapter 3 adds **geometry** to vector spaces: length, distance and angle.

![The unit "circle" for three norms](norms.png)

**Norms (3.1)**: a norm ‖x‖ is a length, with three rules: it's 0 only for 0, scales (‖λx‖ = |λ|‖x‖), and obeys the
**triangle inequality**. The ℓ₁ (Manhattan) and ℓ₂ (Euclidean) norms come up all the time: ℓ₂ in least squares and
weight decay, ℓ₁ in lasso and sparse models. The picture shows why they differ: the ℓ₁ "circle" has corners on the axes.

**Inner products (3.2)**: the dot product xᵀy is one inner product, but not the only one. Any **symmetric,
positive definite** bilinear form works. In ℝⁿ every inner product looks like **⟨x, y⟩ = xᵀAy** for a symmetric
positive definite (SPD) matrix A. Remember the test for SPD: xᵀAx > 0 for every x ≠ 0.

**Why bother with other inner products?** Different inner products give different notions of length and angle. In
course 3 you'll see the Mahalanobis distance of a Gaussian, which is exactly this with A = Σ⁻¹.
