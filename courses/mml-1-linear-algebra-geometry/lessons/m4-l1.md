# Read: linear mappings and matrix representations (2.7, first half)

Section 2.7 is long. Split it into two sittings; this lesson covers up to and including the **transformation matrix**.

**Key ideas**

- A **linear mapping** Φ keeps addition and scaling: Φ(x + y) = Φ(x) + Φ(y) and Φ(λx) = λΦ(x).
- Injective (one-to-one), surjective (onto), bijective; **isomorphism** = bijective linear map. Any two vector spaces
  of the same finite dimension are isomorphic: ℝ³ and polynomials of degree ≤ 2 are "the same" space.
- Once you choose a basis in each space, a linear map **is** a matrix: the **transformation matrix** A_Φ. Column j holds
  the coordinates of Φ(b_j), the image of the j-th basis vector. That's exactly 3Blue1Brown's "where do the basis
  vectors land".
- **Coordinates** depend on the basis: the same vector has different coordinates in different bases.
