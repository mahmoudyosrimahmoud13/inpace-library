# Read: lengths, distances (3.3) and angles (3.4)

- Every inner product gives a norm: ‖x‖ = √⟨x, x⟩, and a distance d(x, y) = ‖x − y‖.
- **Cauchy-Schwarz**: |⟨x, y⟩| ≤ ‖x‖‖y‖. That's what makes the next definition legal.
- **Angle**: cos ω = ⟨x, y⟩ / (‖x‖‖y‖). It always lands in [−1, 1] thanks to Cauchy-Schwarz.
- **Orthogonal**: ⟨x, y⟩ = 0. Important subtlety from the book's example: (1, 1) and (−1, 1) are orthogonal with the dot
  product but **not** with another inner product. Orthogonality depends on which inner product you use.

**Machine learning link:** cosine similarity (for text and embeddings) is the cosine of the angle between vectors,
which ignores their lengths.
