# Read: orthogonal projections (3.8)

Section 3.8 is one of the most used ideas in the whole book: **least squares, PCA and Gaussian conditioning are all
projections.** Take your time.

![Projection of x onto the line through b](projection.png)

**Onto a line (3.8.1)**: three steps you can redo for any case.

1. The projection is a multiple of b: π(x) = λb.
2. The error x − π(x) must be orthogonal to b: ⟨x − λb, b⟩ = 0.
3. Solve for λ: λ = bᵀx / bᵀb. The projection matrix is P = bbᵀ / bᵀb (rank 1, and P² = P).

**Onto a subspace spanned by the columns of B (3.8.2)**: same three steps with a vector λ:
Bᵀ(x − Bλ) = 0 gives the **normal equations** BᵀBλ = Bᵀx, so λ = (BᵀB)⁻¹Bᵀx and P = B(BᵀB)⁻¹Bᵀ.
(BᵀB)⁻¹Bᵀ is the **pseudo-inverse**. With an orthonormal basis, BᵀB = I and everything simplifies to P = BBᵀ.

**Gram-Schmidt (3.8.3)**: subtract from each new vector its projection onto the ones before.

**Affine subspaces (3.8.4)**: shift so the subspace goes through 0, project, shift back.

**Remark to notice:** when Ax = b has no solution, projecting b onto the column space of A gives the closest solvable
system. That's least squares, chapter 9.
