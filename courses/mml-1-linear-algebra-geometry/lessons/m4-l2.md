# Read: change of basis, image and kernel (2.7, second half)

**Change of basis (2.7.2)** is the part people find hardest. The formula Ã = T⁻¹AS says: to apply the map in the new
bases, translate new coordinates to old (S), apply A, translate back to the new output coordinates (T⁻¹).

- When the map goes from a space to itself and you use the same basis change S on both sides: Ã = S⁻¹AS. Matrices
  related like this are **similar**: same map, different coordinates. In course 2, eigendecomposition picks the basis
  that makes Ã diagonal.
- **Equivalent** matrices (Ã = T⁻¹AS with different S and T) also describe one map.

**Image and kernel (2.7.3)**: the **image** (range, column space) is everything Φ can produce; the **kernel**
(null space) is everything sent to 0. The **rank-nullity theorem** ties them together:
dim(ker Φ) + dim(Im Φ) = dim(V).
