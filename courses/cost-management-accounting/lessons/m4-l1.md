# Absorption costing and variable costing

Two ways to put a cost on inventory, and they can give **different profits** for the same month.

- **Absorption costing** (required for published statements and tax): product cost includes **fixed manufacturing overhead**. Some fixed cost sits in inventory until the goods are sold.
- **Variable costing** (for internal decisions): product cost includes **only variable manufacturing costs**. All fixed costs are expensed in the month.

## Nour's month

- Made **500** cakes, sold **450** (50 left in the freezer for next month's orders)
- Price 120; variable manufacturing cost 45 per cake (ingredients 35, box 5, baker's piece pay 5); variable selling (delivery) 5 per cake
- Fixed manufacturing overhead 10,000 (kitchen rent, oven, head baker); fixed selling and admin 11,000

Under absorption, fixed overhead per cake = 10,000 ÷ 500 made = **20**, so product cost = 45 + 20 = **65**.

**Variable costing**

| | |
|---|---|
| Sales (450 × 120) | 54,000 |
| Variable cost of goods sold (450 × 45) | (20,250) |
| Variable selling (450 × 5) | (2,250) |
| **Contribution margin** | **31,500** |
| Fixed manufacturing overhead | (10,000) |
| Fixed selling and admin | (11,000) |
| **Operating profit** | **10,500** |

**Absorption costing**

| | |
|---|---|
| Sales | 54,000 |
| Cost of goods sold (450 × 65) | (29,250) |
| **Gross profit** | **24,750** |
| Selling and admin (2,250 + 11,000) | (13,250) |
| **Operating profit** | **11,500** |

## Why 1,000 more?

Under absorption, the 50 unsold cakes carry 50 × 20 = **1,000** of fixed overhead into inventory. That cost will hit next month, when they're sold. Under variable costing, all 10,000 was expensed now.

| If... | Then |
|---|---|
| Production > sales (inventory grows) | Absorption profit **higher** |
| Production < sales (inventory falls) | Absorption profit **lower** |
| Production = sales | Same profit |

> **Difference = Change in inventory units × Fixed overhead per unit** = 50 × 20 = 1,000

## Why managers care

Absorption costing lets a manager **raise profit just by producing more** than can be sold: fixed overhead hides in the warehouse. That's why internal reports often use variable costing, which shows profit moving only with **sales**, and fits CVP and decisions.

## Try it

Fixed overhead 24,000; produced 8,000 units; sold 7,000. Variable costing profit is 40,000. What's absorption costing profit?

**Answer:** fixed overhead per unit 3; inventory grew by 1,000 units; absorption profit = 40,000 + 3,000 = **43,000**.
