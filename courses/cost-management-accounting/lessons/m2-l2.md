# Target profit, safety margin and operating leverage

## Selling for a target profit

> **Units for target profit = (Fixed costs + Target profit) ÷ CM per unit**

Nour wants 7,000 profit a month: (21,000 + 7,000) ÷ 70 = **400 cakes**.

After tax: if tax is 20% and she wants 8,000 **after** tax, the pre-tax target is 8,000 ÷ 0.8 = 10,000, so (21,000 + 10,000) ÷ 70 = **443 cakes**.

## Margin of safety

How far can sales fall before losses start?

> **Margin of safety = Actual sales − Break-even sales**

At 450 cakes: 450 − 300 = **150 cakes** (18,000 of sales), or 150 ÷ 450 = **33%**. Sales could drop a third before she loses money.

## Operating leverage

> **Degree of operating leverage = Contribution margin ÷ Operating profit**

At 450 cakes: 31,500 ÷ 10,500 = **3.0**.

Meaning: a 10% rise in sales gives a **30%** rise in profit. Check: 495 cakes → CM 34,650 − 21,000 = 13,650, up 30% from 10,500. ✓

It works both ways: a 10% fall in sales cuts profit by 30%. Businesses with high fixed costs (airlines, factories, software) have high leverage: great in good years, painful in bad ones.

## Several products: the sales mix

Nour also sells boxes of cookies at 40 (variable cost 20, CM 20). Usually she sells **3 cookie boxes for every cake**.

A "bundle" of 1 cake + 3 cookie boxes has CM = 70 + 3 × 20 = **130**.

Break-even bundles = 21,000 ÷ 130 = **161.5**, so about **162 cakes and 485 cookie boxes**.

If the mix shifts toward cookies (lower CM), break-even rises. Selling more of the high-CM product lowers it.

## The assumptions behind CVP

CVP is simple because it assumes: price and variable cost per unit stay constant, fixed costs stay fixed in the range, the sales mix is constant, and what's produced is sold. Real life bends these, so treat CVP as a quick, strong first estimate.

## Try it

Price 80, variable cost 50, fixed costs 30,000. (a) Units for a 15,000 profit? (b) At 1,800 units, what's the margin of safety (%) and operating leverage?

**Answers:** (a) (30,000 + 15,000) ÷ 30 = **1,500 units**. (b) break-even 1,000; margin of safety 800 ÷ 1,800 = **44%**; CM 54,000, profit 24,000, leverage **2.25**.
