# Start here: costs from the inside

Financial accounting (the first two courses) reports **to outsiders**: owners, banks, the tax authority. **Management accounting** serves the people **running** the business. It answers questions like:

- How much does one cake really cost us?
- How many must we sell to stop losing money?
- Should we accept a big order at a lower price?
- What will our cash look like in three months?
- Which branch is doing well, and why?

| | Financial accounting | Management accounting |
|---|---|---|
| For | Outsiders | Managers |
| Rules | IFRS, local standards | Whatever is useful |
| Looks | Mostly backward | Mostly forward |
| Detail | Whole company | Products, branches, customers |
| How often | Quarterly, yearly | Weekly, daily, whenever needed |

## Nour's bakery, one year later

Nour now runs a busier bakery. She sells cakes at **120** each. Her monthly numbers:

| Cost | Amount | Changes with the number of cakes? |
|---|---|---|
| Ingredients | 35 per cake | Yes |
| Box | 5 per cake | Yes |
| Delivery | 10 per cake | Yes |
| Rent | 6,000 a month | No |
| Salaries | 12,000 a month | No |
| Oven depreciation | 1,000 a month | No |
| Other (insurance, phone, cleaning) | 2,000 a month | No |

We'll use these numbers all course.

## The cost words you need

**By behavior** (how the cost reacts when activity changes):
- **Variable costs** change in total with activity: ingredients, boxes, delivery. Per cake they stay the same (50).
- **Fixed costs** stay the same in total within a normal range: rent, salaries. Per cake they **fall** as volume rises: 21,000 ÷ 300 cakes = 70 each, but 21,000 ÷ 500 = 42 each.
- **Mixed costs** have both parts: electricity (a fixed monthly charge plus a part that grows with oven use). Next lesson splits them.

**By traceability:**
- **Direct costs** can be traced to one product easily: the flour in a cake.
- **Indirect costs (overhead)** are shared: rent, the supervisor's salary, oven depreciation.

**By timing in the statements:**
- **Product costs** are part of making the goods (materials, production labor, factory overhead). They sit in inventory until sold, then become cost of goods sold.
- **Period costs** are expensed when incurred: selling and administrative costs (marketing, the accountant's salary, office rent).

**For decisions:**
- **Relevant costs** differ between the options you're choosing between. **Sunk costs** are already spent and can't be changed: they never matter for a decision.
- **Opportunity cost:** what you give up by choosing one option. If the oven bakes wedding cakes, it can't bake bread at the same time.

## Try it

Classify each for Nour as variable or fixed, and direct or indirect to a cake: flour; the shop manager's salary; cake boxes; insurance; the decorator paid per cake.

**Answers:** flour: variable, direct. Manager: fixed, indirect. Boxes: variable, direct. Insurance: fixed, indirect. Decorator per cake: variable, direct.
