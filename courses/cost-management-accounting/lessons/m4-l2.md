# Relevant costs: making decisions

For any decision, only **relevant costs and revenues** matter: future amounts that **differ** between the options. Ignore **sunk costs** (already spent) and costs that stay the same either way.

## 1. A special order

A hotel wants **100 cakes at 70 each** for an event next month. Normal price is 120. Nour has spare oven time. The hotel collects the cakes, so no delivery (variable cost 40 instead of 50).

| | |
|---|---|
| Extra revenue (100 × 70) | 7,000 |
| Extra variable cost (100 × 40) | (4,000) |
| **Extra profit** | **3,000** |

Fixed costs don't change, so they're irrelevant. **Accept**, as long as: there's spare capacity (otherwise count the profit lost on normal cakes), and regular customers won't demand the same low price.

## 2. Make or buy

Nour makes sugar decorations for 200 cakes a month: variable cost 8 each, plus a part-time decorator costing 500 a month who would leave if she stopped. A supplier offers them at **9** each.

| | Make | Buy |
|---|---|---|
| Variable (200 × 8) | 1,600 | |
| Avoidable fixed (decorator) | 500 | |
| Purchase (200 × 9) | | 1,800 |
| **Total** | **2,100** | **1,800** |

**Buy**: it saves 300 a month. Shared costs (rent, oven) that stay anyway are not relevant. Also weigh quality and reliability.

## 3. Drop a product line?

The cookie line's report: sales 6,000; variable costs 4,000; fixed costs allocated 3,000. "Loss" of 1,000. Should she drop it?

Of the 3,000 fixed, only **1,000** is avoidable (a part-time packer); 2,000 is a share of rent and salaries that stays.

| If dropped | |
|---|---|
| Lost contribution margin | (2,000) |
| Fixed cost saved | 1,000 |
| **Change in profit** | **−1,000** |

**Keep it**: dropping cookies makes the bakery 1,000 *worse* off. Allocated costs that don't disappear can make a good product look like a loser.

## 4. A scarce resource

Oven time is limited to 200 hours a month. A cake (CM 70) needs 0.5 oven hours; a fruit pie (CM 40) needs 0.2 hours.

| | Cake | Pie |
|---|---|---|
| CM per unit | 70 | 40 |
| Oven hours per unit | 0.5 | 0.2 |
| **CM per oven hour** | **140** | **200** |

When oven time is the limit, make pies first (up to demand), then cakes. **Rank by CM per unit of the scarce resource**, not by CM per unit.

## The checklist for any decision

1. List the options.
2. Keep only future costs and revenues that differ.
3. Include opportunity costs.
4. Compare, then weigh what numbers miss (quality, customers, staff, risk).

## Try it

A machine bought last year for 50,000 (sunk) can be upgraded for 12,000 to save 5,000 a year for 3 years. Ignoring interest, should you upgrade? What role does the 50,000 play?

**Answer:** savings 15,000 > 12,000, so **upgrade** (gain 3,000). The 50,000 is sunk: it plays no role.
