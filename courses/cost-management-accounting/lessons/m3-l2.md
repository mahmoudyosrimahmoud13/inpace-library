# Process costing and activity-based costing

## Process costing

When identical units flow through steps (bread, cement, drinks, paint), you don't cost each loaf. You cost each **process** for a period and divide by the units: **process costing**.

The trick is units that are half-done at the end of the period: **equivalent units**.

### Example: the dough department, one month

- 10,000 loaves of dough completed and moved to baking
- 2,000 still in progress at month end, **50% complete** for labor and overhead (conversion)
- Materials (flour, water) are all added **at the start**
- Costs this month: materials 24,000; conversion (labor + overhead) 33,000

| | Materials | Conversion |
|---|---|---|
| Completed units | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| Ending work in progress | 2,000 × 100% = 2,000 | 2,000 × 50% = 1,000 |
| **Equivalent units** | **12,000** | **11,000** |
| Costs | 24,000 | 33,000 |
| **Cost per equivalent unit** | **2.00** | **3.00** |

- Cost of completed units: 10,000 × (2 + 3) = **50,000**
- Ending work in progress: 2,000 × 2 + 1,000 × 3 = **7,000**
- Check: 50,000 + 7,000 = 57,000 = 24,000 + 33,000 ✓

(Opening work in progress adds a step: the *weighted average* or *FIFO* method. The idea is the same.)

## Activity-based costing (ABC)

Traditional costing spreads overhead with one base (like labor hours). If products use overhead very differently, that misleads.

Nour makes **standard cakes** (big batches, simple) and **custom cakes** (small batches, many setups and design changes). Kitchen overhead: 18,000.

**Traditional, by labor hours** (standard 900 hours, custom 300 hours of 1,200):

| | Standard | Custom |
|---|---|---|
| Overhead | 18,000 × 900/1,200 = **13,500** | **4,500** |

**ABC, by what drives the cost:**

| Activity | Cost | Driver | Standard | Custom |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Setups (cleaning, changing molds, design) | 8,000 | 100 setups | 20 → 1,600 | 80 → 6,400 |
| Baking | 10,000 | 1,000 oven hours | 700 → 7,000 | 300 → 3,000 |
| **Total** | **18,000** | | **8,600** | **9,400** |

Under the traditional method, custom cakes got only 4,500 of overhead; ABC says 9,400. Custom cakes were **undercosted** (and probably underpriced), standard cakes overcosted.

ABC costs more to run, so it's worth it when overhead is large and products differ a lot.

## Try it

A mixing department: 8,000 units completed; 1,000 units in ending WIP, 40% complete for conversion; materials added at the start. Materials 18,000; conversion 25,200. Find the cost of completed units and of ending WIP.

**Answer:** EU materials 9,000 → 2.00; EU conversion 8,400 → 3.00. Completed 8,000 × 5 = **40,000**. WIP = 1,000 × 2 + 400 × 3 = **3,200**.
