# Job costing: the cost of one order

When every order is different (a wedding cake, a printed book, a house, a consulting project), each one is costed separately: **job costing**.

A job's cost has three parts:

1. **Direct materials:** traced from requisitions (what was taken from the store for this job).
2. **Direct labor:** traced from time sheets (hours on this job × wage rate).
3. **Manufacturing overhead:** shared costs (rent, oven, supervisor), **applied** with a rate, because you can't trace them.

## The predetermined overhead rate

At the start of the year, estimate:

> **Overhead rate = Budgeted overhead ÷ Budgeted activity**

Nour budgets 18,000 of kitchen overhead for the period and 1,200 direct labor hours:

> 18,000 ÷ 1,200 = **15 per labor hour**

Why budgeted, not actual? Because you need to price jobs **during** the year, and actual overhead is only known at the end. Also, a monthly actual rate would swing with seasons.

## Costing a wedding cake

| | |
|---|---|
| Direct materials (from the requisition) | 900 |
| Direct labor: 10 hours × 40 | 400 |
| Overhead applied: 10 hours × 15 | 150 |
| **Total job cost** | **1,450** |

With a 40% markup on cost, the quote is 1,450 × 1.4 = **2,030**.

The **job cost sheet** collects these numbers for each order. It's the basis for pricing and for knowing which kinds of orders really make money.

## Over- and underapplied overhead

At year end, compare overhead **applied** to jobs with overhead **actually** incurred:

- Applied 18,600 (1,240 hours × 15), actual 18,000: **overapplied by 600** (jobs were charged too much).
- Applied less than actual: **underapplied**.

Small differences are usually closed to cost of goods sold. Large ones mean the rate needs fixing next year.

## Choosing the activity base

The base should **drive** overhead. Labor hours suit a labor-heavy kitchen; machine hours suit an automated factory. A bad base distorts costs: next lesson shows activity-based costing, which fixes that.

## Try it

Budgeted overhead 60,000; budgeted machine hours 4,000. A job uses materials 2,500, labor 30 hours at 50 and 20 machine hours. What's the job cost? With a 25% markup, the price?

**Answer:** rate 15 per machine hour. Cost = 2,500 + 1,500 + 300 = **4,300**. Price **5,375**.
