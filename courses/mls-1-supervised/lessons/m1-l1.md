About 1 to 1.5 hours a day. This is a **refresh at an advanced level**: each lesson opens the original slides for
that part, then the notes go further, the way interviews do: why the maths is what it is, what breaks in practice,
and how to say it clearly.

This is part 1 of 3. Part 2 covers neural networks, advice for applying ML, and decision trees. Part 3 covers
clustering, anomaly detection, recommenders and reinforcement learning.

**Credit:** the slides are from the *Machine Learning Specialization* by DeepLearning.AI and Stanford Online, taught
by Andrew Ng, shared under Creative Commons BY-SA 2.0 for non-commercial educational use. Take the full course on
Coursera for the videos and labs.

Maths is written in plain text: `w·x` is a dot product, `Σ` a sum over examples `i = 1..m`, `^` a power.
