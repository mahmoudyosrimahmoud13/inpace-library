# Supervised and unsupervised learning

Arthur Samuel's definition: *the field of study that gives computers the ability to learn without being explicitly
programmed*. In practice: **learn a mapping from data, then use it on new data**.

## Supervised learning: learn from right answers

You have inputs `x` and labelled outputs `y`. The model learns `x → y`.

| Kind | Output | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Regression** | a number, from infinitely many | house price, minutes to finish a course |
| **Classification** | a category, from a small set | spam or not, tumour benign or malignant, which digit |

## Unsupervised learning: find structure without labels

| Kind | Does | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Clustering** | groups similar points | news stories on the same topic, customer segments |
| **Anomaly detection** | finds unusual points | fraud, a failing machine |
| **Dimensionality reduction** | compresses data, keeping what matters | visualizing, speeding up training |

## Going further (interview level)

- **Self-supervised** learning (how large language models are pre-trained) makes labels from the data itself: hide a
  word, predict it.
- **Semi-supervised**: a few labels plus lots of unlabelled data.
- **Reinforcement learning** (part 3): learn from rewards, not labels.

> **Interview:** "Is predicting tomorrow's temperature classification or regression? And whether it rains?"
> Regression; classification. "Can you turn a regression problem into a classification one?" Yes, by bucketing the
> output (cold/mild/hot), losing detail but sometimes making the problem easier or more useful.
