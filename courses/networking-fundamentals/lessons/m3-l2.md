# Subnetting, step by step

Subnetting = splitting a network into smaller ones. You'll do it whenever you plan a cloud VPC, an office, or read a firewall rule.

## The seven answers for any address and prefix

For `172.16.35.123/20`:

1. **Prefix**: /20, so the mask is `255.255.240.0`.
2. **Interesting byte**: the one where the mask isn't 255 or 0. Here the 3rd (240).
3. **Block size**: 256 − 240 = **16**. Networks in that byte go 0, 16, 32, 48...
4. **Network address**: 35 falls in the block starting at 32: `172.16.32.0`.
5. **Broadcast**: one less than the next block (48): `172.16.47.255`.
6. **First and last host**: `172.16.32.1` to `172.16.47.254`.
7. **Host count**: 2^(32−20) − 2 = **4094**.

## Splitting a network

Split `10.0.0.0/16` into subnets of /24:

- Each /24 has 256 addresses; a /16 holds 2^(24−16) = **256** of them: `10.0.0.0/24`, `10.0.1.0/24`, ... `10.0.255.0/24`.

Plan a cloud VPC `10.0.0.0/16` in a way you'll thank yourself for later:

| Subnet | CIDR | Use |
|---|---|---|
| public-a | 10.0.0.0/24 | Load balancers, zone A |
| public-b | 10.0.1.0/24 | Load balancers, zone B |
| app-a | 10.0.10.0/24 | App servers, zone A |
| app-b | 10.0.11.0/24 | App servers, zone B |
| db-a | 10.0.20.0/24 | Databases, zone A |
| db-b | 10.0.21.0/24 | Databases, zone B |

Leave gaps for growth, and never overlap ranges with networks you may connect to later (office VPN, other VPCs): overlapping ranges can't be routed together.

## Practise

Do ten a day for a week and it becomes automatic. Websites like subnettingpractice.com generate endless questions. Check yourself with `ipcalc`.
