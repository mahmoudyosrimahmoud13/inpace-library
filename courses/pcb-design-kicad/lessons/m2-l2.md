# Footprints, parts and the BOM

The schematic says what connects. Now decide what each part physically **is**.

## Through-hole or surface mount?

| | Through-hole (THT) | Surface mount (SMD) |
|---|---|---|
| Hand soldering | easy | fine from 0805 size up; harder for tiny parts |
| Board space | large | small |
| Price in quantity | higher | lower |
| Factory assembly | less common | standard; the factory can solder everything for you |

For a first board, mixing is fine: an SMD resistor size you can still handle (**0805** = 2.0 × 1.25 mm, or **1206**), and through-hole for connectors and the electrolytic capacitor.

Common SMD size names:

```text
code   size (mm)     hand-soldering
1206   3.2 x 1.6     easy
0805   2.0 x 1.25    comfortable with tweezers
0603   1.6 x 0.8     needs practice
0402   1.0 x 0.5     hard; leave to the factory
```

Chips also come in packages: **DIP-8** (through-hole, breadboard friendly), **SOIC-8** (small outline, 1.27 mm pin spacing, easy SMD), and much smaller ones like QFN.

## Assigning footprints

Open **Tools → Assign Footprints**. For each part, pick a footprint from the right-hand list (use the filter buttons and search):

| Part | Footprint (KiCad library name) |
|---|---|
| R1, R2, R3 | `Resistor_SMD:R_0805_2012Metric` |
| C2, C3 | `Capacitor_SMD:C_0805_2012Metric` |
| C1 (10 µF electrolytic) | `Capacitor_THT:CP_Radial_D5.0mm_P2.00mm` |
| LED | `LED_SMD:LED_0805_2012Metric` or `LED_THT:LED_D5.0mm` |
| 555 | `Package_DIP:DIP-8_W7.62mm` (or `Package_SO:SOIC-8_3.9x4.9mm_P1.27mm`) |
| Power connector | `Connector_PinHeader_2.54mm:PinHeader_1x02_P2.54mm_Vertical` |

The footprint viewer (the icon at the top) shows the pads and a 3D preview, so you can check it matches the real part.

## Checking a footprint against the datasheet

Before trusting any footprint, compare it with the **package drawing** in the part's datasheet:

1. **Pin 1 position and numbering** (the most common error, and it ruins a board).
2. **Pad spacing (pitch)**: 2.54 mm for DIP, 1.27 mm for SOIC.
3. **Hole diameter** for THT parts: the lead diameter plus about 0.2 to 0.4 mm.
4. For transistors and regulators: the **pin order** (E-B-C, or IN-GND-OUT) differs between models; match the symbol's pin numbers to the footprint's.

## The BOM (bill of materials)

The **BOM** lists every part to buy: reference, value, footprint, quantity, and a manufacturer or supplier part number. In the Schematic Editor: **Tools → Generate Bill of Materials**.

A simple BOM, and its cost:

```python
bom = [
    ("R1", "1k 0805", 1, 0.01), ("R2", "68k 0805", 1, 0.01), ("R3", "470 0805", 1, 0.01),
    ("C1", "10uF 16V radial", 1, 0.05), ("C2", "10nF 0805", 1, 0.01), ("C3", "100nF 0805", 1, 0.01),
    ("D1", "red LED 0805", 1, 0.03), ("U1", "NE555 DIP-8", 1, 0.25), ("J1", "2-pin header", 1, 0.05),
]
per_board = sum(qty * price for _, _, qty, price in bom)
print(f"{len(bom)} lines, parts per board about ${per_board:.2f}")
print(f"for 10 boards: ${per_board * 10:.2f} (buy a few spares of the small parts)")
```

```output
9 lines, parts per board about $0.43
for 10 boards: $4.30 (buy a few spares of the small parts)
```

Add a field like **LCSC** or **MPN** (manufacturer part number) to each symbol (press E, add a field). Factories that assemble boards use it to pick the exact part.

## Summary

- Choose THT or SMD per part; 0805 is a good hand-soldering size; know DIP vs SOIC.
- Assign footprints and check pin 1, pitch, hole size and pin order against the datasheet.
- Generate a BOM with part numbers; it's both your shopping list and the factory's assembly list.
