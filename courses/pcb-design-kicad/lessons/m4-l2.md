# Manufacturing files and ordering

## Gerber files

**Gerber** is the standard file format PCB factories read: one file per layer (top copper, bottom copper, solder masks, silkscreens, board outline), plus a **drill file** listing every hole.

In the PCB Editor: **File → Fabrication Outputs → Gerbers**.

1. Choose an output folder, for example `gerbers/`.
2. Include the layers: F.Cu, B.Cu, F.Paste, B.Paste, F.Silkscreen, B.Silkscreen, F.Mask, B.Mask, Edge.Cuts.
3. Click **Plot**.
4. Click **Generate Drill Files**, keep the defaults (Excellon format, millimetres), and generate.
5. Zip the folder.

Many factories publish exact KiCad export settings; follow theirs if they differ.

## Check the files before sending

Open the zip in KiCad's **Gerber Viewer** (or the factory's online viewer after uploading). Look at each layer separately:

- Is the outline closed and the right size?
- Are all holes there, the right size?
- Is any text mirrored or missing?

What the viewer shows is what the factory will make.

## Ordering

Upload the zip to a PCB manufacturer. Popular online services for hobby and small professional orders include JLCPCB, PCBWay, Aisler, OSH Park and Eurocircuits. You'll choose:

| Option | Usual choice for a first board |
|---|---|
| Layers | 2 |
| Thickness | 1.6 mm |
| Copper | 1 oz |
| Solder mask colour | green (cheapest and fastest; other colours cost little more) |
| Surface finish | HASL (lead-free) is cheap and easy to solder; ENIG (gold) is flatter, good for fine-pitch parts |
| Quantity | 5 or 10 |

```python
boards = 10
board_price, shipping = 5.0, 15.0          # example numbers; check current prices
cost_each = (board_price + shipping) / boards
print(f"{boards} boards: about ${board_price + shipping:.0f} total, ${cost_each:.2f} each with shipping")
```

```output
10 boards: about $20 total, $2.00 each with shipping
```

Shipping (and customs, depending on your country) is often more than the boards themselves, so order a few designs together when you can.

## Assembly by the factory (optional)

Many factories can also **solder the parts** for you (PCBA). You upload:

- the **BOM** with the factory's part numbers (for example LCSC numbers for JLCPCB)
- the **position file** (CPL / "pick and place"): File → Fabrication Outputs → Component Placement

They check part availability, show a preview with parts placed, and solder everything. Great for tiny SMD parts; for a first board, hand-soldering 0805 parts is also very doable (next module).

## Keep a record

In your project folder (and in git):

```text
led-flasher/
  led-flasher.kicad_*     the design
  gerbers/v1.0/           exactly what you sent
  bom-v1.0.csv
  notes.md                order date, factory, options, what to fix in v1.1
```

When the boards arrive and something is wrong, you'll know exactly which files made them.

## Summary

- Export Gerbers (one per layer) plus drill files, zip them, and check them in a Gerber viewer.
- Standard first order: 2 layers, 1.6 mm, 1 oz, HASL, 5 to 10 boards; shipping often costs more than boards.
- Factories can also assemble boards from your BOM and position file; keep every version's files.
