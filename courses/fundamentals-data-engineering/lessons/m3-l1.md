# Read: Chapter 3, Designing Good Data Architecture

A long chapter with a lot of vocabulary. Give it time; you'll hear these words in every data team.

## What is data architecture?

Read how the authors build their definition, from enterprise architecture down. Their definition of data
architecture: the design of systems that support the **evolving** data needs of a company, reached through **flexible
and reversible decisions** made by carefully weighing trade-offs. Underline "evolving" and "reversible": the chapter
keeps coming back to them. Also note the difference between **operational** architecture (what needs to happen) and
**technical** architecture (how it happens).

## The nine principles of good data architecture

This is the heart of the chapter. For each principle, write one sentence in your own words:

1. Choose common components wisely.
2. Plan for failure: read the four terms **availability, reliability, recovery time objective (RTO)** and **recovery
   point objective (RPO)**.
3. Architect for scalability: scale up *and* down.
4. Architecture is leadership.
5. Always be architecting.
6. Build loosely coupled systems: read the **Bezos API mandate** story.
7. Make reversible decisions: one-way and **two-way doors**.
8. Prioritize security: hardened perimeter versus zero trust, and the shared responsibility model.
9. Embrace FinOps: in the cloud, cost is a design input.

## Major architecture concepts

Domains and services; distributed systems (horizontal scaling, a leader node handing work to worker nodes); **tight versus loose
coupling** (tiers, monoliths, microservices); single versus multitenant; **event-driven architecture**; brownfield
versus greenfield, and the **strangler pattern** for replacing old systems piece by piece.

## Examples and types of data architecture

Read each with one question in mind, *what problem was this invented to solve?*

- **Data warehouse** and the **data lake**, then their convergence (the lakehouse, data platforms).
- The **modern data stack**: cloud, modular, plug-and-play.
- **Lambda** (separate batch and streaming paths) and **Kappa** (one streaming backbone for everything).
- The **Dataflow model**: batch as a special case of streaming.
- IoT architectures (skim if you don't work with devices).
- **Data mesh**: four ideas, domain ownership, data as a product, a self-serve platform, federated governance.

Skim *Other Data Architecture Examples* and read *Who's Involved*.

## After reading

Think of a system you know. Find one tight coupling in it (a part that breaks when another part breaks) and one
decision in it that would be a one-way door.

## What to take away

- Good architecture supports changing needs through reversible decisions.
- Loose coupling and planning for failure are what make systems survive real life.
- Every architecture type answers a specific problem; learn the problem, not just the name.
