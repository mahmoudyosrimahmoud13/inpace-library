# Read: Chapter 4, Choosing Technologies

The authors' first line sets the tone: architecture is the *what* and *why*; tools are the *how*. Choose the
architecture first, then the tools that fit it. Read the whole chapter. The next lesson opens Appendix B.

## Chapter 4: the factors

Read each section asking, "how would this change the tool I pick for Inkwell?"

- **Team size and capabilities**: a small team should lean on managed services and avoid "cargo-cult" copying of
  what big tech companies do.
- **Speed to market**: deliver value early; avoid "undifferentiated heavy lifting".
- **Interoperability**: how well a tool connects to the rest of your stack.
- **Cost optimization and business value**: read the three ideas carefully, **total cost of ownership (TCO)**, with
  direct and indirect costs and capex versus opex; **total opportunity cost of ownership (TOCO)**, what you can't do
  because of what you chose; and **FinOps**.
- **Today versus the future**: **immutable** technologies (object storage, networking, SQL, bash...) versus
  **transitory** ones that come and go. Note the **Lindy effect** and the advice to re-evaluate tools about every two
  years.
- **Location**: on premises, cloud, hybrid, multicloud. Read the section on cloud economics and the arguments about
  **cloud repatriation**: when does moving back from the cloud make sense, and for whom?
- **Build versus buy**: community-managed open source, commercial open source, proprietary walled gardens. Note the
  questions to ask about any open source project (mindshare, maturity, troubleshooting, roadmap...).
- **Monolith versus modular**, and the **distributed monolith** anti-pattern.
- **Serverless versus servers**: and containers in between. Read the advice list: workload size, frequency and
  duration, networking, language, cost.
- **Benchmark wars**: why vendor benchmarks so often mislead (old data sizes, nonsense cost comparisons, tuning only
  their own side). Read this one for fun; it's useful scepticism.

## After reading

List three tools or services you've used. Mark each immutable or transitory, and say why.

## What to take away

- Choose tools that fit the architecture, the team and the budget, in that order.
- Count the people cost (TCO) and the lost options (TOCO), not just the price.
- Build on immutable technologies; treat the rest as replaceable, and review them every two years.
