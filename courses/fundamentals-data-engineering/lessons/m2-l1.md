# Read: Chapter 2, The Data Engineering Lifecycle

This chapter is the map for the whole book. Chapters 5 to 9 each go deep on one stage, and the "undercurrents" come
back at the end of every chapter. Read it all.

## What is the data engineering lifecycle?

Five stages (Figure 2-1): **generation**, **storage**, **ingestion**, **transformation** and **serving**. Notice that
storage sits *under* the other stages, because data is stored throughout, and that generation isn't really yours: the
data engineer consumes what source systems produce.

Also read *The Data Lifecycle Versus the Data Engineering Lifecycle*: the data engineering lifecycle is the part of
the full data lifecycle that data engineers control.

For each stage, the chapter gives **key questions to ask**. Don't memorize them; notice their shape. They're about
who produces or uses the data, how much, how fast, how reliable, and what happens when something changes.

- **Generation**: what kind of source is it, how fast does it produce data, how is quality, will the schema change?
- **Storage**: does it fit the reads and writes you need, will it scale, how fresh must data be? Read the box on
  *hot, warm and cold data*.
- **Ingestion**: **batch versus streaming** and **push versus pull**. Note the authors' advice: streaming only when
  there's a real business reason; batch is simpler and enough for most uses.
- **Transformation**: turning data into something useful, and why business logic is the hardest part.
- **Serving**: analytics (business, operational, embedded), machine learning, and **reverse ETL**.

## The undercurrents

Six practices that run under every stage (Figure 2-7). Read each carefully; they're what separate a pipeline that
works once from one a company can trust:

- **Security**: least privilege, and people as the weakest link.
- **Data management**: governance (discoverability, metadata, accountability), **data quality** (accuracy,
  completeness, timeliness), modeling, lineage, integration, lifecycle, ethics and privacy. Note the four kinds of
  metadata: business, technical, operational and reference.
- **DataOps**: the three pillars, **automation**, **monitoring and observability**, and **incident response**.
- **Data architecture**: the next chapter.
- **Orchestration**: running jobs in the right order, as a DAG, with dependencies, schedules and alerts.
- **Software engineering**: data processing code, open source, streaming, infrastructure as code, pipelines as code.

## After reading

Draw the lifecycle for one dataset you know (sales in a shop, sensor readings, app logins). Under each stage, write
the system it lives in and one thing that could go wrong there.

## What to take away

- Five stages: generation, storage, ingestion, transformation, serving. Storage runs under all of them.
- Six undercurrents apply at every stage: security, data management, DataOps, architecture, orchestration, software
  engineering.
- Prefer batch until the business really needs streaming.
