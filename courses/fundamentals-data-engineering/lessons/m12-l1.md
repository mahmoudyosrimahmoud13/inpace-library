# Read: Chapter 10, Security and Privacy, and Chapter 11, The Future of Data Engineering

Two short chapters to finish the book. Chapter 10 is practical and worth rereading every year. Chapter 11 is the
authors' predictions: read it critically, a few years after it was written, and decide which came true.

## Chapter 10: Security and Privacy

The chapter goes in the order the authors think matters: **people, processes, technology**.

**People.** "The weakest link in security and privacy is you." The power of **negative thinking** (imagine what could
go wrong), and **always be paranoid** (check before giving out credentials or data, even to colleagues). You're also
the first line of defence for privacy and ethics: raise concerns.

**Processes.**

- **Security theater versus security habit**: long policies nobody reads, versus simple practices people actually do.
- **Active security**: think about real threats to *your* systems, not only checklists.
- **The principle of least privilege**: only the access needed, only for as long as needed.
- **Shared responsibility in the cloud**: the provider secures the data centre; you secure what you build and
  configure. Most cloud breaches are customer mistakes.
- **Always back up your data**, and **test restoring** it regularly. Ransomware makes this urgent.
- **An example security policy**: short and practical. Read it in full; you could adopt it tomorrow.

**Technology.** Patch and update; **encryption** at rest and in transit; **logging, monitoring and alerting** on
access, resources, **billing** (a spike can mean an attacker) and **excess permissions**; network access; and security
in low-level data engineering (the libraries you depend on).

## Chapter 11: The Future of Data Engineering

- The lifecycle isn't going away; tools get **simpler** and more managed.
- The **cloud-scale data OS**: services that work together through common standards.
- "**Enterprisey**" data engineering: data management and governance become everyday work.
- **Titles and responsibilities will morph**: data engineering spreads into software and ML engineering.
- Beyond the modern data stack, toward the **live data stack**: streaming pipelines, real-time analytical databases,
  data fused into applications, tight feedback between apps and ML.
- **Dark matter data**: spreadsheets, which run much of the world's analytics outside any data system.

## After reading

Write the three security rules you'd put on one page for Inkwell. Then pick one prediction from Chapter 11 and find

## What to take away

- Security is people first, then habits, then tools.
- Least privilege, encryption, monitoring and tested backups are the everyday basics.
- Tools will keep changing; the lifecycle and its undercurrents stay.
