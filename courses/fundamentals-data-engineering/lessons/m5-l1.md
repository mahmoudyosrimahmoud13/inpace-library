# Read: Chapter 5, Data Generation in Source Systems

Part II of the book starts here: one chapter per lifecycle stage. You rarely own source systems, but you depend on
them completely. This chapter teaches you how they work so you can ask their owners the right questions.

## Main ideas

- **Files and unstructured data**, and **APIs**: the most common ways data is handed over.
- **Application databases (OLTP)**: built for many small reads and writes, one row at a time. Read the part on
  **ACID** and why it matters to the app (and less to you). Then **OLAP**: built for big scans over many rows.
- **Change data capture (CDC)**: getting every change from a database, not just its current state.
- **Logs** and **database logs**: the record of what happened, and the log a database writes before changing data.
  Note the log resolution levels and why database logs make CDC possible.
- **CRUD versus insert-only**: in a CRUD table an UPDATE overwrites the old value; in an insert-only table every
  change is a new row (Table 5-1). Note the two costs of insert-only: tables grow, and finding the current state
  takes more work.
- **Messages and streams**: a message is consumed and removed from the queue; a stream is an **append-only log**
  kept for weeks or months, which you can replay.
- **Types of time**: **event time**, **ingestion time**, **process time** and **processing time** (Figure 5-6). The
  advice: record a timestamp at every stage.

## Practical details

This part ages faster, as the authors warn. Read it as a tour:

- **Databases**: the questions to ask about any database (how it stores and finds data, how it scales, CRUD,
  consistency), then relational, key-value, document, wide-column, graph, search, time-series.
- **APIs**: REST, GraphQL, webhooks, RPC and gRPC.
- **Data sharing** and **third-party data**.
- **Message queues and event-streaming platforms**: topics, partitions, consumers; **exactly-once** versus
  **at-least-once** delivery; and why order isn't guaranteed.

## Whom you'll work with, and the undercurrents

Read *Whom You'll Work With* carefully: the advice to set up **data contracts** and service level agreements with
the source system's owners is one of the most practical in the book. Then the undercurrents, applied to sources.

## After reading

For one source you know, answer: is it CRUD or insert-only? Does it keep history? What time does each record carry:
when it happened, or when it was saved?

## What to take away

- OLTP databases are built for the app, not for analytics; read from them carefully.
- CRUD tables forget history. Insert-only tables, logs and CDC keep it.
- Know which time a timestamp means, and record time at every stage.
