# Forks: one piece, two targets

A **fork** is one piece attacking two (or more) enemy pieces at the same time. The opponent can only save one.

## The knight fork

Knights are the best forkers, because their L-shaped move is hard to see, and they can attack a queen or a rook without being attacked back.

```
8  r . . . k b . r
7  p p . . . p p p
6  . . . . . . . .
5  . . . N . . . .
4  . . . . . . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  P P P . . P P P
1  R . . . K . . R
   a b c d e f g h
```

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/r3kb1r/pp3ppp/8/3N4/8/8/PPP2PPP/R3K2R_w_KQkq_-_0_1)

White plays **Nc7+**: check to the king on e8 and an attack on the rook on a8. The king must move, and White takes the rook. A fork with check is especially strong, because the opponent has no time to do anything else.

A fork of king and queen is called a **royal fork**. A fork of king, queen and rook is a **family fork**.

## The pawn fork

A pawn that moves forward attacks two squares diagonally. If two enemy pieces stand on those squares, the pawn wins one.

```
8  . . . . k . . .
7  . . . . . . . .
6  . . . n . n . .
5  . . . . . . . .
4  . . . . P . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  . . . . . . . .
1  . . . . K . . .
   a b c d e f g h
```

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/4k3/8/3n1n2/8/4P3/8/8/4K3_w_-_-_0_1)

**e5!** attacks both knights. Neither knight can take the pawn, so White wins a knight for a pawn.

## The queen fork

The queen moves in eight directions, so it often attacks two things at once. Checks are the most common way.

```
8  . . . . k . . .
7  p p . . . p p p
6  . . . . . . . .
5  . . . . . . . .
4  . b . . . . . .
3  . . . . . . . .
2  P P . . . P P P
1  . . . Q . . K .
   a b c d e f g h
```

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/4k3/pp3ppp/8/8/1b6/8/PP3PPP/3Q2K1_w_-_-_0_1)

**Qa4+** checks the king along the diagonal and attacks the undefended bishop on b4. After the king moves (or Black blocks with ...b5), **Qxb4** wins the bishop. Notice how the loose bishop made the fork possible.

## How to find forks

1. Find enemy pieces that are **undefended**, or more valuable than your attacker, and the **king**.
2. Ask: is there a square from which one of my pieces attacks two of them?
3. Check that square is **safe** (or that the fork is with check).
