# Calculation: checks, captures and threats

Knowing the patterns is half the job. The other half is **finding** them in a real game and making sure they work.

## Look at forcing moves first

**Forcing moves** limit what the opponent can do:

1. **Checks** (the opponent must answer)
2. **Captures** (the opponent usually must recapture)
3. **Threats** (attacks on pieces or mate threats)

On every move, list all your checks, captures and threats, even the ones that look silly. Sacrifices hide there: **Qd8+!** and **Qg8+!** from the last module look crazy until you calculate them.

## Candidate moves

Before you calculate anything, write down (in your head) **two or three candidate moves**. Then look at each one in turn. This stops you from falling in love with the first idea.

## Calculate to a quiet position

For each candidate, follow the forcing moves for both sides until the position is **quiet** (no more checks or captures), then judge: who is ahead in material? Is my king safe?

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/4k3/pp3ppp/8/8/1b6/8/PP3PPP/3Q2K1_w_-_-_0_1)

Candidates for White: **Qa4+**, **Qd8+**? and **Qe2+**.

- **Qd8+?** Kxd8. The queen is lost. Rejected in one move.
- **Qe2+** Kf8 and nothing more happens.
- **Qa4+!** Now list every Black reply: ...Kf8, ...Ke7, ...Kd8, or the block ...b5. In every case **Qxb4** follows, and White wins a bishop.

## The blunder check

When you've chosen a move, take one last look **from your opponent's side**: what are **his** checks, captures and threats after my move? This catches most mistakes.

## Train your eyes

- Solve puzzles **without moving the pieces** until you are sure.
- Mix fast puzzles (pattern recognition) with slow ones where you take 5 minutes and calculate everything.
- Use the Lichess **puzzle streak** and **puzzle storm** for speed, and normal puzzles for depth.

## Try it

You see a lovely sacrifice: your queen takes a pawn next to the enemy king with check. What must you check before playing it?

**Answer:** every reply: can the king take the queen, and if so, what then? Do you have a follow-up (another check, a mate, a fork)? If any reply leaves you just a queen down, don't play it.

## Your practice

Solve 10 puzzles slowly: for each, write your candidate moves and the line you calculated before making a move. Compare with the solution.
