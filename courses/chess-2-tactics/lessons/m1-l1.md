# Safety first: loose pieces and counting

A **tactic** is a short sequence of moves that wins material or gives mate. Most games below club level are decided by tactics, not deep plans. And most tactics start with one simple fact: a piece that is **not defended** is a target.

## Loose pieces drop off

Players call it **LPDO**: *loose pieces drop off*. An undefended piece can be won by any move that attacks it together with something else. So two habits:

- Keep your pieces **defended**, or at least know which ones are not.
- Look at your opponent's **undefended** pieces every move. They are your targets.

## Counting attackers and defenders

To know whether a capture wins, count:

- How many of your pieces **attack** the square?
- How many enemy pieces **defend** it?

If attackers outnumber defenders, you can usually win the piece. But also **look at values**: start with your **least valuable** attacker, so you never give a big piece for a small one.

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/r1bqkbnr/pppp1ppp/8/4p3/3nP3/2P2N2/PP1P1PPP/RNBQKB1R_w_KQkq_-_1_4)

Black's knight jumped to d4. It is attacked twice (the knight on f3 and the pawn on c3) and defended once (the pawn on e5). Which capture is best?

- **4. cxd4!** exd4: White gives a pawn (1) and wins a knight (3).
- **4. Nxd4?!** exd4 5. cxd4: White gives a knight and gets a knight and a pawn back, so it only wins a pawn.

Capturing with the **pawn first** wins the most. (Black's ...Nd4 was a real mistake, by the way: the right move was a developing one like ...Nf6.)

## Trades: when to swap

- **Even trades** (knight for bishop, rook for rook) are fine, especially when you are **ahead** in material: fewer pieces make your extra material count more.
- When you are **behind**, avoid trades and keep pieces on the board to create chances.
- A trade that **removes a strong enemy piece** or **damages his pawns** can be good even if it's "equal" in points.

Black to move. White's pawn on d4 attacks e5. How many times is e5 attacked and defended? What should Black do?

**Answer:** e5 is attacked twice (d4 and Nf3) and defended once (Nc6), so it's in danger. Black should take: **3... exd4** (then 4. Nxd4 is the Scotch Game, a normal opening).
