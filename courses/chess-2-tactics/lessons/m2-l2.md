# Discovered attacks and double check

## The discovered attack

When one of your pieces **moves out of the way**, it can uncover an attack by a piece behind it. The moving piece can then make its own threat, so you create **two attacks with one move**.

![Chess board](board-4fa08bef49.png)

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess to play it out](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/3q2k1/5ppp/8/3B4/8/8/5PPP/3R2K1_w_-_-_0_1)

The bishop on d5 stands between the white rook on d1 and the black queen on d8. **Bxf7+ (bishop takes on f7, check)** gives check and, at the same time, uncovers the rook's attack on the queen. Black must answer the check, so **Rxd8 (rook takes on d8)** wins the queen for a bishop and a pawn.

## Discovered check

If the uncovered piece gives **check**, the moving piece can go almost anywhere and attack anything, because the opponent must deal with the check first. Discovered checks are among the most powerful tactics in chess.

## Double check

If both the moving piece **and** the uncovered piece give check, it's a **double check**. You can't block two checks or capture two pieces at once, so the **king must move**.

![Chess board](board-589dfe07ff.png)

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess to play it out](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/4k3/8/8/7q/4N3/8/8/4R1K1_w_-_-_0_1)

**Nf6+ (knight to f6, check)** is a double check: the knight checks the king on e8, and moving it opens the rook's check along the e-file. The king has to move, and the knight, which also attacks h5, takes the queen.

## How to find them

Look for your **long-range piece** (rook, bishop, queen) aimed at an enemy target with **one of your own pieces in between**. That in-between piece is loaded: every move it makes is a discovered attack. Ask what is the most damaging square for it.

## Try it

![Chess board](board-96465461e3.png)

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess to play it out](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/r3k3/8/8/8/4B3/8/8/4R1K1_w_-_-_0_1)

White to move. Win the rook.

**Answer:** **Bxa8+ (bishop takes on a8, check)**. The bishop takes the rook on a8, and by leaving the e-file it uncovers check from the rook on e1. Black must answer the check, and White is a rook up.
