# Mating patterns you must know

Strong players recognize mating patterns at a glance, the way you recognize a word without spelling it. Learn these and you'll start seeing mates in your own games.

## Back-rank mate

A rook or queen mates the king on its back rank, behind its own pawns. You've seen it many times now. Prevent it by making **luft** (h3 / ...h6) at a quiet moment.

## Smothered mate

A knight mates a king that is completely surrounded by its own pieces.

![Chess board](board-be602b56a7.png)

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess to play it out](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/5r1k/6pp/7N/q2Q4/8/8/6PP/6K1_w_-_-_0_1)

**Qg8+! (queen to g8, check)** The queen can't be taken by the king (the knight on h6 guards g8), so the rook must take: **Rxg8 (rook takes on g8)**. Now the king is smothered by its own rook and pawns, and **Nf7# (knight to f7, checkmate)** is mate.

## The Arabian mate

One of the oldest patterns in chess, found in Arabic manuscripts from more than a thousand years ago. A rook and a knight work together against a king in the corner.

![Chess board](board-1db9c65b9f.png)

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess to play it out](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/7k/1R6/5N2/8/8/8/8/6K1_w_-_-_0_1)

**Rh7# (rook to h7, checkmate)**: the rook checks from h7, protected by the knight on f6, which also covers g8. The rook covers g7. No escape.

## Queen and bishop on h7

A queen supported by a bishop (a **battery**) aimed at h7 or h2.

![Chess board](board-f01ba02405.png)

*White to move.* [Open on Lichess to play it out](https://lichess.org/analysis/standard/5rk1/5ppp/8/7Q/8/3B4/8/6K1_w_-_-_0_1)

**Qxh7# (queen takes on h7, checkmate)**: the bishop on d3 protects the queen, and the black rook on f8 takes away the king's escape square.

## Two-rook ladder mate

Two rooks (or a rook and a queen) take turns checking, pushing the king one rank at a time to the edge. One rook cuts off a rank while the other checks on the next one.

![Chess board](board-7a6cdca624.png)

**Answer:** **Re8# (rook to e8, checkmate)**, a back-rank mate.
