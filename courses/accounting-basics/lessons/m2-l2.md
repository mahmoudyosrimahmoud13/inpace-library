# Revenue, expenses and the owner's drawings

Equity is the part of the equation that moves the most. It's worth being precise about what moves it.

## Revenue

**Revenue** is what the business earns by doing its work: selling cakes, repairing phones, teaching a class, charging rent.

- Recorded when the work is **done** (the goods delivered or the service performed), whether or not the cash has arrived.
- Increases **equity**, because the profit belongs to the owner.
- Names you'll see: *sales*, *sales revenue*, *service revenue*, *fees earned*, *rent revenue*, *interest revenue*.

Not revenue:
- A **loan** from the bank (it must be paid back: a liability)
- The owner **investing** money (that's capital, not earnings)
- **Collecting** money a customer already owed (the revenue was recorded earlier)

## Expenses

**Expenses** are the costs of earning revenue in a period: wages, rent used, electricity, supplies used, advertising, the wear on equipment.

- Recorded when they're **used up** or **incurred**, whether or not they're paid yet.
- Decrease **equity**.

Not expenses:
- **Buying an asset** that will last (an oven, a car). It's recorded as an asset, then spread over its life as *depreciation* (Day 6).
- **Paying a supplier** for something already recorded. The cost was recorded when the goods arrived.
- **Paying back a loan's principal.** That just lowers a liability. Only the **interest** is an expense.
- **The owner's personal spending** (drawings).

## Drawings

When the owner takes cash or goods out of the business for personal use, it's **drawings** (in a company, the similar idea is *dividends* to shareholders).

Drawings lower equity, like expenses do, but they're kept separate because they didn't help earn revenue. Mixing them would make the business look less profitable than it is.

## Profit

> **Profit (net income) = Revenue − Expenses**

If expenses are bigger, it's a **loss**.

For Nour's June so far: revenue 3,600 − expenses 1,200 = **2,400** profit. (On Day 6 we'll see this number is too high: she used up rent, supplies and part of her oven that aren't counted yet.)

## How equity changes over a period

> **Ending equity = Beginning equity + Owner investments + Profit − Drawings**

For Nour: 0 + 20,000 + 2,400 − 500 = **21,900**, matching the balance sheet in the last lesson (22,400 assets − 500 liabilities).

## Quick check

Which of these change profit?

1. Nour borrows $5,000 from the bank.
2. Nour pays $200 for a newspaper ad.
3. Nour buys a $1,200 mixer for cash.
4. Nour pays back $1,000 of the loan plus $50 interest.
5. A customer pays $300 for cakes delivered last week.

**Answers:** only **2** (an expense of $200) and the **$50 interest** in 4. The loan, the mixer, the principal repayment and the collection don't change profit.
