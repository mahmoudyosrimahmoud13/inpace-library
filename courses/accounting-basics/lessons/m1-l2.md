# The accounting equation

Everything in accounting rests on one line:

> **Assets = Liabilities + Equity**

## The three parts

**Assets** are things the business owns or controls that will bring it a benefit.
Examples: cash, money customers owe (*accounts receivable*), flour and boxes (*supplies* or *inventory*), rent paid in advance (*prepaid rent*), the oven (*equipment*), a building, a car.

**Liabilities** are what the business owes to others.
Examples: money owed to suppliers (*accounts payable*), a bank loan (*loans payable*), wages earned by staff but not paid yet (*wages payable*), money a customer paid for an order not delivered yet (*unearned revenue*).

**Equity** is the owner's claim: what would be left for the owner if the business sold every asset and paid every liability.
That's why it's often written the other way round:

> **Equity = Assets − Liabilities**

## Why it always balances

Look at it as two views of the same things:

- The **left side** says what the business **has**.
- The **right side** says **who has a claim** on it: outsiders (liabilities) or the owner (equity).

Every dollar of assets came from somewhere: either someone lent it, or the owner put it in (or the business earned it, which belongs to the owner). So both sides are always equal.

## Example: Nour's first two days

On June 1, Nour puts **$20,000** of her savings into the bakery's new bank account.

| Assets | = | Liabilities | + | Equity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cash 20,000 | = | 0 | + | Capital 20,000 |

On June 2, she buys an oven for **$8,000** cash.

| Assets | = | Liabilities | + | Equity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cash 12,000 + Equipment 8,000 = 20,000 | = | 0 | + | Capital 20,000 |

Total assets didn't change: one asset (cash) became another (an oven).

On June 3, she buys flour and supplies **on credit** for **$1,500** (she'll pay the supplier later).

| Assets | = | Liabilities | + | Equity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cash 12,000 + Supplies 1,500 + Equipment 8,000 = 21,500 | = | Accounts payable 1,500 | + | Capital 20,000 |

Both sides went up by $1,500. Still equal.

## The expanded equation

Equity changes for four reasons:

- The owner **invests** money (capital goes up)
- The business **earns revenue** (equity goes up)
- The business **has expenses** (equity goes down)
- The owner **takes money out** for personal use, called **drawings** (equity goes down)

So the full version is:

> **Assets = Liabilities + Capital + Revenue − Expenses − Drawings**

Revenue minus expenses is **profit** (or loss). Profit belongs to the owner, so it raises equity.

## Try it

A friend's phone repair shop has: cash $3,000, tools $5,000, spare parts $1,200, a $2,000 loan from her brother, and $700 owed to a parts supplier.

1. What are total assets?
2. What are total liabilities?
3. What is equity?

**Answers:** assets = 3,000 + 5,000 + 1,200 = **$9,200**. Liabilities = 2,000 + 700 = **$2,700**. Equity = 9,200 − 2,700 = **$6,500**.
