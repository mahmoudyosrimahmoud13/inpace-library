# Start here: what accounting is for

Nour bakes the best cakes in her street. Friends keep ordering, so she rents a small shop, buys an oven and hires an assistant. Three months later she has a question she can't answer: **"Am I actually making money?"**

There is cash in the drawer, but some of it belongs to the flour supplier. A café owes her for a catering order. She paid three months of rent at once. The oven cost a lot, but it will last for years. Looking at the cash drawer tells her almost nothing.

**Accounting** is the system that answers Nour's question. It records every money event in a business, sorts them, and turns them into a few reports that show:

- **How the business did** over a period (did it make a profit?)
- **Where it stands** on a given day (what does it own, and what does it owe?)
- **Where the cash went** (why is there less cash than profit, or more?)

## Who uses accounting information

| Who | What they want to know |
|---|---|
| **The owner or managers** | Is it profitable? Which products earn the most? Can we afford a second oven? |
| **Banks and lenders** | Can this business pay back a loan? |
| **Investors** | Is this a good business to put money into? |
| **The tax authority** | How much profit was made, so how much tax is due? |
| **Suppliers** | Will this customer pay me if I sell on credit? |
| **Employees** | Is my job safe? Is there room for a raise? |

Every one of them reads the same reports, so the reports must follow rules everyone trusts.

## Bookkeeping and accounting

- **Bookkeeping** is recording: writing down every sale, purchase and payment in the right place, every day.
- **Accounting** is bookkeeping plus everything after it: adjusting the records at the end of a period, preparing reports, analyzing them, and advising.

This course teaches both, from the first record to finished reports.

## Four ideas to hold on to from day one

1. **The business is separate from its owner** (the *business entity* idea). Nour's personal phone bill is not a bakery expense. When she takes cash for herself, it's recorded as money leaving the business.
2. **Everything is measured in money.** A loyal customer is valuable, but it isn't recorded because it has no reliable money amount.
3. **Records show what things cost** (*historical cost*). The oven is recorded at the $8,000 Nour paid, not at what she hopes it's worth.
4. **Every record needs evidence**: a receipt, an invoice, a bank statement. No evidence, no record.

## What you'll be able to do after this course

- Analyze any business transaction and record it with **debits and credits**
- Keep a **journal** and a **ledger**, and prove they balance with a **trial balance**
- Make the **adjusting entries** that turn cash records into true profit
- Prepare a simple **income statement** and **balance sheet**
- **Close the books** at the end of a period and start the next one

We'll follow Nour's bakery through its first month the whole way, then you'll do a full month for another small business on your own.

> **How to study this course:** keep a notebook or a spreadsheet open. Every lesson has small examples. Redo them yourself before reading the answer. Accounting is learned with a pencil, not by reading.
