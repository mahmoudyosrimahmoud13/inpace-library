# The chart of accounts

A business needs a fixed list of the accounts it uses, so everyone records the same thing in the same place. That list is the **chart of accounts**.

## The usual numbering

Most charts give each account a number. The first digit tells you the type:

| Range | Type | Normal balance |
|---|---|---|
| 1000–1999 | Assets | Debit |
| 2000–2999 | Liabilities | Credit |
| 3000–3999 | Equity | Credit |
| 4000–4999 | Revenue | Credit |
| 5000–6999 | Expenses | Debit |

The order is the order of the reports: balance sheet accounts first (assets, liabilities, equity), then income statement accounts (revenue, expenses).

Leaving gaps between numbers (1010, 1020, 1030) lets you add accounts later without renumbering.

## Contra accounts

Two accounts above are marked **contra**. A contra account sits with a group but has the **opposite** normal balance, and it's subtracted from its partner:

- **Accumulated depreciation** (credit balance) is subtracted from Equipment to show how much of the oven's cost has been used up. You'll meet it on Day 6.
- **Drawings** (debit balance) is subtracted from Capital.

## Current and non-current

Assets and liabilities are often split by time:

- **Current assets:** cash, or things that will turn into cash or be used up within a year (receivables, supplies, prepaid rent, inventory).
- **Non-current (long-term) assets:** used for more than a year (equipment, buildings, vehicles).
- **Current liabilities:** due within a year (payables, wages payable, unearned revenue).
- **Non-current liabilities:** due later (a 5-year bank loan, except the part due this year).

This matters on the balance sheet: a bank wants to know if current assets can cover current liabilities.

## Good habits

- Use **clear names** ("Delivery van" not "Car stuff").
- Don't make **too many** accounts. A small business needs 20 to 40.
- Don't make **too few** either. "Expenses" as one account tells you nothing. Split the ones you want to watch (rent, wages, marketing).
- **Never** put personal spending in a business expense account.

## Try it

Give a number range and a type for each: Bank loan (5 years), Delivery van, Interest expense, Catering revenue, Inventory of cakes.

**Answers:** Bank loan: 2000s, non-current liability (the part due this year is current). Delivery van: 1000s, non-current asset. Interest expense: 5000s, expense. Catering revenue: 4000s, revenue. Inventory: 1000s, current asset.
