# Finding and fixing errors

Sooner or later a trial balance won't balance, or a number will look strange. Here's how professionals hunt mistakes.

## When the trial balance doesn't balance

Work out the **difference** between the two totals, then use these clues:

1. **Re-add both columns.** The most common mistake is the simplest.
2. **Look for an account with the difference as its balance.** Maybe a whole balance was left out of the list.
3. **Divide the difference by 2.** If the result matches an amount in the books, that amount was probably put on the **wrong side** (a debit written as a credit doubles the gap).
4. **Divide the difference by 9.** If it divides exactly, suspect a **transposition** (two digits swapped: 54 written as 45, difference 9) or a **slide** (a misplaced decimal point: 300 written as 30, difference 270).
5. **Check the balance of each ledger account**, then the postings from the journal, entry by entry.

### Example

Totals: debits 24,100, credits 23,200. Difference **900**.

- 900 ÷ 2 = 450. Any 450 in the books? No.
- 900 ÷ 9 = 100 exactly. Suspect a transposition or a slide.
- Looking at the ledger: Wages expense shows **900 Dr**, but a check of the trial balance shows Wages was **left out**. Found it: rule 2 would have caught it first.

## Errors that don't show up

Remember from the last lesson: some mistakes keep the trial balance balanced. They're found by:

- **Bank reconciliation:** comparing your Cash account with the bank statement every month.
- **Statements from suppliers:** comparing your Accounts payable for each supplier with their statement.
- **Counting:** checking inventory and supplies on the shelf against the books.
- **Reasonableness:** does this month's electricity look three times bigger than usual? Ask why.

## How to fix a mistake

In accounting you **never erase or delete** a posted entry. You correct it with a new entry, so the trail stays visible.

### Wrong account

Rent of 1,000 was debited to Utilities expense instead of Rent expense.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Rent expense | 1,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Utilities expense | | 1,000 |
| *Correcting entry: rent recorded as utilities on Jun 5* | | |

### Wrong amount

A cash sale of 420 was recorded as 240. The fix records the missing 180:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | 180 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sales revenue | | 180 |

### Recorded twice

Reverse the duplicate with the opposite entry.

### The two ways to correct

- **Correcting entry:** just enough to fix the difference (as above). Shortest.
- **Reverse and redo:** reverse the wrong entry completely, then record the right one. Longer, but easier for someone else to follow. Many companies prefer it.

## Habits that prevent mistakes

- Record from **documents**, never from memory.
- Number every invoice and receipt, and note the number in the entry.
- Record **every day** or every week, not all at the end of the month.
- Reconcile the bank account **monthly**.
- In a team, the person who handles cash shouldn't be the only one who records it (*separation of duties*).

## Try it

A trial balance shows debits 15,630 and credits 15,360. What kind of mistake would you look for first?

**Answer:** the difference is 270, and 270 ÷ 9 = 30 exactly, so look for a transposition (like 630 written as 360) or a slide. Indeed 15,630 vs 15,360: the digits 6 and 3 are swapped somewhere.
