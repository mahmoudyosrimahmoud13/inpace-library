# Posting to the ledger

The journal lists transactions by date. But to answer "how much cash do I have?" you'd have to add up every cash line in the whole journal. The **ledger** solves that: it has one page (one T-account) **per account**, collecting every amount that touched it.

Copying amounts from the journal to the ledger is called **posting**.

## How to post

For each line of each journal entry:

1. Find that account's page in the ledger.
2. Write the date and the amount on the **same side** (a journal debit goes on the ledger's debit side).
3. Write a **reference** to the journal page (for example `J1`), and in the journal write the account number, so you can follow the trail both ways.
4. Update the account's **balance**.

## Reading a balance

An account's balance should be on its **normal side**: assets and expenses debit, liabilities, equity and revenue credit.

If Cash ever shows a credit balance, something is wrong: either a mistake, or the bank account is overdrawn (and then it's really a liability).

## The general ledger and sub-ledgers

All the accounts together are the **general ledger**. Businesses with many customers also keep a **sub-ledger** with one page per customer, so they know *who* owes what. The total of all customer pages must equal the Accounts receivable balance in the general ledger. The same goes for suppliers and Accounts payable.

## Software does this for you, but...

In accounting software (QuickBooks, Xero, Odoo, Zoho Books and others) you enter the journal entry, or an invoice, and the posting happens by itself. You still need to understand posting to read the reports, find mistakes and explain the numbers. That's why we do it by hand first.

## Try it

Post these to a Cash T-account that starts at 9,400: Jul 1 sale for cash 800; Jul 2 pay supplier 500; Jul 3 owner invests 2,000; Jul 4 pay wages 900. What's the balance?

**Answer:** 9,400 + 800 − 500 + 2,000 − 900 = **10,800** debit.
