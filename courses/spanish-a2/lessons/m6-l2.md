## The grammar of this unit: pretérito perfecto and futuro simple

**The present perfect** = **haber** (present) + **participle**. It talks about **experiences** (ever, never) and things that happened in a time that **isn't over yet** (today, this week, this year).

| haber | + participle |
|---|---|
| he | estado, viajado, comido |
| has | visitado |
| ha | ido |
| hemos | vivido |
| habéis | |
| han | |

Participles: -ar → **-ado** (*viajado*), -er/-ir → **-ido** (*comido, ido, vivido*). Common irregular ones: *hecho* (done), *visto* (seen), *dicho* (said), *escrito* (written), *abierto* (opened), *puesto* (put), *vuelto* (returned), *roto* (broken).

- *¿Has estado en Valencia? — No, nunca he estado.*
- *Hoy he trabajado mucho. Este año he viajado más que nunca.*

(In much of Latin America people prefer the preterite here: *Hoy trabajé mucho*. Both are understood.)

**The simple future** = infinitive + **-é, -ás, -á, -emos, -éis, -án**: *viajaré, comerás, vivirá*. Irregular stems: *tendré, haré, podré, saldré, vendré, diré, querré*. You already use *ir a* for plans; the future adds predictions and promises: *Te encantará. Mañana lloverá. Te llamaré.*

## Speak: your voice note

Record a 90-second voice note in two parts. First, phone a hotel (both roles): book a room, ask the price and about breakfast, spell your name. Then talk about three places you've been and one place you've never been but will visit one day.

Record it in inPace as a voice note (or on your phone). Listen back once, fix one thing, and record it again. Saying the same task twice makes the second try faster and more accurate.

He estado en Túnez, en Turquía y en España. Nunca he estado en Japón, pero algún día iré. Tendré que ahorrar mucho.
