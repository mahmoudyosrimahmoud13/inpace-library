# I don't feel well

Carmen has had a headache for two days. Notice me duele (it hurts me), which works like me gusta, and the doctor's advice.

## Listen first: the conversation

**At the health centre**

| Spanish | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Doctora:** Buenos días. ¿Qué le pasa? | Good morning. What's the matter? |
| **Carmen:** Me duele mucho la cabeza desde hace dos días, y también me duele la garganta. | I've had a bad headache for two days, and my throat hurts too. |
| **Doctora:** ¿Tiene fiebre? | Do you have a temperature? |
| **Carmen:** Sí, un poco. Ayer tuve treinta y ocho. Y estoy muy cansada. | Yes, a little. Yesterday I had thirty-eight. And I'm very tired. |
| **Doctora:** A ver... abra la boca, por favor. Sí, tiene la garganta inflamada. Es una infección leve. | Let's see... open your mouth, please. Yes, your throat is swollen. It's a mild infection. |
| **Carmen:** ¿Qué tengo que hacer? | What do I have to do? |
| **Doctora:** Tome este medicamento cada ocho horas, beba mucha agua y descanse. No debe ir a trabajar hasta el lunes. | Take this medicine every eight hours, drink lots of water and rest. You shouldn't go to work until Monday. |
| **Carmen:** Vale. Muchas gracias, doctora. | OK. Thank you very much, doctor. |
| **Doctora:** De nada. Si no está mejor en tres días, vuelva. | You're welcome. If you're not better in three days, come back. |

## Shadow it

Play the conversation in your head or read it aloud **three times**: first slowly, then at normal speed, then without looking at the meaning column. If you have the audio from the links below, pause after each line and copy the speaker's rhythm and melody, not just the words. This is called **shadowing**, and it trains your ear and your mouth together.

## Say it right

**Regional sounds you'll hear.** In Argentina and Uruguay, **ll** and **y** sound like *sh* or the *s* in *measure*: *me llamo* → "me SHA-mo", *yo* → "sho". In much of Andalusia and the Caribbean, an **s** at the end of a syllable becomes a soft *h* or disappears: *¿Cómo estás?* → "¿Cómo ehtá?". You don't need to copy these, but recognising them will help you understand real speakers.
