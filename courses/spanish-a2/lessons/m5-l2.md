## The grammar of this unit: doler, tener que / deber, and commands

**doler** (to hurt) works like *gustar*: the part of the body is the subject.

- *Me duele **la cabeza**.* (one thing → duele)
- *Me duelen **los ojos**.* (several → duelen)
- *¿Te duele?* *A Carmen le duele la garganta.*

Use **the**, not *my*, with body parts: *Me duele **la** cabeza* (not *mi cabeza*).

**Obligation and advice:**

- **tener que** + infinitive = have to: *Tengo que tomar un medicamento.*
- **deber** + infinitive = should, must: *Debes descansar. No debe ir a trabajar.*
- **hay que** + infinitive = one has to (general): *Hay que beber agua.*

**The imperative (commands)** for advice and instructions:

| | tú (friendly) | usted (polite) |
|---|---|---|
| tomar | **toma** | **tome** |
| beber | **bebe** | **beba** |
| abrir | **abre** | **abra** |
| descansar | **descansa** | **descanse** |

The **tú** command looks like the *él/ella* present (*toma, bebe*). The **usted** command swaps the vowel: -ar → **-e**, -er/-ir → **-a**. Some common irregular tú commands: *ven* (come), *haz* (do), *ten* (have), *di* (say), *sal* (go out), *pon* (put).

## Speak: your voice note

Record a 60-second voice note: you're at the doctor's. Say what hurts, since when, and how you feel. Then give a friend with a cold four pieces of advice using tú commands and debes.

Record it in inPace as a voice note (or on your phone). Listen back once, fix one thing, and record it again. Saying the same task twice makes the second try faster and more accurate.

A model answer (yours can be different):

> Doctor, me duele mucho el estómago desde ayer y no tengo hambre. Esta mañana tuve un poco de fiebre. Estoy muy cansado. Y a ti, Laura, que estás resfriada: bebe mucha agua y toma té con miel. Descansa y no salgas por la noche... bueno, quédate en casa. Debes dormir mucho. ¡Que te mejores!
