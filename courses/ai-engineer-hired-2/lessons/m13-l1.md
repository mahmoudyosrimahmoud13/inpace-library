# Mock interviews that actually help

Solving alone and solving while someone watches are different skills. The [Tech Interview Handbook study plan](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/coding-interview-study-plan/) suggests starting mock interviews about 60% of the way through your preparation. Ways to get them:

- **Friends or classmates:** take turns being the interviewer. Being the interviewer teaches you what a strong answer looks like.
- **[Pramp](https://www.pramp.com/)** (now part of Exponent): free peer mock interviews.
- **[interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/):** anonymous mocks with engineers from big companies (mostly paid).
- **Yourself:** record your screen and voice on a fresh problem, then watch it with the rubric.

## A mock interview that teaches

- 45 minutes, a problem the candidate hasn't seen, a shared editor without autocomplete (or a whiteboard, since in-person rounds are coming back).
- The interviewer stays mostly quiet and gives a hint only after 5 minutes of no progress.
- 10 minutes of feedback using the four rubric areas, with one thing to keep and one to change.
- Log it in your tracker like a problem.

**Schedule:** 1 to 2 a week from week 6, then 3 a week in the last two weeks.
