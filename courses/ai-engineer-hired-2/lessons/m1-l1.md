# Start here: the intensive

This is part 2 of *Get hired as an AI engineer*. It trains the round that rejects the most people: live coding. Expect about **10 hours a week for 12 weeks**. It's hard on purpose: interviews are a skill, and skills come from many hours of the right practice, not from reading.

## What AI engineer loops actually test

Alexey Grigorev's [AI Engineering Field Guide](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/ai-engineering-field-guide) studied the interview processes companies publish and candidates report. Coding shows up in two forms:

- **Algorithm rounds** (LeetCode-style): hash maps, tries, linked lists, graphs, greedy. Reported at Amazon, Databricks, IBM and others. The guide's advice: 75+ Easy and Medium problems.
- **Implementation rounds** (45 to 90 minutes): build something in layers, like a key-value store, a web crawler or an in-memory database, with new requirements arriving as you go. Reported at OpenAI and Anthropic.
- **ML coding**: for example cosine similarity in NumPy (Amazon), or logistic regression from scratch (Mistral).

This part covers all three: modules 2 to 10 are the algorithm patterns, module 11 is ML coding, module 12 is implementation rounds, and module 13 is mock interviews.

## Your tracker

Make a spreadsheet now, one row per attempt: **date, problem, pattern, minutes, solved alone (yes/no), key idea, redo date**. It's how you'll see progress and choose what to redo. Language: use **Python** unless the job asks for another; its short syntax saves minutes in every interview.
