# The functions accountants use most

## Adding with conditions: SUMIFS

`SUMIFS(sum_range, criteria_range1, criteria1, [criteria_range2, criteria2], ...)`

A Table `Sales` with columns Date, Region, Product, Amount:

| Question | Formula |
|---|---|
| Total sales in Cairo | `=SUMIFS(Sales[Amount], Sales[Region], "Cairo")` |
| Cairo sales of cakes | `=SUMIFS(Sales[Amount], Sales[Region], "Cairo", Sales[Product], "Cake")` |
| Sales in March 2026 | `=SUMIFS(Sales[Amount], Sales[Date], ">="&DATE(2026,3,1), Sales[Date], "<="&DATE(2026,3,31))` |
| Sales above 1,000 | `=SUMIFS(Sales[Amount], Sales[Amount], ">1000")` |

Put criteria in cells, not inside the formula: `=SUMIFS(Sales[Amount], Sales[Region], $G2)`, then copy down a list of regions. This is how you build summaries from raw data.

The family: `COUNTIFS` (how many), `AVERAGEIFS`, `MAXIFS`, `MINIFS`, all with the same pattern.

## Rounding properly

- `=ROUND(A2, 2)` to 2 decimals, `=ROUND(A2, 0)` to whole units, `=ROUND(A2, -3)` to thousands.
- `ROUNDUP`, `ROUNDDOWN`, and `MROUND(A2, 5)` to the nearest 5.

Round tax and invoice amounts at the line level when the law requires it, so totals match the invoices.

## Decisions: IF, IFS, AND, OR

- `=IF(B2>C2, "Over budget", "OK")`
- `=IF(AND(B2>0, C2="Paid"), B2, 0)`
- `=IFS(D2>90, "Over 90", D2>60, "61-90", D2>30, "31-60", TRUE, "Current")`

Keep IFs short. A nest of seven IFs is a sign you need a lookup table (Day 2).

## Errors: IFERROR

`=IFERROR(A2/B2, 0)` shows 0 instead of `#DIV/0!`. Use it carefully: it can hide real mistakes. Prefer fixing the cause, and use `IFERROR` only where an error is expected (like a lookup for a code that may not exist yet).

## Text you'll need

- `=TRIM(A2)` removes extra spaces (the reason many lookups fail).
- `=TEXT(A2, "mmm yyyy")` turns a date into "Mar 2026".
- `=LEFT(A2, 4)`, `=RIGHT`, `=MID`, `=LEN`.
- `=A2 & " - " & B2` joins text (or `TEXTJOIN`).
- `=VALUE(A2)` turns a number stored as text into a number.

## Dynamic arrays (Excel 365, Google Sheets)

One formula that returns many cells:
- `=UNIQUE(Sales[Region])`: the list of regions
- `=SORT(UNIQUE(Sales[Customer]))`
- `=FILTER(Sales, Sales[Amount]>1000)`: every row over 1,000

Combine: list regions with `UNIQUE`, then next to it `=SUMIFS(Sales[Amount], Sales[Region], G2#)` (the `#` means "the whole spilled list").

## Try it

From a Table of 20 expenses (date, category, amount, paid yes/no), build: total per category with `SUMIFS`, count of unpaid items with `COUNTIFS`, the largest single expense with `MAX`, and a list of unpaid items with `FILTER`.
