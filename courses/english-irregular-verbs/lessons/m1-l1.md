# Three forms of every verb

Every English verb has three forms you need to know:

| | Base | Past simple | Past participle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regular | work | worked | worked |
| Irregular | go | went | gone |

- **Regular verbs** just add **-ed**: *work, worked, worked*. Most verbs are regular, and new verbs are always regular (*google, googled*).
- **Irregular verbs** change in their own way: *go, went, gone*. There are only about 200, and you need about 100 of them. The good news: the most common verbs are irregular, so you meet them every day and they stick fast.

## Where each form goes

| Form | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Base | now, habits, after *to*, after *did* / *didn't* | I **go** to work. Did you **go**? |
| Past simple | a finished time in the past (yesterday, in 2020, last week) | I **went** to Aswan last year. |
| Past participle | after *have / has / had*, and in the passive (*was / is* + participle) | I **have gone** there twice. It **was written** in 1950. |

The two rules that fix most mistakes:
- After **did / didn't**, always the **base**: *Did you **see** it?* (not *saw*)
- After **have / has / had**, always the **participle**: *I've **seen** it.* (not *saw*)

## Why groups work

Learning 100 verbs one by one is hard. But they aren't random: most of them change the **same way** as a few others.

- *buy → bought, bring → brought, think → thought* all end in **-ought**.
- *sing → sang → sung, drink → drank → drunk* change **i → a → u**.
- *cut, put, hit* **don't change at all**.

So this course teaches **patterns**. Learn the pattern once, and every verb in it comes for free. There are four big families:

| Family | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A | all three forms the same | cut, cut, cut |
| B | the base comes back as the participle | come, came, come |
| C | past and participle the same | buy, bought, bought |
| D | all three different | write, wrote, written |

## How to practise (10 minutes a day)

1. **Say all three forms out loud**, like a little song: *buy, bought, bought*. Your ear learns the pattern faster than your eyes.
2. **Cover two columns** of the table with your hand and say them from memory. Then check.
3. **Make one true sentence** for each verb about your own life: *I bought a new phone last month.* Sentences about you stick.
4. **Come back tomorrow.** Five minutes tomorrow beats an hour today. inPace keeps your place, so just press **Continue**.
