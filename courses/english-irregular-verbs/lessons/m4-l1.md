# Family D, part 1

These need all three forms, but they still come in patterns.

## i → a → u

| Base | Past simple | Past participle | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **begin** | began | begun | The film has already begun. |
| **drink** | drank | drunk | I've drunk three cups of tea today. |
| **ring** | rang | rung | The phone rang twice. |
| **sing** | sang | sung | She has sung on TV. |
| **swim** | swam | swum | We swam in the Red Sea. |
| **sink** | sank | sunk | The boat sank in minutes. |
| **shrink** | shrank | shrunk | My shirt shrank in the wash. |
| **spring** | sprang | sprung | The cat sprang onto the table. |

**Tip:** the vowels go in alphabet order backwards from **i**: **i**, then **a**, then **u**. *sing, sang, sung.*

## o in the past, -en at the end

| Base | Past simple | Past participle | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **break** | broke | broken | Who broke the glass? It's broken. |
| **speak** | spoke | spoken | Have you spoken to her? |
| **steal** | stole | stolen | My bike was stolen. |
| **choose** | chose | chosen | You chose well. |
| **freeze** | froze | frozen | The lake froze last winter. |
| **wake** | woke | woken | I woke up at 7. |
| **forget** | forgot | forgotten | I've forgotten his name. |

## i → o → i + en

| Base | Past simple | Past participle | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **write** | wrote | written | She has written three books. |
| **ride** | rode | ridden | I rode a camel in Giza. |
| **drive** | drove | driven | He has driven a truck before. |
| **rise** | rose | risen | Prices have risen again. |
| **bite** | bit | bitten | A dog bit him. |
| **hide** | hid | hidden | Where have you hidden the gift? |

**Tip:** many family D participles end in **-en** or **-n**: *broken, spoken, written, driven*. If you're not sure of a participle, *-en* is a good first guess.

## Say it and hear it

The **i-a-u** verbs change only the vowel: *sing* /ɪ/ → *sang* /æ/ → *sung* /ʌ/. Keep the three vowels clearly different, because *sang* and *sung* mean different things. In **-en** participles the last syllable is weak: *written* /ˈrɪtn/, *given* /ˈɡɪvn/, *driven* /ˈdrɪvn/, *eaten* /ˈiːtn/. *Written* has a short *i*, even though *write* has a long one.

Hear any verb in the [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/) (British and American audio), and listen for these verbs in real speech on [BBC Learning English](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/).

**Speak:** Chant: *sing, sang, sung; drink, drank, drunk; swim, swam, swum; write, wrote, written.* Record it, then say three things you have written, drunk or given this week.
