# Family B: the base comes back

Only a few verbs, but you use them all the time. The past changes, then the participle **goes back to the base**:

| Base | Past simple | Past participle | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **come** | came | come | They came late. They have come home. |
| **become** | became | become | She became a doctor in 2020. |
| **run** | ran | run | I have run 5 km today. |

**Tip:** think of a circle. *come → came → come*: you start and finish in the same place.

- *She **came** late.* (past simple)
- *She **has come** back.* (not *has came*)

## How to practise (10 minutes a day)

1. **Say all three forms out loud**, like a little song: *buy, bought, bought*. Your ear learns the pattern faster than your eyes.
2. **Cover two columns** of the table with your hand and say them from memory. Then check.
3. **Make one true sentence** for each verb about your own life: *I bought a new phone last month.* Sentences about you stick.
4. **Come back tomorrow.** Five minutes tomorrow beats an hour today. inPace keeps your place, so just press **Continue**.

## Say it and hear it

Listen to the vowel change: *come* /kʌm/ → *came* /keɪm/ → *come* /kʌm/; *run* /rʌn/ → *ran* /ræn/ → *run*. The /ʌ/ in *come, run, become* is short, like the *u* in *cup*. The /æ/ in *ran* is an open sound between *a* and *e*.

Hear any verb in the [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/) (British and American audio), and listen for these verbs in real speech on [BBC Learning English](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/).

**Speak:** Say them like a chant, three times: *come, came, come; become, became, become; run, ran, run.* Record it, then add two true sentences with *has become* and *ran*.
