# British and American English

Both are correct. Just be consistent in one text.

| Base | British | American |
|---|---|---|
| learn | learnt / learned | learned |
| dream | dreamt / dreamed | dreamed |
| burn | burnt / burned | burned |
| spell | spelt / spelled | spelled |
| get | got, **got** | got, **gotten** |
| fit | fitted | fit |
| prove | proved | proved / **proven** |

- *I've **got** a car* (British, = I have a car) / *I've **gotten** better at English* (American, = I became better).
- When in doubt, the **-ed** form (*learned, dreamed*) is correct everywhere.

## How to practise (10 minutes a day)

1. **Say all three forms out loud**, like a little song: *buy, bought, bought*. Your ear learns the pattern faster than your eyes.
2. **Cover two columns** of the table with your hand and say them from memory. Then check.
3. **Make one true sentence** for each verb about your own life: *I bought a new phone last month.* Sentences about you stick.
4. **Come back tomorrow.** Five minutes tomorrow beats an hour today. inPace keeps your place, so just press **Continue**.

## Say it and hear it

British English often uses **-t** where American English uses **-ed**: *learnt / learned, dreamt / dreamed, burnt / burned, spelt / spelled*. Both are correct; just be consistent. Note the sound: *dreamt* is /dremt/, with a short *e*. And Americans say *gotten* where British speakers say *got*: *It's gotten cold* (US) / *It's got cold* (UK).

Hear any verb in the [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/) (British and American audio), and listen for these verbs in real speech on [BBC Learning English](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/).

**Speak:** Watch one BBC Learning English video (British) and one VOA Learning English report (American) and listen for *learnt / learned* or *got / gotten*.
