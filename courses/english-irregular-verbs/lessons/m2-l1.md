# Family A: verbs that never change

These verbs look the same in all three forms. Most of them are **short and end in -t or -d**: that's your clue.

| Base | Past simple | Past participle | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **cut** | cut | cut | I cut my finger yesterday. |
| **put** | put | put | Where have you put my keys? |
| **set** | set | set | She set an alarm for 6 a.m. |
| **let** | let | let | My dad let me drive his car. |
| **hit** | hit | hit | The ball hit the window. |
| **cost** | cost | cost | The tickets cost 40 dollars. |
| **hurt** | hurt | hurt | I've hurt my back. |
| **shut** | shut | shut | He shut the door quietly. |
| **quit** | quit | quit | She quit her job last month. |
| **read** | read | read | I read that book last year. (said: red) |
| **spread** | spread | spread | The news spread fast. |
| **bet** | bet | bet | I bet you can't do it. |
| **split** | split | split | We split the bill. |
| **burst** | burst | burst | The balloon burst. |
| **broadcast** | broadcast | broadcast | The match was broadcast live. |

## Watch out

- **read** is spelled the same, but said differently: today *I read* (reed), yesterday *I read* (red), *I have read* (red).
- With these verbs, only the **time words** tell you if it's past: *I cut my hair **every month*** (now) / *I cut my hair **yesterday*** (past).
- With **he / she / it** in the present, they still get **-s**: *It costs 5 pounds* (now) / *It cost 5 pounds* (past).
